## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Subjuntivo formal avanzado + expresiones de propósito

### El subjuntivo en C1

En C1 el subjuntivo aparece en estructuras que en B2 se evitaban. Es la gramática de las exigencias formales, los deseos intensos y las cláusulas de condición hipotética.

---

### Subjuntivo presente: verb (base form) sin -s

Aparece después de expresiones de:
- **Exigencia / recomendación formal:** `demand, insist, require, recommend, propose, suggest, urge`
- **Importancia:** `it is essential / vital / crucial / imperative that`
- **Deseo formal:** `wish that (en registro literario)`

```
It is essential that every candidate submit a portfolio.
The committee demands that the report be filed by Friday.
I insist that this issue be addressed immediately.
She proposed that the decision be postponed.
```

> ⚠️ El verbo en subjuntivo es siempre la **forma base** — sin -s, sin auxiliares, sin inflexión:
> - ❌ It is essential that he **submits**
> - ✅ It is essential that he **submit**
> - ❌ They require that it **is** approved
> - ✅ They require that it **be** approved

---

### Expresiones de propósito en C1

Más allá de `in order to`:

| Expresión | Registro | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **with a view to** + gerundio | formal | She took the course with a view to advancing her career. |
| **so as to** + infinitivo | formal | He documented everything so as to avoid future misunderstandings. |
| **with the aim of** + gerundio | formal | The program was designed with the aim of developing technical leaders. |
| **for the purpose of** + gerundio | muy formal | The data was collected for the purpose of identifying patterns. |
| **lest** + subjunctive | literario/formal | She double-checked her work lest she make a mistake. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Entrevista de trabajo: la pregunta sobre fracasos

> **Situación:** Sofía prepara su respuesta para la pregunta más difícil de una entrevista de trabajo de nivel C1.

```
Interviewer: Tell me about a significant professional failure and 
             what you learned from it.

Sofía:       I'll tell you about one that still informs how I work.

             During my final capstone project at RIWI, I was the 
             de facto technical lead for a four-person team.
             We were building a React application with TypeScript 
             and a Node.js backend.

             About two weeks before the deadline, I realized 
             our architecture had a critical flaw.
             We had built the state management in a way that made 
             the application impossible to scale beyond 
             what we had already built.
             Fixing it would require refactoring most of the codebase.

             I had two options: disclose the problem to the team 
             and the instructor, or manage around it and ship something 
             that worked but was structurally compromised.

             I chose the second option. And I regret it.

             We shipped. The application worked. 
             We received a decent grade.
             But I knew what I had avoided, and it sat with me.

             What that experience made clear is that the instinct 
             to protect the project's appearance at the cost of its integrity 
             is exactly the instinct that leads to real failures later — 
             in production, with actual users, with actual consequences.

             What I insist on now — with myself and with any team I'm part of — 
             is that problems be surfaced early.
             It is essential that difficult truths be communicated 
             before they become unmanageable.
             I learned that protecting my ego was not the same 
             as protecting the project.

Interviewer: That's a very honest answer. What specifically changed 
             in your practice after that?

Sofía:       I now require that every PR I open include a section 
             on known limitations or technical debt.
             Not lest the team judge me — but so the team can 
             make informed decisions about what to prioritize.
             Transparency about what's broken is more useful 
             than the appearance of what's perfect.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `What I insist on now is that problems be surfaced` — subjuntivo. `It is essential that difficult truths be communicated` — subjuntivo formal. `I require that every PR I open include` — subjuntivo. `lest the team judge me` — lest + subjuntivo.

### Caso 2 — Reflexión sobre metas y propósito (texto)

> **Situación:** Diego escribe una reflexión para su portafolio profesional sobre qué lo motiva.

```
On Goals, Purpose, and the Difference Between Them

I have a goal: to become a senior frontend developer within three years.
I have a purpose: to build things that make difficult processes simpler 
for the people who use them.

These are not the same thing.

The goal is a milestone. It can be achieved, checked off, and replaced.
The purpose is directional — it shapes which goals I pursue and how I pursue them.

What I've come to understand is that goals without purpose tend to be abandoned 
when they become difficult. The moment difficulty arrives, 
the question "why am I doing this?" arises — and if the answer is only 
"to achieve the goal," that's rarely sufficient.

Purpose provides the answer that sustains effort.
When I'm debugging something for the fourth hour,
what keeps me at it isn't the desire to be senior — 
it's the understanding that the person who will use what I'm building 
deserves something that works.

This is not idealism. It is, I think, practical psychology.

The people I most admire professionally are those whose goals 
serve their purpose rather than the reverse — 
who choose what to build toward with a view to making something 
that genuinely matters, not simply something that credentials them.

That distinction is subtle. It took me longer to understand than I expected.
And I insist that it be part of how I make every professional decision going forward.
```

### Caso 3 — Conversación sobre metas de carrera

> **Situación:** Valentina y Carlos hablan sobre sus planes a cinco años, después del último módulo de RIWI.

```
Valentina: Where do you see yourself in five years? 
            And I mean honestly, not the interview answer.

Carlos:    Honestly? I want to be leading a team.
            Not because I want authority — because I think I have 
            a particular way of working that could make teams better.
            I want to test that theory.

Valentina: That's more self-aware than most people.
            Usually the answer is "senior developer" 
            with no thought about what that actually means.

Carlos:    What about you?

Valentina: I want to build something of my own. 
            A product, not a feature inside someone else's product.
            I need to work inside a company first — 
            I need to learn how companies actually function, 
            not just how code functions.
            But that's the direction.

Carlos:    What kind of product?

Valentina: Something in education. I'm not sure the specific shape yet.
            But I'm taking every client project I can with a view to 
            understanding what people actually struggle with —
            not what they say they struggle with in surveys.

Carlos:    That's a good distinction.

Valentina: What worries me most is irrelevance — 
            building something technically excellent 
            that solves a problem nobody has.
            So I insist that every project I take involve 
            real user feedback before I commit to building anything.
            The instinct to build is strong.
            The discipline to validate first is harder.

Carlos:    That's the thing they don't teach at RIWI.

Valentina: They can't. You can only learn it by wasting time 
            on something nobody wanted.
            Which is, in retrospect, a valuable thing to learn.
```

### Caso 4 — Artículo sobre el síndrome del impostor

> **Situación:** Ana escribe sobre el imposter syndrome para el blog de RIWI.

```
Imposter Syndrome in Tech: What It Is and What to Do About It

Imposter syndrome — the persistent belief that you don't deserve 
your position or achievements, and that you will soon be "found out" —
is extraordinarily common among developers.

Research suggests it may be more prevalent among high performers 
than among average performers. The explanation is counterintuitive:
competence expands your awareness of what you don't know.
The more you learn, the more clearly you see the gap between 
what you know and what there is to know.
Someone who knows very little doesn't have this problem.

What makes it particularly acute in tech is the pace of change.
The field moves fast enough that "keeping up" is genuinely impossible.
It is essential that developers develop a tolerance for permanent incompleteness —
for the reality that someone will always know more about something, 
and that this does not disqualify you from contributing meaningfully.

Several things are known to help:
- Documenting your progress rather than just your current position.
  What you couldn't do a year ago matters.
- Normalizing uncertainty. The most experienced developers 
  say "I don't know" frequently. It is a skill, not a deficit.
- Separating performance from identity.
  A failed deployment is a technical event, not a verdict on your worth.

It is vital that organizations create cultures in which 
questions are welcomed rather than penalized —
lest the most thoughtful employees be the ones who stay silent.

If you feel like an imposter: you are probably not one.
The people who are genuinely unqualified rarely feel it.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Subjuntivo y propósito en distintos contextos

| Estructura | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| it is essential that + subjunctive | It is essential that the work be reviewed. |
| demand/require/insist + subjunctive | She demands that the contract be renegotiated. |
| with a view to + gerund | He joined the team with a view to learning from senior developers. |
| with the aim of + gerund | She documented everything with the aim of helping future developers. |
| lest + subjunctive | He explained clearly lest there be any misunderstanding. |
| so as to + infinitive | She prepared meticulously so as to make the best possible impression. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### -s en subjuntivo

❌ It is essential that he **submits** the application.
✅ It is essential that he **submit** the application.

### `Demand that` sin subjuntivo

❌ She demanded that he **to explain**.
✅ She demanded that he **explain**. (no `to`, forma base directamente)

### `With a view to` + infinitivo (debe ser gerundio)

❌ She studied with a view to **get** a better job.
✅ She studied with a view to **getting** a better job.

## 8. 🧪 Ejercicios

### A — Subjuntivo o forma indicativa

1. It is essential that every applicant ___ (submit) a portfolio.
2. The board requires that the proposal ___ (be) reviewed by legal.
3. I insist that this issue ___ (address) before the deadline.
4. She recommended that he ___ (take) the advanced course.
5. It is vital that the team ___ (be) informed immediately.

### B — Reescribe usando la expresión de propósito indicada

1. She took the job because she wanted to learn from senior developers. → (with a view to)
2. He double-checked everything because he didn't want to make mistakes. → (lest)
3. They built the prototype to demonstrate the concept. → (with the aim of)
