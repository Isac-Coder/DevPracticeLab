## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Estructuras de exigencia y reclamación formal

### Escalada de formalidad en reclamaciones

En C1, una reclamación formal no dice simplemente "I want a refund." Escala a través de:
1. Descripción factual del problema
2. Referencia a derechos o políticas
3. Impacto específico
4. Demanda concreta con plazo
5. Consecuencia si no se resuelve

---

### Estructuras clave de reclamación formal

**Exigencia con subjuntivo:**
```
I insist that a full refund be issued immediately.
I demand that the matter be investigated without delay.
I require that compensation be provided in line with EU regulation.
```

**Expresiones de consecuencia formal:**
```
Should this matter not be resolved within 14 days, I will have no choice but to...
In the event that no satisfactory response is received, I intend to...
Unless I receive a written response by [date], I will escalate this to...
```

**Referencia a derechos:**
```
Under [Regulation/Policy X], I am entitled to...
In accordance with your stated terms and conditions...
As set out in the contract of carriage...
Your obligations under consumer protection law require that...
```

---

### Reported speech en contexto de reclamación

Las reclamaciones frecuentemente reportan lo que el personal prometió:

```
Your agent assured me that the luggage would arrive on the next flight.
The representative stated that compensation would be processed within 48 hours.
I was informed that a replacement would be provided.
I was told that no additional charge would apply.
```

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Reclamación formal a una aerolínea

> **Situación:** Carlos escribe una carta de reclamación formal después de un viaje desastroso.

```
Subject: Formal Complaint — Flight AV1045 — Baggage Loss and Compensation Claim

Dear Customer Relations Department,

I am writing to formally report a significant incident that occurred 
in connection with flight AV1045 from Bogotá (BOG) to Madrid (MAD) 
on October 8, 2025, booking reference AVCO-88421.

INCIDENT SUMMARY:

Upon arriving at Madrid Barajas on October 9th, 
I discovered that my checked luggage had not arrived.
I filed a Property Irregularity Report (PIR) at the baggage office, 
reference number PIR-MAD-2025-71043.

At that time, your representative assured me that the luggage 
would be located and delivered to my accommodation within 48 hours.
I was informed that a tracking number would be provided by email.

Neither the luggage nor any communication was received 
in the 72 hours following the filing of the report.

IMPACT:

As a result of the baggage loss, I was required to purchase 
essential clothing and toiletries at my own expense, 
totaling €187.50, for which I retain all receipts.

I was traveling for a professional conference, and the loss of 
presentation materials contained in the luggage caused 
significant professional inconvenience.

DEMANDS:

Under your conditions of carriage and applicable EU passenger rights regulations:

1. I insist that the status of my luggage be confirmed in writing 
   within five business days of this communication.

2. I require that reimbursement of €187.50 be processed 
   within 14 calendar days, against the receipts attached to this letter.

3. Should the luggage be permanently lost, I demand that 
   full compensation be provided in accordance with 
   the Montreal Convention liability limits.

Should this matter not be resolved within the timelines stated above,
I will have no choice but to escalate to the relevant regulatory authority 
and pursue the matter through the appropriate legal channels.

I expect a written response within five business days.

Yours faithfully,
Carlos Moreno
Passport: CC-10234567
Contact: c.moreno@mail.com / +57 310 555 0089
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I insist that the status be confirmed`, `I require that reimbursement be processed`, `I demand that full compensation be provided` — subjuntivo en reclamaciones formales. `Should this matter not be resolved` — inversión condicional formal. `Your representative assured me that...`, `I was informed that...` — reported speech de promesas.

### Caso 2 — Conversación en el aeropuerto con el agente

> **Situación:** Ana habla con un agente de la aerolínea después de que su vuelo fue cancelado.

```
Ana:     Excuse me. I need to understand what's happening 
          with flight AV304. The departures board says "cancelled" 
          but there's been no announcement.

Agent:   Yes, I'm sorry — the flight has been cancelled 
          due to a technical issue with the aircraft.

Ana:     I understand. What are my options?

Agent:   We can rebook you on the next available flight, 
          which would be tomorrow morning at 7 AM.

Ana:     Tomorrow morning. That's nearly 18 hours from now.
          I have a connection in Lima that I'll miss.

Agent:   I understand. I can rebook the Lima connection as well.

Ana:     Good. Now — what is the airline's duty of care obligation 
          in this situation? Am I entitled to accommodation 
          and meals while I wait?

Agent:   For cancellations within our control, yes — 
          we provide hotel vouchers and meal allowances.

Ana:     I'd like that confirmed in writing, please.
          I also want to note that this cancellation will result 
          in expenses I've already incurred — 
          a non-refundable hotel booking in Lima tonight.

Agent:   You'd need to file a claim for that separately 
          through our customer relations portal.

Ana:     I'll do that. I also want to formally assert my right 
          to the EU regulation compensation for the flight cancellation — 
          assuming this doesn't qualify as extraordinary circumstances.

Agent:   Our legal team will assess that as part of your claim.

Ana:     Fine. What I need right now is:
          a confirmed rebooked itinerary in writing, 
          a hotel voucher for tonight, 
          and a meal allowance confirmation. 
          Can you provide all three before I leave this desk?

Agent:   Yes, I can process all of that right now.

Ana:     Thank you. I appreciate your help.
```

### Caso 3 — Reflexión sobre el choque cultural (texto)

> **Situación:** Valentina escribe sobre su experiencia de adaptación cultural durante un intercambio.

```
On Getting It Wrong Abroad — And What That Teaches You

I spent three months in Japan as part of a remote work experiment.
I arrived believing I was culturally aware.
I was, as it turned out, culturally aware in theory 
and culturally clumsy in practice.

The first week, I made mistakes that I didn't know I was making.
I arrived to a meeting ten minutes early — 
early enough to be disruptive to a space that wasn't ready.
I handed someone a business card with one hand.
I spoke at a volume that was — I was later told, very gently — 
slightly too loud for the office context.

None of this was addressed directly. In a culture that prioritizes 
social harmony over direct confrontation, 
the feedback was ambient rather than explicit.
It took me several weeks to decode it.

What struck me most was not the specific rules — 
those are learnable — but the underlying logic:
a social architecture built around collective comfort 
rather than individual expression.

This was genuinely challenging for me.
I come from a culture where directness is respected,
where saying what you think is a form of respect for the listener.
In Japan, the same directness often reads as rudeness — 
as placing your own comfort above the group's.

Neither framework is correct. Both are internally coherent.
The experience of culture shock is, in large part, 
the experience of discovering that something you thought 
was universal — a social instinct, a value, a way of reading a room —
is in fact cultural.

That discovery is uncomfortable. It's also invaluable.
The person I was when I left was genuinely different 
from the person I was when I arrived.
Not just more informed — actually different.
```

### Caso 4 — Guía práctica para viajeros en problemas

> **Situación:** Diego escribe una guía para el blog de RIWI sobre cómo manejar imprevistos en viajes.

```
When Travel Goes Wrong: A Practical Guide

FLIGHT DELAYS AND CANCELLATIONS

Under EU Regulation 261/2004, passengers on flights departing 
from or arriving into the EU are entitled to compensation 
for significant delays and cancellations — unless the disruption 
was caused by "extraordinary circumstances" 
(severe weather, air traffic control strikes, etc.).

What to do:
1. Ask immediately for written confirmation of the delay/cancellation reason.
   Airlines will sometimes describe preventable technical issues 
   as extraordinary circumstances to avoid liability.
   The burden of proof is on the airline, not the passenger.

2. Assert your right to duty of care: meals, accommodation, 
   and communication facilities for significant delays.
   Do not accept a voucher that waives your right to further compensation.

3. File a formal claim in writing, citing the specific regulation.
   Email is better than phone — it creates a record.

LOST LUGGAGE

File the Property Irregularity Report before leaving the airport.
This is essential — claims filed after leaving have significantly 
less standing.

Under the Montreal Convention, airlines are liable for lost luggage 
up to approximately 1,131 Special Drawing Rights.
Keep all receipts for essential purchases made due to the delay.

CULTURAL MISSTEPS

These are harder to manage because they're often invisible until after.
The most effective approach is not memorizing rules for each culture —
it's developing a posture of genuine curiosity and the willingness 
to ask: "Is there something I should know about how this is done here?"

Most people, when asked directly, are remarkably willing to help.
The question signals respect. And respect travels everywhere.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Estructuras de exigencia en distintos registros

| Registro | Estructura | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Muy formal (carta) | I insist that X be done | I insist that a full refund be issued. |
| Formal | I require that | I require that this be confirmed in writing. |
| Semi-formal | I would like to formally request | I would like to formally request compensation. |
| Neutral | I'd like / I need | I need a written confirmation of the rebooking. |
| Condicional formal | Should X not be resolved... | Should this not be resolved, I will escalate. |

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Subjuntivo en reclamaciones — la forma base sin `-s`

❌ I insist that a refund **is** issued immediately.
✅ I insist that a refund **be** issued immediately.

### `Should` en condicional formal invertido

`Should this not be resolved...` = `If this is not resolved...` — más formal, más contundente.
❌ Should this won't be resolved... (no mezcles modal + negación)
✅ Should this **not** be resolved...

### `Entitled to` + sustantivo o gerundio

❌ I am entitled to receive a refund. (correcto pero informal)
✅ I am entitled to **a refund**. (más directo)
✅ I am entitled to **receive** full compensation. (también correcto con infinitivo)
