## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Futuro Perfecto + estructuras predictivas avanzadas

### Future Perfect — lo que habrá ocurrido

```
Subject + will have + past participle
```

| Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Acción que estará completada en un punto futuro | By 2050, AI will have transformed most cognitive work. |
| Predicción de finalización | By the time she retires, she will have worked in three industries. |
| Suposición sobre el presente/pasado | You will have heard about the latest AI developments. |

---

### Future Continuous — lo que estará ocurriendo

```
Subject + will be + verb-ing
```

| Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Acción en progreso en un punto futuro | In 2040, most developers will be working alongside AI systems. |
| Predicción sobre acciones en curso | By next year, everyone will be using these tools. |

---

### Expresiones predictivas avanzadas de C1

| Expresión | Certeza | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **is set to** | alta | AI is set to transform the job market. |
| **is on course to** | procesual | The population is on course to reach 10 billion by 2058. |
| **is poised to** | inminente | Quantum computing is poised to disrupt encryption. |
| **is projected to** | basado en datos | Global temperatures are projected to rise by 1.5°C by 2035. |
| **is expected to** | expectativa | Renewable energy is expected to overtake fossil fuels by 2040. |
| **shows every sign of** | evidencia presente | The technology shows every sign of maturing rapidly. |
| **is unlikely to** | baja probabilidad | Fully autonomous vehicles are unlikely to be widespread before 2035. |

---

### Futuros hipotéticos con `were to`

```
Were AI to become fully autonomous, the implications would be profound.
Were the trend to continue, we would face a significant skills gap.
Were this prediction to prove accurate, policy makers would need to act.
```

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Ensayo sobre el futuro del trabajo en tech

> **Situación:** Carlos escribe un ensayo sobre cómo la IA cambiará el rol del desarrollador.

```
The Developer in 2035: Augmented, Automated, or Obsolete?

By 2035, AI systems will have transformed the practice of software development 
in ways that are difficult to predict with confidence 
but possible to outline with reasonable evidence.

The debate tends to polarize around two positions:
AI will augment developers, making them vastly more productive;
or AI will automate development to the point of eliminating most roles.

Both positions are probably too simple.

What the current trajectory suggests is something more nuanced:
AI is set to automate a significant portion of the tasks 
that currently occupy junior developers — boilerplate code, 
basic debugging, documentation, test generation.
By the time today's junior developers reach mid-career, 
much of what they were hired to do will have been automated.

This does not mean their roles will have disappeared. 
It means their roles will have changed.
The skills that are unlikely to be automated are precisely 
those that are hardest to teach: judgment about what to build, 
understanding of human context, the ability to navigate ambiguity, 
and the capacity to translate between what people need 
and what systems can provide.

Were AI to become capable of performing these tasks reliably, 
the implications would extend far beyond software development.
We are not there. Whether we will get there is genuinely unclear.

What does seem clear is that the developers who will be most valuable 
in 2035 will be those who have invested not just in technical skills 
but in the meta-skills that make technical knowledge applicable: 
communication, systems thinking, and the ability to work effectively 
with non-technical stakeholders.

By the time today's RIWI graduates are ten years into their careers, 
the field will have changed substantially.
The question is not whether to prepare for that change — 
it is how.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `will have transformed`, `will have been automated`, `will have changed`, `will have invested` — Future Perfect distribuido naturalmente. `is set to automate`, `are unlikely to be automated`, `were AI to become` — estructuras predictivas de C1.

### Caso 2 — Panel de discusión: ¿Qué habilidades importarán en 2040?

> **Situación:** Panel de egresados de RIWI sobre el futuro del trabajo tech.

```
Moderator: Given current AI trends, what skills will matter most 
            for developers in 2040?

Panelist 1 (Valentina):
By 2040, I think most routine code generation will have been automated.
What will be left — and what will be valued — 
is the capacity to define problems precisely.
AI is very good at solving well-defined problems.
It remains poor at defining them.
The developer who can sit with a business problem, 
extract what actually needs to be built, and communicate that 
to both technical and non-technical stakeholders — 
that person will have a role.
Whether that person is called a developer, a product architect, 
or something we haven't named yet, I don't know.

Panelist 2 (Diego):
I'd add ethics and accountability.
As AI systems make more consequential decisions — 
in healthcare, finance, legal processes — 
someone will be responsible for how those systems behave.
That person will need deep technical knowledge 
and the ability to reason about impact and values.
By the time these systems are deployed at scale, 
organizations will be looking for people who can 
ask "should we" as fluently as "can we."

Panelist 3 (Ana):
And I'd argue: learning agility.
Whatever skills matter in 2040, they are on course to be 
different from what matters today.
The most valuable thing is not knowing a specific skill — 
it's the ability to acquire new skills quickly 
when the landscape shifts again.
The developers who will be thriving in 2040 
are not necessarily the ones with the most knowledge right now —
they're the ones who will have been learning continuously 
the whole time.
```

### Caso 3 — Artículo sobre cambio demográfico

> **Situación:** Sofía escribe sobre las tendencias demográficas globales para clase.

```
The Age of the Old World: Demographics and What They Mean

By 2050, for the first time in human history, 
there will be more people over 65 than under 15 on Earth.

This is not a distant abstraction — it is already reshaping 
labor markets, healthcare systems, pension structures, 
and political priorities in most wealthy countries.

Japan offers the clearest preview. 
Its population has been declining for over a decade.
By 2040, more than one in three Japanese citizens will be over 65.
The country is on course to face a labor shortage 
of historic proportions — one that immigration, automation, 
and delayed retirement combined may not fully address.

Most wealthy countries are following, at different speeds.
South Korea has the lowest fertility rate ever recorded for any country.
Much of Southern and Eastern Europe is projected to lose 
10-20% of its population by 2100 without significant immigration.

The political implications are significant and underappreciated.
A society where older voters form a supermajority 
is structurally inclined to prioritize stability over change, 
asset preservation over investment, the present over the future.
Whether democratic systems can address long-term challenges — 
climate, infrastructure, education — 
while being governed primarily by those with shorter time horizons 
is a genuine structural question.

Were these trends to continue unmodified by policy, 
the implications for social contract and intergenerational equity 
would be profound.
The question is not whether the world will be older — 
it will. The question is what kind of institutions 
an older world will build.
```

### Caso 4 — Reflexión sobre el futuro personal

> **Situación:** Diego escribe sobre lo que ve delante en su propia carrera.

```
By the time I'm 35 — about thirteen years from now —
I want to have built something of my own.
Not necessarily a company. Possibly a product.
Certainly something that I can point to and say: 
this exists because I decided it should.

I don't know exactly what that will be.
What I do know is the direction: 
interfaces that make complex things simple, 
built for people who don't have time to learn new software 
but do have real problems to solve.

By the time the field I'm entering has matured another decade, 
the tools I'm learning today will have been superseded — 
probably more than once.
The specific frameworks won't matter as much as I currently worry they do.
What will matter is whether I've learned to think clearly 
about problems and communicate that thinking to others.

I'm not building toward a title or a salary, exactly.
I'm building toward a kind of work — 
where the problems are hard enough to be interesting,
where the impact is real enough to be motivating,
and where the people around me are good enough 
to make me better.

Whether I'll have achieved that in thirteen years, I can't say.
By then I will have learned things I can't currently anticipate 
and made mistakes I can't currently imagine.

That's not a reason not to have a direction.
It's just a reason not to confuse the map with the territory.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Future Perfect y predictivas en distintos contextos

| Tipo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Future Perfect (acción completada) | By 2030, AI will have replaced most data entry jobs. |
| Future Continuous (en progreso) | In 2040, most developers will be collaborating with AI systems. |
| `is set to` (inminente y alta certeza) | The sector is set to grow significantly over the next decade. |
| `is projected to` (datos) | Global urbanization is projected to reach 68% by 2050. |
| `Were X to` (hipótesis formal) | Were automation to accelerate, policy responses would be needed. |
| `is unlikely to` (baja probabilidad) | AGI is unlikely to emerge before mid-century. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Future Perfect — no es Past Perfect

❌ By 2035, AI has transformed the industry.
✅ By 2035, AI **will have** transformed the industry.

### `Is set to` — solo para afirmativo fuerte

❌ The technology is set to probably maybe change things.
✅ The technology **is set to** change things. (afirmación fuerte, sin hedging redundante)
→ Si hay duda: `is likely to change` / `may change`

### `Were X to` — sin `if`

❌ If were AI to become conscious...
✅ **Were** AI to become conscious... (inversión elimina `if`)
