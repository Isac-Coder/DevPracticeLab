---

## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Gerundios e infinitivos — usos avanzados

### Gerundio como sujeto de la oración

El gerundio puede funcionar como sujeto. Es más común que el infinitivo en posición de sujeto en inglés moderno:

```
Reading fiction improves empathy.       ✅ (natural)
To read fiction improves empathy.       ✅ (posible pero más formal)
```

---

### Infinitivo de propósito

```
I read to escape.                       → para escapar
She reads in order to improve her English.
```

---

### Verbo + preposición + gerundio

Muchos verbos y adjetivos se combinan con preposiciones específicas seguidas de gerundio:

| Expresión | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| interested **in** reading | I'm interested in reading more non-fiction. |
| good **at** understanding subtext | She's good at understanding what's unsaid. |
| tired **of** reading the same genre | I'm tired of reading thrillers. |
| excited **about** starting a book | I'm excited about starting this series. |
| fond **of** literary fiction | He's fond of literary fiction. |
| capable **of** reading a book a week | She's capable of finishing a book in two days. |
| accustomed **to** reading at night | I'm accustomed to reading before bed. |
| responsible **for** recommending books | She's responsible for curating the team library. |

---

### Infinitivo vs. gerundio con cambio de significado

| Verbo | Con infinitivo | Con gerundio |
|---|---|---|
| **stop** | I stopped **to read** the sign. (me detuve para leer) | I stopped **reading** at midnight. (dejé de leer) |
| **remember** | Remember **to return** the book. (no olvides devolverlo) | I remember **reading** this as a child. (recuerdo haberlo leído) |
| **try** | Try **to finish** the chapter. (intenta lograrlo) | Try **reading** out loud. (pruébalo como experimento) |
| **forget** | I forgot **to bookmark** the page. (olvidé hacerlo) | I'll never forget **reading** that ending. (nunca olvidaré haberlo leído) |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Club de lectura del equipo

> **Situación:** El equipo de RIWI tiene un club de lectura mensual. Discuten el libro del mes: "The Pragmatic Programmer".

```
Ana:      Okay, let's start. Overall impressions?

Carlos:   I'll be honest — I started reading it expecting something dry,
           but I couldn't put it down. The chapter on broken windows 
           theory alone was worth the whole book.

Valentina: I agree. What I found most useful was how the advice is 
            structured — short, concrete principles rather than abstract theory.
            I'm interested in re-reading sections on testing because 
            I feel I only absorbed half of it the first time.

Diego:    I remember trying to read it a year ago and stopping after 
           the introduction. I wasn't ready for it yet, I think.
           Coming back to it now, I'm good at understanding the context —
           after a year of actual coding, the advice lands differently.

Ana:      That's a really good point. Technical books are worth 
           reading at different stages. What you need to know shifts 
           as your experience does.

Carlos:   The section I'll try re-reading before the next sprint 
           is the one on estimation. I'm terrible at estimating tasks.

Valentina: Stop blaming the book — that's just you, Carlos.
Ana:      Ha! Fair. Any section that didn't land for you?

Diego:    The chapter on careers felt a bit dated.
           Some of the job market advice was written for a different era.
           I remember reading that section and thinking: 
           "This doesn't describe how I got into development at all."

Ana:      Agreed. The core technical principles age well, 
           the soft advice less so.
           For next month — I'd suggest trying something lighter. 
           Any recommendations?
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I'm interested in re-reading`, `I'm good at understanding`, `I stopped after the introduction` (stop + gerundio = dejé de leer), `I remember trying to read` (remember + gerundio = recuerdo haber intentado), `I'll try re-reading` (try + gerundio = probar como experimento).

### Caso 2 — Recomendación de libro por email

> **Situación:** Sofía le escribe a su equipo recomendando un libro después de leerlo.

```
Subject: Book Recommendation — "Never Split the Difference" by Chris Voss

Hey everyone,

Quick recommendation: I just finished "Never Split the Difference" 
by Chris Voss and I think it's relevant for all of us.

Voss was the FBI's lead hostage negotiator for years.
The book is technically about negotiation, but it's really 
about communication — how to listen properly, how to ask questions 
that make the other person feel heard, and how to get what you need 
without compromising unnecessarily.

Why I think it's worth reading for developers:
- The section on "tactical empathy" is genuinely useful for 
  client conversations and code reviews.
- The chapter on mirroring (repeating the last few words of 
  what someone said to keep them talking) is something 
  I've already started practicing in meetings.
- His point about avoiding "yes/no" questions changed how I 
  frame questions in retrospectives.

I'm not accustomed to reading management or negotiation books — 
I'm more fond of technical reading — but I'm excited about 
applying these ideas in real situations.

It's available on PDF if anyone wants a copy. 
I'd recommend reading at least the first three chapters 
before forming a strong opinion — it starts slowly.

Let me know what you think!
Sofía
```

### Caso 3 — Análisis de un libro para clase de inglés

> **Situación:** Diego escribe un análisis breve para la clase de inglés avanzado en RIWI.

```
Book Analysis: "The Old Man and the Sea" — Ernest Hemingway

"The Old Man and the Sea" is a short novel about Santiago, 
an aging Cuban fisherman who struggles to catch a giant marlin 
after 84 days without a catch. On the surface, it's a story 
about persistence. Beneath it, it's about aging, dignity, 
and what it means to define yourself by your work.

Hemingway is known for his minimalist prose style.
He tends to avoid adjectives, preferring to let action carry meaning.
Reading this novel means learning to read between lines — 
the emotion is never stated directly, only shown.

What I found most powerful was the character of Santiago.
I remember reading about his hands — scarred, strong, bleeding — 
and feeling that each description carried more weight 
than any emotional declaration would have.

The pacing is slow by modern standards. 
I'm accustomed to reading faster-paced fiction, 
so stopping to fully sit with the prose required deliberate effort.
But trying to rush through it means missing the point entirely.

The ending is ambiguous — some readers see it as triumph, 
others as defeat. I believe Hemingway intended both simultaneously.
The old man lost the fish and returned with only the skeleton.
But he went out. He fought. He did not stop trying.

There's something in that worth thinking about.
```

### Caso 4 — Lista de lectura para desarrolladores (artículo)

> **Situación:** RIWI Blog publica una lista de lecturas recomendadas para desarrolladores en formación.

```
10 Books Worth Reading Before Your First Dev Job

A note before the list: reading about software is not a substitute 
for writing software. But reading thoughtfully, and reading the right things,
makes you better at thinking about software. These books do that.

TECHNICAL:
1. "Clean Code" — Robert C. Martin
   Stop writing code that only you can read. Start learning what 
   readable, maintainable code looks like. Reading this early 
   is harder, but re-reading it after a year of experience is worth it.

2. "The Pragmatic Programmer" — Hunt & Thomas
   A collection of principles for how to think about being a developer.
   I'm fond of saying this book aged better than any framework ever will.

3. "You Don't Know JS" — Kyle Simpson (free online)
   Stop assuming you know how JavaScript works. Try reading this 
   to discover how much you were guessing.

CAREER & PROCESS:
4. "The Phoenix Project" — Gene Kim et al.
   A novel about DevOps. Yes, a novel. 
   Reading fiction to learn operations feels strange, 
   but it works better than a textbook.

5. "Deep Work" — Cal Newport
   Not technical, but essential. Being capable of deep, 
   uninterrupted focus is the skill that separates good developers 
   from great ones.

PERSPECTIVE:
6. "Sapiens" — Yuval Noah Harari
   Not about tech at all. But being good at understanding 
   how humans think is directly relevant to building things for humans.

Stop waiting for the perfect time to start reading. 
Pick one. Open it tonight.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Gerundios e infinitivos en distintos contextos

| Estructura | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Gerundio como sujeto | Reading non-fiction expands perspective. |
| Infinitivo de propósito | I read to unwind. |
| Verbo + preposición + gerundio | I'm interested in trying literary fiction. |
| Stop + gerundio (dejar de) | I stopped reading at chapter 3. |
| Stop + infinitivo (detenerse para) | I stopped to read the back cover. |
| Remember + gerundio (recuerdo haber) | I remember reading this years ago. |
| Remember + infinitivo (no olvides) | Remember to return the library book. |
| Try + gerundio (probar como experimento) | Try reading before bed for better sleep. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Interested in` + gerundio (no infinitivo)

❌ I'm interested in to read more.
✅ I'm interested in **reading** more.

### `Good at` + gerundio (no infinitivo)

❌ She's good at to understand subtext.
✅ She's good at **understanding** subtext.

### `Stop to read` vs `stop reading`

- `I stopped **to read**` → me detuve para leer
- `I stopped **reading**` → dejé de leer
