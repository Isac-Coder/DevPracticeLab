## 1. 🎯 Objetivos

- Dominar frases de transición y cierre de conversación.

---

## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Respuestas de seguimiento y question tags

### ¿Por qué son importantes aquí?

El small talk no es un monólogo — es un intercambio de pequeños turnos. Las respuestas de seguimiento y las question tags son las herramientas que mantienen viva la conversación y muestran interés genuino.

---

### Respuestas de seguimiento (Follow-up responses)

Estas respuestas muestran que escuchas y quieres saber más:

| Expresión | Cuándo usarla |
|---|---|
| **Really?** | sorpresa o interés genuino |
| **Oh, interesting!** | algo nuevo o inesperado |
| **That's great / That's too bad.** | reacción positiva / negativa |
| **I know what you mean.** | empatía, acuerdo |
| **I can imagine.** | empatía sin experiencia propia |
| **No kidding?** | sorpresa informal |
| **Tell me more about that.** | pedir más información |
| **How did that go?** | pedir seguimiento de un evento |
| **What was that like?** | pedir descripción de experiencia |

---

### Question Tags

Las question tags convierten afirmaciones en preguntas de confirmación. Son esenciales para el small talk porque invitan al otro a participar.

**Regla:**
- Oración afirmativa → tag negativo
- Oración negativa → tag afirmativo

```
The weather is nice today, isn't it?
You don't work on Fridays, do you?
She's new here, isn't she?
They've met before, haven't they?
```

**Fórmula:**
```
Oración + auxiliar opuesto + pronombre sujeto?
```

| Auxiliar en la oración | Tag |
|---|---|
| am / is / are | isn't / aren't / am I? |
| was / were | wasn't / weren't |
| have / has | haven't / hasn't |
| will | won't |
| can | can't |
| do / does | don't / doesn't |
| did | didn't |

> ⚠️ `I am` → tag especial: `I am right, **aren't I**?` (no `am I not` en conversación)

---

### Entonación de question tags

- **Entonación descendente (↘):** ya sabes la respuesta, solo buscas confirmación.
  - *Nice weather today, **isn't it?** ↘*
- **Entonación ascendente (↗):** genuinamente no estás seguro.
  - *You've been here before, **haven't you?** ↗*

## 3. 📚 Vocabulario esencial

### Temas seguros para small talk

| Inglés | Español |
|---|---|
| the weather | el clima |
| weekend plans / the weekend | planes del fin de semana |
| local news / current events | noticias locales |
| sports | deportes |
| travel | viajes |
| movies / TV | películas / series |
| food / restaurants | comida / restaurantes |
| work (in general terms) | trabajo (en términos generales) |
| mutual connections | conocidos en común |

### Temas a evitar en small talk

| Tema | Por qué evitarlo |
|---|---|
| salary / money | demasiado personal |
| politics / religion | muy divisivos |
| personal appearance | puede ofender |
| relationship status | invasivo |
| age (especialmente a mujeres) | inapropiado culturalmente |
| health problems in detail | incómodo en contextos nuevos |

### Frases para iniciar y cerrar

| Situación | Expresión |
|---|---|
| Iniciar | How's everything going? / How are things? |
| Comentar el entorno | Great event, isn't it? / What a day! |
| Introducir un tema | Have you heard about...? / Did you catch...? |
| Mostrar interés | I was just reading about that. |
| Transición | Anyway... / Well, I should let you go. |
| Cerrar | It was great catching up! / We should do this again. |
| Despedida profesional | I'll let you get back to it. / Let's stay in touch. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Small talk en el ascensor con un colega nuevo

> **Situación:** Diego se encuentra con un colega de otro equipo en el ascensor de la empresa el primer día.

```
Diego:    Good morning! You're new here, aren't you?
Colleague: Ha, is it that obvious? Yes, today is my first day.
            I'm Miguel, from the data team.
Diego:    Welcome! I'm Diego — frontend. 
           It's a great place to work, from what I've seen.
Colleague: Good to know. The office is bigger than I expected.
Diego:    It is, isn't it? I got lost for the first week.
           The cafeteria is on the third floor, by the way — 
           that's the most important thing to know.
Colleague: Ha! Good tip. Is the coffee decent?
Diego:    It's not bad at all. The afternoon pastries are the real highlight though.
Colleague: I'll keep that in mind.
           [elevator opens]
Diego:    This is my floor. Welcome again — hope the first day goes well!
Colleague: Thanks a lot. I'm sure I'll see you around.
Diego:    Definitely. Let me know if you need anything.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `You're new here, aren't you?` — tag afirmación positiva → tag negativo. `It's a great place, isn't it?` — mismo patrón. `The office is bigger than I expected` — comparativo natural en conversación. Respuestas de seguimiento: `Ha, is it that obvious?`, `Good to know`, `I'll keep that in mind`.

### Caso 2 — Small talk en un evento de networking

> **Situación:** Valentina inicia conversación con un desconocido en una conferencia tech.

```
Valentina: Quite a turnout, isn't it? I didn't expect so many people.
Stranger:  Right? I've been to this event three times and it just 
            keeps growing.
Valentina: Oh really? What brings you back every year?
Stranger:  Honestly, the talks are always solid. Last year's keynote 
            on AI ethics was one of the best I've seen. You?
Valentina: First time. I'm a frontend developer — still fairly new 
            to the industry, so I figured this was a good place to learn.
Stranger:  Great choice. Have you seen the schedule for today?
Valentina: I have. I'm most interested in the React panel at 2 PM.
Stranger:  Oh, you're into React? Same here. I've been working with 
            it for about four years.
Valentina: No kidding! Any tips for someone still building experience?
Stranger:  Honestly? Just build things. Break them. Figure out why.
            No course replaces that.
Valentina: That's exactly the approach I've been taking.
            Well, I should grab a coffee before the first talk starts.
            It was great chatting — I'm Valentina, by the way.
Stranger:  Marco. Great to meet you. See you at the React panel maybe!
Valentina: Definitely. Save me a seat!
```

**Nota gramatical:** `Quite a turnout, isn't it?` — tag en small talk de apertura. `Have you seen...?` — Present Perfect para conectar experiencia con el presente. `No kidding!` — respuesta de seguimiento de sorpresa. `I should grab a coffee` — cierre natural de conversación.

### Caso 3 — Small talk con un cliente antes de una reunión

> **Situación:** Ana espera a un cliente en la sala de reuniones y hace small talk mientras llega el resto del equipo.

```
Ana:     Thanks for coming in, Mr. Rodríguez. How was the drive?
Client:  Not bad, actually. The traffic wasn't as bad as I expected 
          for a Friday morning.
Ana:     That's lucky — it can be a nightmare on Fridays, can't it?
Client:  It really can. Though I have to say, this part of the city 
          has improved a lot. There's a new pedestrian zone nearby, isn't there?
Ana:     Yes! They finished it last month. Have you been to this area before?
Client:  A few times, but not recently. I noticed there are a lot more 
          coffee places than before.
Ana:     Ha, it's very much a tech district now. 
          Can I get you anything while we wait for the others?
Client:  I'd love a coffee, thank you.
Ana:     Of course. Do you take it black?
Client:  Black with one sugar, please.
Ana:     [coming back] Here you go. The rest of the team should be 
          here in about five minutes. 
          Is this your first project with our company?
Client:  No, we worked together about two years ago — 
          a smaller scope, but a good experience.
Ana:     Oh great! So you know how we work, then.
Client:  I do. That's partly why I came back.
Ana:     That's really good to hear.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `It can be a nightmare, can't it?` — question tag con modal. `There's a new pedestrian zone, isn't there?` — tag con `there is`. Transición natural de small talk a contexto de reunión al final.

### Caso 4 — Guía de small talk para profesionales colombianos en contextos internacionales

> **Situación:** Un artículo de RIWI Blog sobre comunicación intercultural.

```
Small Talk Across Cultures: What Colombian Professionals Should Know

Small talk is not wasted time. In English-speaking professional cultures — 
particularly in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia — it's a trust-building ritual.
The person who skips small talk entirely can seem cold or abrupt,
even if that's not the intention.

THE BASICS
The most reliable topics are the weather, recent local events, 
the event you're both attending, and light professional topics.
The weather might seem trivial, but it's precisely because it's trivial 
that it works — nobody feels exposed talking about it.

WHAT TO AVOID
Don't ask about salary, marital status, age, or weight.
In Colombia, "¿ya tienes novio/a?" or commenting on someone's appearance 
is normal between acquaintances. In many English-speaking contexts, 
it can feel invasive — even with good intentions.

THE FOLLOW-UP IS EVERYTHING
The difference between good and bad small talk isn't the topic — 
it's what you do with the answer. If someone says "I had a busy week," 
the natural follow-up is "Oh really? What was going on?" — not silence.
Showing curiosity is more important than finding the perfect opening line.

CLOSING GRACEFULLY
Ending a conversation well is just as important as starting it.
"It was great talking to you" + a forward reference ("see you at the panel 
later" / "let's stay in touch") closes cleanly without awkwardness.

One more thing: question tags signal engagement and invite participation.
"Good session, wasn't it?" or "That speaker was impressive, don't you think?"
are natural openers that put both people at ease immediately.

Small talk isn't small. It's the door to everything else.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Question Tags en todos los tiempos verbales

| Tiempo | Oración | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | She works here, | doesn't she? |
| Past Simple | You met him before, | didn't you? |
| Future (will) | They'll be there, | won't they? |
| Present Continuous | He's presenting today, | isn't he? |
| Present Perfect | You've heard the news, | haven't you? |
| Modal (can) | She can help us, | can't she? |
| Modal (should) | We should leave, | shouldn't we? |
| There is/are | There's time, | isn't there? |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Question tag con el auxiliar equivocado

❌ She works here, doesn't she? → She **works** → ✅ doesn't she
❌ She is working, don't she? → She **is** → ✅ isn't she

### Tag después de `Let's`

- *Let's go, **shall we**?* → `shall we` es el tag especial para `let's`

### Usar `isn't it` para todo

❌ You came early, isn't it?
✅ You came early, **didn't you**?
> El tag debe corresponder al tiempo verbal y pronombre de la oración.

### Small talk demasiado directo (calco del español)

❌ "How old are you?" (primer contacto en contexto profesional)
✅ "Have you been coming to this conference long?" (indirecto, abierto)
