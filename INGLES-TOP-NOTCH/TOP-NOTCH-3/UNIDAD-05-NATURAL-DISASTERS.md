## 1. 🎯 Objetivos

- Describir desastres naturales y sus consecuencias con vocabulario específico.
- Reportar noticias y hechos usando la voz pasiva y el estilo periodístico.
- Usar tiempos perfectos para hablar de consecuencias que perduran.
- Expresar causa, resultado y contraste en textos de tipo informativo.

---

## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Pasiva avanzada + Past Perfect en narrativa

### Pasiva en contexto periodístico

Los reportes de desastres usan masivamente la voz pasiva porque el foco está en lo que ocurrió, no en quién lo causó.

**Pasiva con agente implícito (el más común en noticias):**
```
Hundreds of homes were destroyed.      (no sabemos exactamente por qué)
Roads have been blocked by mudslides.  (las rocas son el agente)
Three people are reported missing.     (alguien lo reporta, pero no importa quién)
```

**Pasiva con agente explícito (solo cuando es relevante):**
```
The evacuation was ordered by local authorities.
The building was condemned by the safety board.
```

---

### Past Perfect en narrativa de desastre

El Past Perfect establece qué ocurrió antes en una secuencia de eventos pasados:

```
By the time rescuers arrived, the landslide had buried the road.
Residents had evacuated before the dam broke.
She hadn't expected the flood to reach the second floor.
```

---

### Reported speech en contexto de noticias

```
Officials said that the situation was under control.
Survivors reported that they had heard no warning.
Authorities confirmed that the dam had been inspected.
```

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **declare** a state of emergency | declarar estado de emergencia | The president declared a state of emergency. |
| **order** an evacuation | ordenar una evacuación | Authorities ordered a mandatory evacuation. |
| **issue** a warning | emitir una advertencia | The weather service issued a storm warning. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Reporte noticioso de un terremoto

> **Situación:** Un artículo periodístico cubre un terremoto en Colombia.

```
BREAKING: Magnitude 6.1 Earthquake Strikes Southwestern Colombia

A magnitude 6.1 earthquake struck the Nariño department of southwestern 
Colombia on Thursday morning, leaving at least 14 people dead and 
dozens injured, according to the National Seismological Network.

The earthquake, which struck at 4:42 AM local time, was felt across 
several municipalities. Several buildings were reported to have collapsed 
in the towns of La Unión and El Rosario.

As of 10 AM, 47 people had been confirmed as injured, and six were still 
listed as missing. Emergency services said that rescue operations had been 
deployed to the worst-affected areas before dawn.

Roads leading to the most isolated villages had been blocked by landslides 
before rescue teams could reach them. A state of emergency was declared 
by the governor of Nariño shortly after 6 AM.

A survivor, who asked not to be named, reported that she had felt 
several strong vibrations before the main quake. 
"I hadn't had time to get out of bed," she said.

Authorities warned that aftershocks of up to magnitude 4.5 
were expected in the following 24 hours. Residents were advised 
to stay away from damaged structures.

Relief organizations have confirmed they are mobilizing aid teams.
Donations can be directed through official government channels.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `were reported to have collapsed`, `had been confirmed`, `had been blocked`, `was declared` — pasiva en diferentes tiempos. `she had felt several vibrations`, `I hadn't had time` — Past Perfect en citas de testigos. `were expected`, `were advised` — pasiva modal perfecta.

### Caso 2 — Testimonio de un sobreviviente

> **Situación:** Una entrevista con una persona desplazada por inundaciones en el Chocó.

```
Interviewer: Can you describe what happened the night of the flood?

Survivor:    By the time we realized the river had risen dangerously, 
              the water had already reached the street in front of our house.
              I had gone to sleep thinking the rain would stop by midnight.
              When I woke up at 2 AM, the living room was flooded.
              
Interviewer: Had you received any official warnings?

Survivor:    Yes. Officials had issued a warning two days before — 
              but we had heard warnings before without anything happening.
              By the time this one was confirmed as serious, 
              we hadn't had time to take most of our belongings.
              
Interviewer: Where did you go?

Survivor:    We went to a school that had been set up as an emergency shelter.
              By the time we arrived, the shelter had already received 
              over 200 families. They hadn't expected that many people.
              
Interviewer: What have you lost?

Survivor:    Almost everything. The house hadn't been fully repaired 
              after the last flood two years ago. 
              It's been condemned now — we can't go back.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `had risen`, `had already reached`, `had gone to sleep`, `had issued a warning`, `hadn't had time`, `had been set up`, `had already received`, `hadn't expected`, `hadn't been repaired` — Past Perfect extensivo para establecer secuencia temporal clara.

### Caso 3 — Informe de evaluación de daños (documento oficial)

> **Situación:** Un informe inicial de daños emitido por las autoridades locales.

```
PRELIMINARY DAMAGE ASSESSMENT REPORT
Incident: Flash Flood — Cartagena Metropolitan Area
Date of Incident: October 9, 2025
Report Date: October 11, 2025

OVERVIEW:
On October 9, 2025, heavy rainfall caused flash flooding across 
four districts of the Cartagena metropolitan area.
A state of emergency was declared at 11:45 PM by the Mayor's Office.

AFFECTED POPULATION:
- An estimated 3,400 residents have been displaced.
- 12 individuals were hospitalized with injuries.
- 1 fatality has been confirmed as of this report.
- 4 individuals are reported missing.

INFRASTRUCTURE:
- 18 km of road have been assessed as impassable.
- Three bridges are reported to have sustained structural damage.
- Power supply has been restored to 60% of the affected area.
- Water supply has not yet been restored in districts 2 and 4.

CURRENT STATUS OF RELIEF OPERATIONS:
- Emergency shelters have been established in 6 community centers.
- Food and water have been distributed to an estimated 2,100 individuals.
- Medical teams have been deployed to all shelters.
- A curfew has been imposed in flooded zones for safety reasons.

NEXT STEPS:
Detailed damage assessment teams are scheduled to be deployed 
on October 12th. A formal report will be issued within 72 hours.
```

**Nota gramatical:** Todo el informe usa pasiva sistemáticamente — `was declared`, `have been displaced`, `was hospitalized`, `has been confirmed`, `have been assessed`, `have been established`, `have been distributed`, `has been imposed`. Esta es la voz estándar de los documentos oficiales de emergencia.

### Caso 4 — Análisis de cambio climático y desastres (artículo de opinión)

> **Situación:** Valentina escribe un artículo de opinión para la clase de inglés avanzado.

```
Why Colombia Must Prepare for More — Not Fewer — Natural Disasters

Between 2000 and 2023, the number of climate-related disasters reported 
globally increased by over 80%. Colombia, which sits at the intersection 
of multiple climate risk zones, is not exempt from this trend.

Last year, flooding affected over 50,000 families in the Chocó region alone.
The year before, wildfires destroyed tens of thousands of hectares in Meta.
These events used to be described as exceptional. 
They no longer can be.

The problem is structural. Much of Colombia's most vulnerable population 
lives in areas that were settled without adequate urban planning. 
By the time government attention arrives after a disaster, 
the damage has already been done and communities have been left 
without support for weeks.

Several things should have been done differently over the past decade:
Early warning systems should have been installed more widely.
Risk maps should have been used to regulate construction in flood zones.
Community preparedness programs should have been funded before the crises, 
not after them.

However, dwelling on what should have been done is only useful 
if it changes what is done next.

The government must prioritize building resilience into communities 
that are most exposed — not as charity, but as infrastructure investment.
Because by the time the next disaster strikes, 
the time to prepare will already have passed.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Pasiva en distintos tiempos en contexto periodístico

| Tiempo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Present Simple passive | Three people are reported missing. |
| Past Simple passive | The dam was breached at 3 AM. |
| Present Perfect passive | Hundreds of homes have been destroyed. |
| Past Perfect passive | Roads had been blocked before rescuers arrived. |
| Future passive | Aftershocks are expected in the next 24 hours. |
| Modal passive | Communities should be evacuated immediately. |
| Modal perfect passive | This should have been prevented. |

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Pasiva sin auxiliar `to be`

❌ Hundreds of homes destroyed.
✅ Hundreds of homes **were** destroyed.

### Past Perfect innecesario (cuando el orden es claro)

En narrativa simple, no uses Past Perfect si el orden temporal ya es obvio:
- *She left and then it flooded.* → no necesita Past Perfect
- *By the time she left, the flooding **had already started**.* → necesario porque establece que el flooding comenzó antes

### `Is reported to` — estructura pasiva + infinitivo

*The dam **is reported to have** failed.* ✅
*It **is reported** that the dam failed.* ✅
*The dam **is reported that** it failed.* ❌
