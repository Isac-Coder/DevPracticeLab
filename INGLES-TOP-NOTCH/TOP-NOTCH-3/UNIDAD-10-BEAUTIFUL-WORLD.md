## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Estructuras de causa y consecuencia avanzadas

### Expresiones de causa

| Expresión | Registro | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **because of / due to** | neutral | Due to deforestation, many species are losing habitat. |
| **as a result of** | formal | As a result of climate change, sea levels are rising. |
| **owing to** | formal/escrito | Owing to pollution, coral reefs are dying at record rates. |
| **on account of** | formal | On account of illegal mining, the river was contaminated. |
| **stem from** | verbo | The problem stems from industrial waste dumping. |
| **be attributed to** | pasiva | The increase in flooding is attributed to deforestation. |
| **give rise to** | verbo | Warming temperatures give rise to more intense storms. |
| **be caused by** | pasiva | Species decline is caused by habitat destruction. |

---

### Expresiones de consecuencia

| Expresión | Registro | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **therefore / thus** | formal | Glaciers are melting; therefore, sea levels rise. |
| **as a result / consequently** | formal/neutral | Species are disappearing; as a result, ecosystems destabilize. |
| **lead to / result in** | verbo | Overfishing leads to the collapse of marine food chains. |
| **have an impact on** | verbo | Climate change has a significant impact on agriculture. |
| **trigger** | verbo (fuerte) | A small temperature increase can trigger mass coral bleaching. |
| **contribute to** | verbo (parcial) | Cattle farming contributes to greenhouse gas emissions. |
| **give way to** | verbo (reemplazo) | Forests give way to agricultural land every year. |

---

### Estructuras de propósito y solución

| Expresión | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| **in order to** | In order to protect biodiversity, strict regulations are needed. |
| **so as to** | Protected areas are created so as to preserve native species. |
| **with the aim of** | Governments act with the aim of reducing carbon emissions. |
| **designed to** | The policy was designed to limit deforestation. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Presentación sobre la biodiversidad de Colombia

> **Situación:** Sofía hace una presentación en inglés para una clase internacional sobre por qué Colombia es un país especial desde el punto de vista ambiental.

```
"Colombia is home to approximately 10% of all species on Earth —
in a territory that covers less than 1% of the planet's surface.

This extraordinary biodiversity stems from several factors.
Due to its location at the intersection of two oceans and two continents,
Colombia receives species from North and South America.
Owing to the presence of the Andes mountain range,
the country contains multiple climate zones within short distances —
from glaciers to tropical lowlands.
As a result, it has developed ecosystems that exist nowhere else on Earth.

The Chocó biogeographic region, for example, is one of the wettest 
and most biodiverse places on the planet.
The Amazon basin, which extends into southern Colombia, 
contains more tree species per hectare than any forest in the world.
The coral reefs of the Caribbean and Pacific coasts 
support thousands of marine species.

However, this richness is under serious threat.
Deforestation, largely due to coca cultivation, cattle ranching, 
and illegal mining, has destroyed millions of hectares of primary forest.
Consequently, hundreds of endemic species — those found nowhere else — 
are now at risk of extinction.

The good news is that Colombia has some of the most 
progressive environmental legislation in Latin America.
Protected areas have been expanded, community conservation programs 
have been established, and sustainable ecotourism is growing.

But laws alone are not enough.
In order to truly protect this heritage,
economic alternatives must be developed for communities 
that currently depend on extractive industries.
Conservation without human development fails. Always."
```

**Nota gramatical:** `Due to`, `Owing to`, `As a result`, `Consequently`, `in order to` — causa y consecuencia distribuidos naturalmente. `stems from`, `give rise to`, `is attributed to` — variedad de verbos causales. Pasiva en leyes y políticas: `have been expanded`, `have been established`.

### Caso 2 — Artículo sobre turismo responsable

> **Situación:** Diego escribe para el blog de viajes de RIWI antes de un viaje al Tayrona.

```
How to Visit Colombia's Natural Parks Without Destroying Them

The Tayrona National Park receives over 700,000 visitors per year.
It is one of the most biodiverse areas in the Caribbean basin —
and one of the most threatened by its own popularity.

The paradox of ecotourism is well documented:
the desire to experience natural beauty gives rise to pressure 
that damages the very thing people came to see.
Overcrowding results in soil compaction, wildlife disturbance, 
waste accumulation, and coral damage in marine zones.

So what does responsible visiting actually look like?

1. STAY ON MARKED TRAILS
Trails exist in order to concentrate human impact on areas 
that can handle it. Going off-trail contributes to habitat fragmentation
and exposes wildlife to human contact. Even well-intentioned exploration 
can trigger stress responses in endemic species.

2. MINIMIZE WASTE
All waste should be carried out of the park.
As a result of careless waste disposal, invasive plant species 
have colonized several areas of Tayrona that were previously pristine.

3. RESPECT ACCESS LIMITS
Some beaches and zones are periodically closed to allow recovery.
On account of repeated overuse, Cabo San Juan was closed for several months.
Respecting these limits is not an inconvenience — it's what makes 
the park survivable for the species that live there.

4. SUPPORT LOCAL COMMUNITIES
Staying at locally-owned accommodation, 
hiring local guides, and buying from community vendors 
means tourism revenue stays in the region 
and creates economic incentives for conservation, not extraction.

5. OFFSET YOUR TRAVEL
If you fly to reach the park, your carbon footprint is significant.
Contributing to verified reforestation programs in the region
doesn't erase the impact, but it contributes to something 
larger than the trip itself.

The goal of visiting a place like Tayrona isn't to consume it.
It's to be temporarily part of it — and leave it intact for the next person.
```

### Caso 3 — Conversación sobre cambio climático

> **Situación:** Carlos y Valentina hablan mientras ven las noticias sobre el deshielo de los glaciares colombianos.

```
Carlos:    Did you see this? The Ritacuba Blanco glacier in the Sierra Nevada 
            is expected to disappear within 20 years.
            
Valentina: That's devastating. And it's not just symbolic — 
            those glaciers feed the rivers that supply water to entire regions.
            As a result of their disappearance, communities downstream 
            will face serious water scarcity.

Carlos:    I knew the glaciers were retreating, but I didn't realize 
            the timeline was that short.

Valentina: It's a compounding problem. The warming triggers faster melting,
            which exposes darker rock, which absorbs more heat,
            which accelerates the warming further. Once it starts, 
            it's very hard to reverse.

Carlos:    Is there anything that can actually be done at this point?

Valentina: Slowing emissions can slow the rate of change —
            it doesn't reverse what's happened, but it buys time.
            There are also geoengineering proposals, 
            though those come with their own risks.

Carlos:    What about local adaptation? 
            Communities can't just wait for global policy to change.

Valentina: Exactly — and that's actually where some of the most interesting 
            work is happening. Reforestation of the highland zones 
            that feed the glaciers helps retain water.
            Water storage infrastructure can compensate partially.
            And importantly, indigenous communities in many of these areas 
            have traditional knowledge about water management 
            that's been largely ignored by official policy.

Carlos:    I didn't know that.

Valentina: Most people don't. Which is part of the problem.
```

### Caso 4 — Propuesta de proyecto: app de turismo sostenible

> **Situación:** Ana presenta una propuesta de capstone conectando tecnología y medio ambiente.

```
PROJECT PROPOSAL: "TerraRoute" — Sustainable Travel for Colombia

PROBLEM:
Colombia's natural areas are under threat from unregulated tourism.
Due to the lack of accessible information about responsible travel,
many visitors inadvertently cause damage — not out of malice,
but out of ignorance.

PROPOSED SOLUTION:
TerraRoute is a mobile-first web application designed to help travelers
plan visits to natural areas responsibly.

CORE FEATURES:
1. Smart Route Planning
   Users enter their destination and the app calculates routes 
   that minimize environmental impact — avoiding overcrowded zones, 
   highlighting community-owned accommodation, 
   and flagging seasonal closures.

2. Real-Time Capacity Data
   Integrated with park authority APIs to show current visitor numbers.
   As a result, users can plan visits during low-traffic periods
   so as to reduce pressure on sensitive ecosystems.

3. Carbon Footprint Calculator
   Calculates travel emissions and connects users to verified 
   Colombian reforestation projects for offsetting.

4. Community Guides
   Local community members are featured as recommended guides,
   in order to ensure that tourism revenue benefits the communities 
   closest to the natural areas.

WHY THIS MATTERS:
Sustainable tourism generates income without extraction.
If done well, it gives rise to economic incentives that make 
conservation more viable than deforestation.
This app won't save the planet — but it could make 
thousands of individual trips meaningfully less harmful,
and that compounds over time.

TECH STACK: React + TypeScript, REST API integration, Mapbox GL, Node.js backend.

This is the kind of project that means something beyond the grade.
```

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Due to` + cláusula (debe ir con noun phrase)

❌ Due to the forest was destroyed, species are disappearing.
✅ **Due to** the destruction of the forest, species are disappearing.
✅ **Because** the forest was destroyed, species are disappearing.

### `Affect` vs `effect`

- `affect` → verbo: *Climate change **affects** biodiversity.*
- `effect` → sustantivo: *The **effect** of climate change on biodiversity is significant.*

### `Contribute to` + gerundio o sustantivo

❌ Cattle farming contributes to produce emissions.
✅ Cattle farming contributes to **producing** emissions.
✅ Cattle farming contributes to **greenhouse gas emissions**.

## 8. 🧪 Ejercicios

### A — Conecta causa y consecuencia

Une las oraciones usando el conector indicado:

1. Temperatures are rising. Coral reefs are bleaching. (as a result of)
2. Illegal mining continues. Rivers are being contaminated. (owing to)
3. Protected areas were expanded. Biodiversity has recovered in some zones. (as a result)
4. Tourists leave waste. Invasive species colonize new areas. (lead to)

### B — `Due to` o `because`

1. ___ the drought, crop yields fell significantly.
2. ___ it was a drought year, crop yields fell significantly.
3. ___ deforestation, the water table has dropped.
4. ___ people cut down trees, the water table has dropped.
