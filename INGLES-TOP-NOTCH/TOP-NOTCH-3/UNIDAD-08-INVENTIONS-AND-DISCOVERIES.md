## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Pasiva en todos los tiempos + especulación

### Pasiva para inventos e historia de la ciencia

La pasiva es la voz natural de la historia científica porque:
- El invento es más importante que el inventor (foco en el objeto)
- Muchos descubrimientos son de autoría colectiva o disputada
- Es el estilo de los textos académicos y divulgativos

**Pasiva completa con tiempos:**

| Tiempo | Estructura | Ejemplo en contexto |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | is/are + PP | The algorithm is used by billions daily. |
| Past Simple | was/were + PP | The printing press was invented in 1440. |
| Present Perfect | has/have been + PP | The model has been refined over decades. |
| Past Perfect | had been + PP | The concept had been theorized before the hardware existed. |
| Future | will be + PP | The technology will be used in medical diagnosis. |
| Modal | can/must/should be + PP | This data must be verified. |
| Continuous | is being + PP | A new vaccine is being developed. |

---

### Especulación sobre el futuro (necesario en esta unidad)

| Expresión | Certeza | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **will** + verb | alta certeza | AI will transform medicine. |
| **is likely to** | probable | Quantum computing is likely to disrupt encryption. |
| **may / might** + verb | posible | It may become the standard within a decade. |
| **could** + verb | posible (menos probable) | This discovery could lead to a cure. |
| **is expected to** | predicción con base | Automation is expected to replace 15% of jobs. |
| **it is possible that** | posibilidad | It is possible that fusion energy will be viable by 2040. |

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **make** a discovery | hacer un descubrimiento | Fleming made the discovery by accident. |
| **have** an impact on | tener un impacto en | The internet has had an enormous impact on society. |
| **lead to** a breakthrough | llevar a un avance | The research led to a major breakthrough. |
| **change the course of** history | cambiar el curso de la historia | The transistor changed the course of computing history. |
| **give rise to** | dar lugar a | The steam engine gave rise to the Industrial Revolution. |
| **be credited with** | ser acreditado por | Berners-Lee is credited with inventing the World Wide Web. |
| **stand the test of time** | resistir el paso del tiempo | Some technologies stand the test of time; others don't. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Debate: ¿Cuál es el invento más importante de la historia?

> **Situación:** Clase de inglés avanzado en RIWI — debate sobre el invento más importante.

```
Instructor: You've each been assigned an invention to defend.
             Carlos — the printing press.

Carlos:     The printing press was invented by Gutenberg around 1440.
             Before it, books had to be copied by hand — they were 
             luxury objects owned only by institutions and the very wealthy.
             After it was developed, knowledge could be mass-produced 
             and distributed. Within 50 years, millions of books 
             had been printed across Europe.
             This laid the groundwork for the Scientific Revolution, 
             the Reformation, and arguably democracy itself.
             Without it, the systems we now take for granted 
             would not have been developed.

Valentina:  I'd argue for antibiotics. Millions of people were dying 
             of bacterial infections that can now be treated with a pill.
             Before penicillin was discovered by Fleming in 1928,
             a scratch could kill you. The average life expectancy 
             was dramatically shortened by infections we now forget exist.
             Every other innovation in medicine has been built on 
             the foundation that antibiotics established.

Diego:      I'd say the transistor — and I'll explain why.
             The transistor was invented in 1947 at Bell Labs.
             Without it, every piece of digital technology we use today 
             would not exist. The internet, the smartphone, AI — 
             all of it is built on the transistor.
             It may be the least famous invention in history 
             but it is arguably the most consequential.

Instructor: All three make compelling cases. 
             What they have in common is that they paved the way 
             for systems larger than themselves.
             Diego, is the transistor a discovery or an invention?

Diego:      Good question. The semiconductor properties it's built on 
             were discovered — but the transistor itself was engineered.
             The distinction matters because discoveries can't be patented,
             but inventions can. The transistor was patented.
```

### Caso 2 — Artículo divulgativo: El impacto de la IA en el trabajo

> **Situación:** Sofía escribe un artículo para el blog de RIWI sobre inteligencia artificial.

```
Artificial Intelligence: What Has Changed and What Is About to Change

Artificial intelligence has been theorized since the 1950s.
The term was coined by John McCarthy in 1956, 
when a research project was organized at Dartmouth College.
For decades, the field developed slowly — constrained by hardware limitations.

The turning point came with the availability of large datasets 
and the development of deep learning architectures. 
By the 2010s, AI systems had been trained to recognize images, 
translate languages, and play chess better than any human.

Today, large language models have been deployed across industries.
Legal research that used to require days can now be done in hours.
Medical images are being analyzed by algorithms trained on 
millions of scans. Code is being generated by systems 
that have been trained on billions of lines.

What is expected to change next?
The replacement of routine cognitive tasks is already happening.
Entry-level positions in law, accounting, and data analysis 
are likely to be significantly reduced in the next decade.
This doesn't mean the end of those professions —
it means they will be transformed, as manufacturing was 
transformed by automation in the 20th century.

As a developer, I find this both exciting and sobering.
The tools I work with are likely to become dramatically more powerful.
The skills that can't be automated — judgment, creativity, context — 
will become more valuable. 
The ones that can be — routine code generation, boilerplate — 
are already being replaced.

The question isn't whether AI will change our field.
It already has. The question is what we build it toward.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `has been theorized`, `was coined`, `was organized`, `had been trained`, `have been deployed`, `is being analyzed`, `is being generated`, `has been trained` — pasiva en múltiples tiempos narrando historia y estado actual. `is likely to`, `are expected to` — especulación sobre el futuro.

### Caso 3 — Línea de tiempo de inventos (texto informativo)

> **Situación:** Material educativo para clase — línea de tiempo de los inventos del siglo XX.

```
THE CENTURY OF TRANSFORMATION: KEY INVENTIONS 1900–2000

1903 — The airplane was successfully flown by the Wright brothers 
        for the first time. Powered flight had been attempted before, 
        but sustained, controlled flight had never been achieved.

1928 — Penicillin was discovered accidentally by Alexander Fleming 
        when a mold was observed to be killing bacteria in his petri dish.
        It would not be developed into a usable drug until the 1940s.

1947 — The transistor was invented at Bell Labs by Shockley, Bardeen, 
        and Brattain. Within years, it would be miniaturized and embedded 
        into every electronic device made thereafter.

1953 — The structure of DNA was described by Watson and Crick, 
        based on X-ray work by Rosalind Franklin, 
        whose contribution was not fully credited until decades later.

1969 — The first message was sent over ARPANET — 
        the network that would eventually become the internet.
        It was supposed to say "login." 
        The system crashed after the first two letters. 
        The first word ever transmitted over the internet was "lo."

1991 — The World Wide Web was made publicly available by Tim Berners-Lee.
        It had been developed at CERN, initially as a way 
        to share scientific documents between researchers.

2000s — The smartphone was developed and popularized, 
         combining tools that had been invented separately 
         (phone, camera, GPS, internet browser) into a single device.
         Within a decade, it would be owned by half the world's population.
```

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Discover` vs `invent`

- `discover` → algo que ya existía pero era desconocido (penicilina, ADN)
- `invent` → algo nuevo creado por humanos (transistor, imprenta)

❌ Fleming invented penicillin.
✅ Fleming **discovered** penicillin.

### `Be credited with` + gerundio

❌ He is credited with to invent the web.
✅ He is credited with **inventing** the web.

### Pasiva sin auxiliar

❌ The transistor invented in 1947.
✅ The transistor **was** invented in 1947.
