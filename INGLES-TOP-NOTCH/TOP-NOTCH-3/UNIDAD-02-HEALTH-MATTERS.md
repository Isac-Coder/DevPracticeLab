## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Expresiones de necesidad y recomendación con subjuntivo

### El subjuntivo en inglés

El subjuntivo es menos visible en inglés que en español, pero existe. Aparece especialmente después de verbos de recomendación, sugerencia, insistencia y exigencia.

```
Verb of recommendation + that + subject + verb (base form)
```

| Verbo desencadenante | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| recommend | The doctor **recommended that** she **rest** for a week. |
| suggest | He **suggested that** I **take** ibuprofen. |
| insist | She **insisted that** he **see** a specialist. |
| require | The hospital **requires that** patients **arrive** 30 minutes early. |
| advise | The pharmacist **advised that** he **stop** the medication. |
| demand | He **demanded that** the doctor **explain** the side effects. |
| propose | She **proposed that** they **try** acupuncture. |
| urge | I **urge that** you **consult** a professional. |

> ⚠️ El verbo después de `that` va en **forma base** para todos los sujetos, sin `-s`:
> - *The doctor recommended that she **rest**.* (no `rests`)
> - *He insisted that she **be** hospitalized.* (no `is`)

---

### Alternativas más comunes en inglés cotidiano

El subjuntivo formal se usa más en inglés escrito y americano. En conversación, estas alternativas son más frecuentes:

| Formal (subjuntivo) | Informal (alternativa) |
|---|---|
| She recommended that I **see** a doctor. | She recommended **seeing** a doctor. |
| He suggested that I **take** a rest. | He suggested **taking** a rest. |
| I advise that you **stop** the medication. | I advise you **to stop** the medication. |

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **run** tests | hacer pruebas/exámenes | The doctor ran several tests. |
| **prescribe** medication | recetar medicación | She was prescribed antibiotics. |
| **take** medication | tomar medicación | Take the pills twice a day with food. |

| **have** a checkup | hacerse un chequeo | You should have a checkup once a year. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Consulta médica detallada

> **Situación:** Ana visita al médico con síntomas persistentes que han afectado su productividad en RIWI.

```
Doctor:   Good morning, Ana. What brings you in today?
Ana:      Hi, Doctor. I've been having some issues for about three weeks now.
          I have chronic headaches — mostly behind my eyes — 
          and I've been feeling exhausted even after a full night's sleep.
          I also have some numbness in my right hand occasionally.
Doctor:   I see. Is the numbness constant or intermittent?
Ana:      Intermittent. It usually happens after I've been typing for a while.
Doctor:   That could be related to posture or repetitive strain.
          Are you working long hours at a computer?
Ana:      Yes — 8 to 10 hours a day, minimum.
Doctor:   Any dizziness? Blurred vision? Changes in appetite?
Ana:      Some blurred vision when I've been staring at the screen too long.
          No dizziness, but I've had trouble sleeping despite being tired.
Doctor:   I'm going to recommend that we run a blood test — 
          I want to rule out anemia and check your thyroid.
          I'd also suggest that you see a physiotherapist 
          about the hand numbness — it sounds like it could be 
          carpal tunnel or a nerve compression issue.
Ana:      Should I be concerned?
Doctor:   Not yet. It's important that you don't ignore it, 
          but these are very common issues in people who do desk work.
          I strongly advise that you take regular breaks — 
          every 45 minutes, step away from the screen.
          And I insist that you prioritize sleep this week.
Ana:      What about the headaches in the meantime?
Doctor:   You can take ibuprofen as needed, but I recommend 
          that you not exceed 400mg per dose.
          I'll also refer you to an ophthalmologist to check your eyesight —
          eye strain often causes exactly these symptoms.
Ana:      That makes a lot of sense. Thank you.
Doctor:   We'll call you when the blood test results are in. 
          That should be within 48 hours.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I'm going to recommend that we run`, `I'd suggest that you see`, `It's important that you don't ignore it`, `I advise that you take`, `I insist that you prioritize`, `I recommend that you not exceed` — subjuntivo y estructuras de recomendación médica formal.

### Caso 2 — Correo al médico de cabecera (seguimiento)

> **Situación:** Diego hace seguimiento por email después de una visita médica.

```
Subject: Follow-up — Appointment October 8th — Diego Vargas

Dear Dr. Herrera,

I'm writing to follow up on my appointment last Wednesday.

You recommended that I start the anti-inflammatory medication 
and suggested that I rest my knee for at least a week.
I've been following both recommendations.

However, I wanted to report that the swelling has not decreased significantly.
In fact, yesterday morning I noticed the inflammation seemed worse, 
and I've had increased pain when walking up stairs.

You mentioned that if the inflammation didn't improve within five days, 
it would be advisable that I return for a follow-up scan.
Today is day six with no improvement.

Could you advise on whether I should come in for the scan this week?
I'd prefer to resolve this before it affects my ability to work further.

Additionally, the physiotherapist you referred me to 
has proposed that we start sessions next Monday, 
pending your approval.

Thank you for your time. I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards,
Diego Vargas
Patient ID: DVR-2025-441
```

**Nota gramatical:** `You recommended that I start`, `suggested that I rest` — subjuntivo en reporte. `it would be advisable that I return` — subjuntivo formal. `has proposed that we start` — subjuntivo presente en reporte de tercero.

### Caso 3 — Debate sobre medicina convencional vs. alternativa

> **Situación:** Un hilo de debate en el foro interno de RIWI sobre salud.

```
Carlos:   Question for the group — does anyone here use alternative medicine?
           My doctor suggested I try acupuncture for my back pain 
           and I'm skeptical.

Valentina: I've tried it! I was in the same position as you — 
            very skeptical. But after six sessions, my chronic neck pain 
            improved noticeably. I'm not sure if it was the acupuncture itself
            or just the forced hour of stillness per week, but it worked.

Ana:       I think it's important to distinguish between things that 
            have evidence behind them and things that don't.
            Acupuncture has decent research support for certain types of pain.
            Homeopathy, on the other hand, doesn't have reliable evidence.

Diego:     Exactly. The issue with alternative medicine as a broad category 
            is that it lumps together things like mindfulness and meditation —
            which are well-researched — with things like crystal healing,
            which have no evidence base at all.

Carlos:    So the recommendation would be... try acupuncture?

Ana:       I'd suggest that you approach it as a complement, not a replacement.
            Keep seeing your regular doctor. The physiotherapy your doctor 
            recommended should remain the primary treatment.
            But if acupuncture helps alongside it, that's fine.

Valentina: I'd also urge that you tell your doctor you're trying it.
            Some practitioners don't ask, but it can affect treatment plans.

Carlos:    That's actually really practical advice. Thanks, all.
```

### Caso 4 — Artículo informativo: cómo hablar con tu médico en inglés

> **Situación:** RIWI Blog publica una guía para estudiantes que pueden necesitar atención médica en el extranjero.

```
How to Communicate Effectively with an English-Speaking Doctor

Whether you're traveling, studying, or working abroad, 
being able to describe your symptoms clearly can make a real difference.

DESCRIBING SYMPTOMS
Be specific about: location, intensity (1-10 scale), duration, 
and whether it's constant or comes and goes.

Useful phrases:
- "I've been having [symptom] for [time]."
- "It started [when] and has been [getting better/worse/the same]."
- "The pain is [sharp/dull/burning/throbbing]."
- "It's worse when I [activity] and better when I [rest/move]."
- "I haven't had this before." / "This is a recurring issue."

ASKING FOR CLARIFICATION
Doctors speak quickly and use technical vocabulary. It's okay to ask:
- "Could you explain what that means in simpler terms?"
- "What are the treatment options?"
- "What should I do if it gets worse?"
- "Are there any side effects I should watch for?"

WHEN GIVEN RECOMMENDATIONS
Make sure you understand the instruction fully:
- "Just to confirm — I should take this twice a day, with food?"
- "For how long should I continue the medication?"
- "Is this a prescription or can I get it over the counter?"

Being clear matters. Medical staff appreciate patients who ask questions —
it helps them treat you more accurately.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Subjuntivo y recomendaciones en distintos tiempos verbales

| Estructura | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Presente formal | I recommend **that** you **see** a doctor. |
| Pasado (reporte) | She recommended **that** I **rest** for a week. |
| Con modal formal | It is essential **that** he **be** monitored. |
| Alternativa informal (presente) | I suggest **seeing** a specialist. |
| Alternativa informal (pasado) | She suggested **taking** more iron. |
| Alternativa con infinitivo | I advise you **to stop** the medication. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Añadir `-s` al verbo en subjuntivo

❌ The doctor recommended that she **rests**.
✅ The doctor recommended that she **rest**.

### Confundir `suggest + gerundio` con `suggest + that`

- *She suggested **seeing** a specialist.* ✅
- *She suggested **that** I **see** a specialist.* ✅
- *She suggested **me to see** a specialist.* ❌ — esta construcción no existe en inglés

### `Prescribe` ≠ `describe`

❌ The doctor described me antibiotics.
✅ The doctor **prescribed** me antibiotics.
