## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Primer Condicional (If + Present Simple, will + verb)

### ¿Por qué se usa?

El primer condicional habla de situaciones **reales y posibles en el futuro**. Es perfecto para tomar decisiones de compra, negociar, y hablar de consecuencias financieras reales.

---

### Estructura

```
If + Present Simple, will + verb (base)
```

| Parte | Tiempo | Ejemplo completo |
|---|---|---|
| If clause (condición) | Present Simple | **If I save** $100 this month, |
| Main clause (resultado) | will + verb | I **will buy** the new phone. |

> ⚠️ La cláusula con `if` usa **Present Simple**, nunca `will`:
> ❌ If I will save...
> ✅ If I save...

---

### Variaciones

**El orden puede invertirse** (sin coma cuando `if` va al final):
- *If I find a better price, I'll come back.*
- *I'll come back if I find a better price.*

**Otras palabras para condicionales reales:**
- `unless` = if not: *I'll buy it **unless** it's too expensive.*
- `as long as` = provided that: *I'll get it **as long as** the quality is good.*
- `when` (con certeza): *When I get paid, I'll pay you back.*

---

### Primer Condicional con modales

En la cláusula principal, otros modales también son posibles:

| Modal | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| will | If it's on sale, I'll get two. |
| can | If you pay cash, you **can** get a discount. |
| should | If it breaks, you **should** return it. |
| might | If I save enough, I **might** buy a laptop. |

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **save up** for something | ahorrar para algo | I'm saving up for a new laptop. |
| **spend money on** something | gastar dinero en algo | She spends a lot on clothes. |
| **run out of** money | quedarse sin dinero | We ran out of money before the trip ended. |
| **cut back on** spending | reducir gastos | I need to cut back on eating out. |
| **afford** something | poder costear algo | I can't afford a new phone right now. |
| **get a good deal** | conseguir una buena oferta | I got a great deal on these shoes. |
| **pay off** a debt/loan | saldar una deuda/préstamo | She'll pay off her loan in two years. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Regateando en un mercado de artesanías

> **Situación:** Carlos está de viaje en Cartagena y quiere comprar recuerdos en el mercado de artesanías.

```
Vendor:  Good afternoon! Come look! Best prices in the market.
Carlos:  Hi! How much is this mochila bag?
Vendor:  For you, $120,000.
Carlos:  That's a bit expensive. What if I buy two — 
          would you give me a better price?
Vendor:  If you buy two, I'll give you both for $210,000.
Carlos:  Hmm. I'll give you $180,000 for both. Cash.
Vendor:  Sir, these are handmade! $200,000 is my best price.
Carlos:  If you come down to $190,000, I'll take them right now.
         I'm also looking at that hammock over there.
Vendor:  If you take the two bags AND the hammock, I'll give you 
         all three for $280,000. That's a saving of $60,000!
Carlos:  Deal! As long as you wrap them up nicely — 
         they're gifts.
Vendor:  Of course! Thank you, sir. Come back and see us!
```

**Nota gramatical:** `If you buy two, I'll give you` — primer condicional para negociación. `If you come down to..., I'll take them` — condición real. `As long as you wrap them up` — variante de condicional.

### Caso 2 — Planificando el presupuesto mensual (conversación)

> **Situación:** Valentina y su compañera de apartamento hablan sobre cómo repartir los gastos.

```
Valentina: Okay, we need to sort out this month's budget.
Sofía:     Right. If we split the rent and utilities equally,
            we each pay about $450,000.
Valentina: That works. What about groceries?
Sofía:     If we shop together on weekends, we'll save more.
            Buying in bulk is almost always cheaper.
Valentina: Agreed. And if we cook at home five days a week,
            we won't spend as much on eating out.
Sofía:     I can do that. Though if you cook, I'll do the dishes.
            Fair?
Valentina: Ha! Deal. What about internet?
Sofía:     It's $80,000. We can split it.
Valentina: Fine. And if either of us has a bad month financially,
            we help each other out, right?
Sofía:     Of course. Unless it becomes a regular thing!
Valentina: Ha! Let's hope not.
```

### Caso 3 — Email de negociación con un proveedor

> **Situación:** Diego negocia el precio de un servicio de diseño para el proyecto final de RIWI.

```
Subject: Project Quote — Potential Collaboration

Hi Sandra,

Thank you for sending over your portfolio. Your work is excellent 
and we're very interested in working with you on our upcoming project.

However, your quoted price of $800 USD is above our current budget.
We have a limit of $550 USD for this scope.

Here's our thinking:
- If you can work within our $550 budget, we'll give you full creative freedom.
- If the project goes well, we'll definitely hire you for our next phase.
- If you complete the work by November 10th, we'll pay 50% upfront 
  and 50% on delivery.

We believe this is a fair offer for both sides.
If you're open to negotiating, we'd love to get on a quick call 
this week to discuss.

Please let me know.

Best regards,
Diego Vargas
RIWI — Capstone Team
```

**Nota gramatical:** Tres condicionales encadenados para presentar una propuesta de negociación. `If... we'll...` — primer condicional cada vez.

### Caso 4 — Artículo de finanzas personales para estudiantes

> **Situación:** El blog de RIWI publica consejos de finanzas para estudiantes.

```
5 Money Rules Every Student Should Know

1. PAY YOURSELF FIRST
If you wait until the end of the month to save, you probably won't.
Instead, if you transfer 10% of your income to savings the day you get paid,
you'll build the habit automatically.

2. TRACK YOUR SPENDING
If you don't know where your money goes, you can't control it.
Start by writing down every expense for one week. 
If you do this consistently, you'll find at least 2 or 3 things 
you can cut without missing them.

3. AVOID HIGH-INTEREST DEBT
Credit cards are useful, but only if you pay them in full each month.
If you carry a balance, you'll pay much more than the original price.
Unless the interest rate is below 10%, avoid carrying debt.

4. HAVE AN EMERGENCY FUND
If your phone breaks, your laptop dies, or you miss a payment,
you'll be in serious trouble without savings.
Even $200 in an emergency fund will prevent a lot of stress.

5. INVEST IN YOURSELF
If you spend money on education, books, or courses, you'll almost always 
get a return. Your skills are the best investment you have as a student.
If you graduate with a skill set that companies need,
the salary increase will more than cover what you spent learning.

Start small. Stay consistent. If you follow these five rules,
your financial life will look very different in two years.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Primer Condicional en distintos contextos

| Variación | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Condicional estándar | If I save enough, I'll buy a laptop. |
| Con `unless` | I'll buy it unless the price goes up. |
| Con `when` (certeza) | When I get paid, I'll pay you back. |
| Con `as long as` | I'll lend you the money as long as you return it Friday. |
| Con modal en vez de `will` | If I pass the exam, I might take a vacation. |
| Pregunta con condicional | What will you do if you don't get the job? |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Will` en la cláusula `if`

❌ If I will save money, I will buy a phone.
✅ If I **save** money, I will buy a phone.

### Confundir primer y segundo condicional

- 1st: situación real y posible: *If I **get** the job, I **will** celebrate.*
- 2nd: situación hipotética/irreal: *If I **got** the job, I **would** celebrate.*

### Olvidar la coma cuando `if` va primero

❌ If I find a better deal I'll let you know.
✅ If I find a better deal**,** I'll let you know.
> (No se necesita coma si `if` va al final)
