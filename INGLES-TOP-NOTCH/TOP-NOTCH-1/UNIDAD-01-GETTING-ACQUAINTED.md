## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Wh- Questions en Present Simple

### ¿Por qué se usan?

Las preguntas con `Wh-` son la herramienta principal para conocer a alguien. Cubren: identidad, origen, trabajo, razones, tiempo y lugar. Dominarlas es esencial para cualquier conversación de primer contacto.

---

### Estructura

```
Wh- word + auxiliary (do/does) + subject + verb (base)?
```

| Wh- word | Pregunta por | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | cosas, información | What do you do? |
| **Where** | lugar | Where are you from? |
| **When** | tiempo | When did you start? |
| **Why** | razón | Why do you like this job? |
| **Who** | persona | Who do you work with? |
| **How** | manera / estado | How do you get to work? |
| **How long** | duración | How long have you lived here? |
| **How often** | frecuencia | How often do you travel? |
| **How many/much** | cantidad | How many languages do you speak? |

---

### Preguntas con TO BE (sin auxiliar `do`)

Cuando el verbo es `to be`, no se usa `do/does`:

```
Where are you from?         (no: Where do you be from?)
What is your job?           (no: What does your job be?)
How old are you?
```

---

### Preguntas de sujeto vs. preguntas de objeto

- Pregunta de **objeto** (lo normal): `What do you study?` → sujeto = you
- Pregunta de **sujeto**: `Who teaches your class?` → `who` es el sujeto → NO usa `do`

❌ Who does teach your class?
✅ Who teaches your class?

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **be from** | ser de | Where are you from? — I'm from Cartagena. |
| **grow up in** | crecer en | I grew up in Medellín. |
| **work for** | trabajar para | She works for a tech company. |
| **work in** | trabajar en (sector) | He works in healthcare. |
| **be in charge of** | estar a cargo de | She's in charge of the frontend team. |
| **be interested in** | estar interesado en | I'm interested in mobile development. |
| **have in common** | tener en común | We have a lot in common. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Primer día de trabajo (conversación informal)

> **Situación:** Sofía llega a su primer día en una startup y conoce a sus compañeros durante el almuerzo.

```
Luis:   Hi! You must be Sofía. I'm Luis — I'm on the backend team.
Sofía:  Hi Luis! Yes, that's me. Nice to meet you.
Luis:   Welcome! Where are you from? Your accent sounds like Barranquilla.
Sofía:  Ha! You're right — I'm from Barranquilla, but I moved here last year.
        What about you?
Luis:   I'm from Cali originally, but I've lived in Medellín for 5 years now.
        So, what brings you to TechCorp?
Sofía:  I got a position as a junior frontend developer. I just finished 
        a bootcamp and this is my first tech job!
Luis:   That's great! What stack do you work with?
Sofía:  React and TypeScript mostly. I'm also learning Node.js on the side.
Luis:   Perfect — that's exactly what we use here.
        How did you hear about this company?
Sofía:  A friend referred me. She works in the design team.
Luis:   Oh, is that Camila?
Sofía:  Yes! Do you know her?
Luis:   Of course! We're on the same project. Small world!
```

**Nota gramatical:** `Where are you from?` — to be, sin do. `What brings you here?` — present simple con valor casi idiomático. `How did you hear...?` — Past Simple para pregunta sobre pasado específico.

### Caso 3 — Speed networking (evento)

> **Situación:** En un evento de RIWI se hace un ejercicio de "speed networking" — 3 minutos por persona.

```
[Round 1: Valentina meets Carlos]
Valentina: Hi! I'm Valentina. What's your name?
Carlos:    Carlos Moreno. Nice to meet you, Valentina.
Valentina: What do you do?
Carlos:    I'm a full-stack developer at a fintech startup.
           I've been there for about a year.
           What about you?
Valentina: I'm still studying — I graduate from RIWI next month.
           I specialize in frontend development.
Carlos:    Interesting! What technologies do you use?
Valentina: React and TypeScript mainly. Are you hiring?
Carlos:    Ha! Not yet, but our team is growing. 
           How long have you been coding?
Valentina: About two years. I started learning on my own, 
           then joined RIWI. How did you get your job?
Carlos:    Through a friend, actually. Networking works!
Valentina: That's exactly why I'm here!
[Timer rings]
Both:      Nice to meet you! Let's connect on LinkedIn.
```

### Caso 4 — Mensaje de LinkedIn (primera conexión)

> **Situación:** Ana envía un mensaje a un profesional que conoció en un evento.

```
Hi Marcos,

It was great meeting you at DevFest yesterday!

As I mentioned, I'm currently finishing my training at RIWI in Cartagena.
I specialize in React and TypeScript, and I'm looking for my first junior 
developer role.

I was really interested in what you said about your team's approach 
to component design. I'd love to learn more about your work and the 
tech stack your company uses.

Would you be open to a quick 15-minute chat sometime this week?
I'd really appreciate any advice you can share.

Looking forward to hearing from you!

Best,
Ana Rodríguez
```

**Nota gramatical:** `It was great meeting you` — gerundio después de adjetivo. `I'd love to learn` — condicional suave para peticiones. `Would you be open to...?` — petición formal educada.

## 6. ⏱️ Wh- Questions en distintos tiempos verbales

| Tiempo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| **Present Simple** | Where do you work? |
| **Past Simple** | Where did you grow up? |
| **Present Continuous** | What are you working on? |
| **Present Perfect** | How long have you lived here? |
| **Future (going to)** | What are you going to do after graduation? |
| **Future (will)** | Where will you work? |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Usar `do` con `to be`

❌ Where do you be from?
✅ Where **are** you from?

### Preguntas de sujeto con `do`

❌ Who does teach your class?
✅ Who **teaches** your class?

### Respuestas largas innecesarias

- Pregunta: `Are you from Bogotá?`
- ❌ `Yes, I am from Bogotá.` (redundante)
- ✅ `Yes, I am.` o `Yes, I'm from Bogotá.` (natural)

### `Work for` vs `work in` vs `work at`

- `work for` → la empresa (empleador): *I work for Google.*
- `work in` → el sector/industria: *I work in technology.*
- `work at` → el lugar específico: *I work at the downtown office.*
