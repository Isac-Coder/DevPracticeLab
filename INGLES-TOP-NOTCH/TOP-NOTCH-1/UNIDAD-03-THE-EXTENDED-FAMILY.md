---

## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Pronombres posesivos + Adverbios de frecuencia

### Adjetivos posesivos vs. Pronombres posesivos

Los **adjetivos** posesivos van antes de un sustantivo. Los **pronombres** posesivos reemplazan al sustantivo completamente — lo evitan.

| Adjetivo posesivo | Pronombre posesivo | Ejemplo de contraste |
|---|---|---|
| my | **mine** | That's my sister. / That sister is **mine**. |
| your | **yours** | Is this your jacket? / Is this jacket **yours**? |
| his | **his** | It's his car. / That car is **his**. |
| her | **hers** | That's her house. / That house is **hers**. |
| our | **ours** | It's our project. / That project is **ours**. |
| their | **theirs** | Those are their kids. / Those kids are **theirs**. |

> ⚠️ Nunca añadas apóstrofe a los pronombres posesivos.
> ❌ your's / her's ✅ yours / hers

---

### Adverbios de frecuencia — posición avanzada

| Adverbio | Posición con verbo normal | Posición con TO BE |
|---|---|---|
| always | She **always** calls on Sundays. | She is **always** on time. |
| usually | We **usually** eat together. | He is **usually** busy. |
| often | They **often** visit grandma. | She is **often** late. |
| sometimes | I **sometimes** work weekends. | He is **sometimes** absent. |
| rarely | He **rarely** misses dinner. | She is **rarely** sick. |
| never | We **never** skip family events. | They are **never** on time. |

**Posición más enfática al inicio:**
> Sometimes I wonder if my family understands me. (énfasis)

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **take after** someone | parecerse a alguien | She takes after her grandmother. |
| **get along with** someone | llevarse bien con | He doesn't always get along with his brother-in-law. |

| **be close to** someone | ser cercano a alguien | I'm very close to my aunt Rosa. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Describir la familia extensa (conversación en clase)

> **Situación:** Carlos describe su familia durante un ejercicio oral en RIWI.

```
"My immediate family is small — just my parents and one sister.
But my extended family is huge. My father has four brothers and two sisters,
so I have six aunts and uncles on his side alone. And their families!
I have fifteen cousins. Seriously — fifteen.

We don't always see each other. My uncle Pedro lives in Canada with his wife
and their three kids, so those cousins are theirs and we only see them 
at Christmas. The ones I'm closest to are my cousin Laura and her brother Tomás.
We grew up together.

My grandmother — my father's mother — is the heart of the family.
She's 78 and she still cooks for twenty people when we visit.
I look up to her a lot. She's always been the one who keeps everyone close."
```

**Nota gramatical:** `those cousins are theirs` — pronombre posesivo sin sustantivo. `The ones I'm closest to` — uso avanzado de superlativo con pronombre.

---

### Caso 2 — Árbol familiar en email

> **Situación:** Sofía le explica su árbol familiar a su nuevo novio antes de conocer a la familia.

```
Subject: My family — a survival guide 😄

Hi Marco,

So, Sunday is the big day! Let me give you a quick overview 
so you're not completely lost.

MY PARENTS:
- Mom: Gloria. She's warm and talkative. You'll love her.
- Dad: Roberto. He's quieter, but once he likes you, he's yours forever.

MY SIBLINGS:
- My brother Felipe — he's the loud one. His girlfriend is Carla.
  They've been together for three years. Theirs is a great relationship.
- My sister Diana — she's in university. She's usually quiet at family events 
  but she's actually very funny once you get to know her.

GRANDPARENTS (on mom's side — they'll definitely be there):
- Grandpa Julio: 80 years old. Hard of hearing but always smiling.
  He'll ask you about your job — it's always his first question.
- Grandma Rosa: 77 years old. She's the cook. Her food is legendary.
  Hers are the best arepas in the city, period.

RELATIVES TO WATCH OUT FOR:
- Uncle Hernán: He's my dad's brother. He's rarely quiet but always funny.
- Aunt Beatriz: She's Hernán's wife. She and my mom sometimes argue about politics.
  It's fine — it happens every time.

You'll be great. They'll love you. See you Sunday! ❤️
Sofía
```

**Nota gramatical:** `Hers are the best arepas` — pronombre posesivo como sujeto. `Theirs is a great relationship` — pronombre posesivo como sujeto.

### Caso 3 — Rutinas familiares (texto descriptivo)

> **Situación:** Valentina escribe un texto corto sobre las tradiciones de su familia.

```
Family Traditions

My family has some traditions that we always keep, no matter what.

Every Sunday, we usually have lunch together at my grandparents' house.
It's always a big deal — my grandmother cooks for everyone, and she 
always makes too much food. We never complain, though.

My aunt Cecilia often brings her guitar, and we sometimes sing together 
after lunch. It sounds cheesy, but it's honestly one of my favorite things.

Once a year, in December, the whole extended family gets together at 
my uncle's farm in Antioquia. There are usually about thirty of us — cousins, 
in-laws, everyone. The kids always run around outside while the adults talk.
It's chaotic and loud and I love it.

My family isn't perfect — no family is. But they're mine, and I wouldn't 
trade them for anything.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Pronombres posesivos en distintos tiempos

Los pronombres posesivos no cambian con el tiempo — el verbo es lo que cambia.

| Tiempo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Present Simple | That opinion is hers, not mine. |
| Past Simple | The house was theirs before the sale. |
| Future | The decision will be yours to make. |
| Present Perfect | This problem has always been ours to solve. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Apostrófe en pronombres posesivos

❌ That bag is her's.
✅ That bag is **hers**.

### Confundir `its` (posesivo) y `it's` (contracción)

❌ It's color is blue.
✅ **Its** color is blue.

### Posición del adverbio de frecuencia

❌ She visits always her grandma.
✅ She **always** visits her grandma.

## 8. 🧪 Ejercicios

1. Is this ___ phone? (your / yours)
2. That car is ___. (their / theirs)
3. I forgot ___ keys. (my / mine)
4. These notes are ___. (her / hers)
5. We gave them ___ report. (our / ours)

### B — Coloca el adverbio en el lugar correcto

1. We see our cousins. (rarely)
2. She calls her grandmother. (always)
3. They are late to family dinners. (never)
