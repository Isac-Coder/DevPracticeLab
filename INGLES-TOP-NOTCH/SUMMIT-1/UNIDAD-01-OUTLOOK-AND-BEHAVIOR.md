## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Cleft Sentences (Oraciones escindidas)

### ¿Por qué son importantes en B2?

Las cleft sentences enfatizan una parte específica de la oración. En debates sobre comportamiento y actitud, constantemente queremos subrayar exactamente qué, quién, cuándo o por qué importa algo. Son la herramienta de la énfasis precisa.

---

### It + be + X + that/who (It-cleft)

La estructura más común. Mueve el foco a la parte que queremos enfatizar:

| Oración normal | Cleft con énfasis |
|---|---|
| Resilience helps people cope. | **It is resilience that** helps people cope. |
| She changed my outlook. | **It was she who** changed my outlook. |
| Overthinking causes the problem. | **It is overthinking that** causes the problem. |
| The attitude matters, not the result. | **It is the attitude that** matters, not the result. |

---

### What-cleft (Pseudo-cleft)

Enfatiza lo que alguien hace, necesita o quiere:

```
What I admire most is her resilience.
What troubles me is the lack of accountability.
What people often underestimate is the power of small habits.
What changed everything was one conversation.
```

---

### All-cleft (énfasis minimalista)

```
All I need is one good reason.
All she did was ask for help.
All it takes is a willingness to try.
```

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Conversación filosófica sobre optimismo y pesimismo

> **Situación:** Sofía y Diego hablan sobre perspectivas ante el estado del mundo, después de ver las noticias.

```
Diego:    I don't know how people stay optimistic. 
           Every time I read the news, it feels like the world is getting worse.

Sofía:    What I think is interesting is that statistically, 
           on almost every measurable metric — infant mortality, 
           extreme poverty, access to education — the world has actually 
           improved dramatically over the last century.

Diego:    Then why does it feel so bleak?

Sofía:    It's the negativity bias. Our brains are wired to notice 
           threats more than improvements. And the news amplifies that.
           What the media reports is exceptional — by definition.
           Nobody reports that 8,000 people got out of poverty today.

Diego:    I understand that intellectually. But it's hard 
           to maintain a positive outlook when you're seeing 
           conflict, inequality, and ecological collapse in real time.

Sofía:    I don't think the goal is to be blindly optimistic.
           It was a pragmatist — I think it was William James — 
           who said that optimism is not about believing everything is fine.
           It's about believing your actions can make a difference.

Diego:    That's a meaningful distinction.

Sofía:    What worries me about pessimism isn't the feeling —
           it's that it tends to lead to passivity. 
           If you believe nothing will change, you stop trying to change it.
           And then it becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Diego:    So it's not optimism vs. pessimism. 
           It's agency vs. fatalism.

Sofía:    Exactly. What matters isn't your assessment of the world —
           it's whether you still think you can act in it.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `What I think is interesting`, `What the media reports`, `What worries me about pessimism`, `What matters isn't` — what-cleft sistemático. `It was a pragmatist who said` — it-cleft para énfasis.

### Caso 2 — Perfil de personalidad en un contexto profesional

> **Situación:** Carlos escribe una reflexión para su portafolio profesional.

```
On Resilience and Professional Growth

What I've learned most from my time at RIWI is not a technical skill.
It is the capacity to sit with difficulty and keep working anyway.

Early in the program, I was terrified of failing. 
It was this fear that almost stopped me from taking risks.
I would avoid submitting work that felt incomplete, 
waiting for a perfection I never reached.

What changed was a conversation with an instructor who said:
"All a pull request needs to be is better than nothing. 
Shipping something is more honest than shipping nothing perfectly."

That reframe was small. But it was transformative.

What I've developed is not fearlessness — I still feel anxiety 
before every deadline. What I've developed is the ability 
to act despite it. That distinction matters.

Tenacity isn't the absence of doubt. It's doubt plus forward motion.
It is this quality — and not raw talent — that I believe 
separates developers who grow from those who plateau.

I am not the most gifted person in my cohort.
What I am is relentlessly curious and genuinely hard to discourage.
I think those two qualities compound over time in ways 
that are more valuable than early technical brilliance.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `What I've learned most`, `It is this fear that`, `What changed was`, `What I've developed is`, `It is this quality that` — alternancia natural entre it-cleft, what-cleft y all-cleft. `All a PR needs to be` — all-cleft.

### Caso 3 — Artículo de divulgación psicológica

> **Situación:** Valentina escribe para el blog de bienestar de RIWI sobre el pensamiento catastrófico.

```
Catastrophizing: Why Our Brains Prepare for the Worst

Catastrophizing is the cognitive pattern of imagining 
the worst possible outcome in any situation.
If you've ever lain awake replaying a conversation and 
concluded it's going to cost you your job, your relationships, 
and your sense of self-worth — you've catastrophized.

It is not a character flaw. It is an evolutionary mechanism.
Our ancestors who anticipated threats survived longer 
than those who didn't. What we're left with is a brain 
that's excellent at generating worst-case scenarios —
whether or not those scenarios are realistic.

The problem arises when catastrophizing becomes the default.
When all you do is amplify every possible negative outcome, 
you create chronic anxiety without solving any actual problems.

What cognitive behavioral therapy addresses is the pattern, 
not the content. It doesn't tell you that bad things won't happen.
It helps you evaluate: How likely is this, really? 
What is the evidence? What would I tell a friend in this situation?

What tends to work for many people is:
1. Naming the catastrophic thought explicitly.
2. Rating its likelihood on a scale of 1–10.
3. Identifying what you actually can control.
4. Taking one concrete action within that control zone.

It is not optimism that interrupts the spiral. 
It is a small, specific, possible action.
What breaks the thought loop is movement — any movement.

If you recognize yourself here: you are not broken.
You are running ancient software on a modern world.
The work is not to delete the software. 
All it takes is learning to update it.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `It is not a character flaw. It is an evolutionary mechanism.` — it-cleft simple. `What we're left with`, `What cognitive behavioral therapy addresses`, `What tends to work`, `What breaks the thought loop` — what-cleft en cada punto clave del argumento. `All it takes is` — all-cleft para cierre.

### Caso 4 — Debate sobre actitud ante el fracaso

> **Situación:** Panel de egresados de RIWI discute cómo manejaron el fracaso en su carrera.

```
Moderator: What's your relationship with failure? 
            How do you handle setbacks professionally?

Panelist 1 (Ana):
What I struggled with most early on was accepting that 
failure is information, not verdict.
It was my first rejected PR that taught me more than 
any tutorial I'd watched. But at the time, it felt like evidence 
that I didn't belong.
What helped was reading other developers' postmortems — 
seeing that everyone ships broken things sometimes.

Panelist 2 (Carlos):
All I wanted when I started was to not embarrass myself.
That's actually a terrible motivation — it's completely reactive.
What changed my approach was reframing from "don't fail" 
to "what can I learn from this iteration?"
It is that shift — from defensive to curious — that unlocked growth for me.

Panelist 3 (Diego):
I'm naturally risk-averse. What that means in practice is 
that I overthink before acting and then catastrophize after.
What helped me most was having a team I trusted enough 
to fail in front of. Psychological safety is not soft —
it is what enables technical risk-taking.
Without it, you optimize for not being wrong 
rather than for being better.

Moderator: What advice would you give someone 
            who's terrified of their first real job?

All three: 
It is not about being ready. All you need is to be willing.
What matters is that you show up and stay curious.
```

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### It-cleft con verbo en lugar de sustantivo

❌ It is believe that helps.
✅ It is **belief** that helps. (sustantivo, no verbo)
> El elemento destacado en it-cleft es siempre un sustantivo, pronombre o adjetivo — nunca directamente un verbo.

### What-cleft — el verbo al final debe concordar con el complemento

❌ What I need are more time.
✅ What I need is more time. (singular)
✅ What I need are more resources. (plural)

### All-cleft con verbo conjugado (no infinitivo)

❌ All I need is to have more confidence.
✅ All I need is more confidence. (sin `to have` si el foco es el sustantivo)
✅ All I did was cry. (sin `to`)
