## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Frases participiales reducidas (Reduced Relative Clauses)

### ¿Qué son?

Son cláusulas de relativo acortadas: eliminas el pronombre relativo y el auxiliar, dejando solo el participio. El resultado es más conciso y más sofisticado.

---

### Cómo reducir

**Participio activo (-ing) → acción en curso o característica activa:**

| Cláusula completa | Reducida |
|---|---|
| Animals **that live** in captivity | Animals **living** in captivity |
| Species **that are facing** extinction | Species **facing** extinction |
| Organizations **that operate** sanctuaries | Organizations **operating** sanctuaries |
| Researchers **who study** animal behavior | Researchers **studying** animal behavior |

**Participio pasivo (-ed/-en) → acción recibida:**

| Cláusula completa | Reducida |
|---|---|
| Animals **that were taken** from the wild | Animals **taken** from the wild |
| Species **that are protected** by law | Species **protected** by law |
| Programs **that are funded** by NGOs | Programs **funded** by NGOs |
| Practices **that are banned** internationally | Practices **banned** internationally |

---

### Cuándo NO reducir

Solo puedes reducir cuando el pronombre relativo es el **sujeto** de la cláusula relativa:

- ✅ Animals **that face** extinction → Animals **facing** extinction (that = sujeto)
- ❌ Animals **that we study** → ~~Animals studying~~ (that = objeto — no se puede reducir)

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Artículo sobre el comercio de mascotas exóticas

> **Situación:** Ana escribe un artículo argumentativo sobre el comercio de fauna silvestre.

```
The Exotic Pet Trade: Choice, Welfare, and the Animals Caught in Between

The exotic pet trade, valued at billions of dollars annually, 
represents one of the most complex intersections of personal freedom, 
animal welfare, and conservation in the modern world.

Animals removed from their natural habitats face mortality rates 
estimated at 80% before reaching the buyer.
For every exotic bird sold in a pet store, 
eight others are estimated to have died in transit.

Those defending the right to own exotic animals argue 
that responsible private ownership supports conservation awareness —
that a family keeping a parrot may develop interest in protecting wild parrots.

Those opposing this position point to something more fundamental:
the welfare of an animal raised in captivity outside its natural context,
deprived of its social structures and environmental stimulation, 
cannot be adequately met by any individual owner.
This applies regardless of their intentions or resources.

The evidence here is consistent: parrots exhibiting self-destructive behavior, 
big cats pacing obsessively, primates developing severe psychological disorders.
These are not exceptional cases — they are documented patterns affecting 
animals kept as pets worldwide.

The conservation argument is also contested.
Animals taken from the wild do not benefit wild populations.
Captive breeding programs operated by certified zoos pursuing real 
conservation missions are fundamentally different from private ownership 
of animals obtained through supply chains tied to illegal poaching.

In Colombia, possessing native wild species is legally prohibited —
yet the illegal trade persists, enabled by online marketplaces 
and inconsistent enforcement.

Respecting animals, it could be argued, requires accepting 
that some relationships with them are not ours to have.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `Animals removed from their natural habitats`, `animals raised in captivity`, `deprived of its social structures`, `parrots exhibiting self-destructive behavior`, `big cats pacing obsessively`, `primates developing severe disorders`, `animals kept as pets`, `programs operated by certified zoos`, `animals obtained through supply chains`, `animals taken from the wild` — frases participiales reducidas a lo largo del texto.

### Caso 2 — Debate sobre la caza de trofeos

> **Situación:** El equipo de RIWI debate la caza de trofeos en su clase de inglés avanzado.

```
Instructor: Can trophy hunting ever be justified as a conservation tool?

Carlos:     I'll argue the uncomfortable position: in certain specific cases, 
             regulated trophy hunting has demonstrably funded conservation.
             In Zimbabwe and Namibia, communities managing wildlife 
             for high-fee hunting have generated more conservation 
             funding than equivalent protected areas.
             The alternative — land converted to agriculture — 
             is categorically worse for wildlife.

Valentina:  The argument assumes the funding model works.
             Evidence reviewed by conservation researchers suggests 
             it often doesn't. The percentage of hunting fees reaching 
             local communities is typically very low.
             The wildlife benefiting from those fees is rarely the species 
             hunted — and the animals killed are often individuals 
             playing critical roles in breeding populations.

Ana:        There's also a moral dimension that the economic argument sidesteps.
             Species threatened by habitat loss face existential pressure 
             precisely because we've treated wildlife as a resource to exploit.
             Using exploitation to fund conservation of the same species 
             seems conceptually contradictory at minimum.

Carlos:     I accept that the moral case is difficult.
             But I'm focused on outcomes. Communities living alongside wildlife 
             need economic incentives to protect it rather than convert the land.
             If trophy hunting provides that incentive and photographic tourism 
             doesn't cover the same area — the animal living in that habitat 
             is better off under a hunting model than under none.

Diego:      What that argument concedes is that the problem 
             is fundamentally political and economic — not biological.
             Animals facing extinction because humans converted their habitat 
             need policy solutions, not market solutions dressed up as conservation.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `communities managing wildlife`, `species threatened by habitat loss`, `individuals playing critical roles`, `communities living alongside wildlife`, `animals facing extinction`, `habitat converted to agriculture` — frases participiales en argumentación sofisticada.

### Caso 3 — Reporte sobre conservación del jaguar en Colombia

> **Situación:** Sofía escribe un informe para clase sobre programas de conservación.

```
CONSERVATION BRIEF: The Jaguar in Colombia

STATUS:
The jaguar (Panthera onca), the largest wild cat in the Americas, 
is classified as Near Threatened on the IUCN Red List.
In Colombia, populations persisting in the Amazon, Orinoco, and 
Pacific regions represent some of the most significant remaining 
habitat connected to the broader Amazonian range.

THREATS:
The primary pressures facing jaguar populations in Colombia are:
1. Habitat loss: forests cleared for cattle ranching and coca cultivation 
   reduce and fragment the connected habitat required for viable populations.
2. Conflict with farmers: jaguars preying on livestock are frequently killed 
   in retaliation by landowners protecting their herds.
3. Illegal trade: cubs captured for the exotic pet trade, 
   and body parts traded internationally for use in traditional medicine.

CONSERVATION PROGRAMS:
Wildlife corridors connecting protected areas have been established 
in several regions to allow movement between fragmented habitats.
Community programs offering compensation for livestock losses 
have reduced retaliatory killings in pilot areas.
Camera trap networks monitored by local communities provide 
population data while building conservation engagement.

WHAT EVIDENCE SHOWS:
Populations studied in areas with functional wildlife corridors 
show significantly better long-term viability than isolated populations.
The most successful programs combining economic incentives with 
community involvement demonstrate that human-wildlife coexistence 
is achievable when the costs of conservation are not borne exclusively 
by those living closest to the wildlife.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Frases participiales en distintos tiempos y formas

| Tipo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Participio activo presente | Species facing extinction need immediate protection. |
| Participio pasivo presente | Animals protected by law recover more quickly. |
| Participio activo pasado (narración) | Having lost its habitat, the jaguar moved toward farmland. |
| Participio en frase adverbial | Living in captivity, exotic pets often develop psychological problems. |

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Reducir cuando el pronombre es objeto (no sujeto)

❌ The species that we study living in the Amazon. (that = objeto — no se puede reducir)
✅ The species **that we study** living in the Amazon. (dejar completa)
✅ The species **living** in the Amazon that we study. (solo se reduce la parte cuyo pronombre es sujeto)

### Confundir participio activo y pasivo

- `Animals **hunting** prey` → animales que cazan (activo — ellos hacen la acción)
- `Animals **hunted** for sport` → animales cazados (pasivo — reciben la acción)
