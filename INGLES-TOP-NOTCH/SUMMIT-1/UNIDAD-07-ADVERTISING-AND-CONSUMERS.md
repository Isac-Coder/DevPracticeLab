## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Verbos de reporte para análisis persuasivo

### ¿Por qué son esenciales aquí?

Cuando analizas publicidad, no adoptas sus afirmaciones — las reportas y las cuestionas. Los verbos de reporte te permiten distanciarte de una afirmación mientras la describes.

---

### Verbos de reporte por función retórica

| Función publicitaria | Verbo para analizarla | Ejemplo de análisis |
|---|---|---|
| Afirmar algo sin evidencia | **claims**, **asserts** | The ad **claims** the product "transforms" skin in 7 days. |
| Sugerir implícitamente | **implies**, **insinuates** | The imagery **implies** that buying the product leads to social success. |
| Prometer resultados | **promises**, **guarantees** | The copy **promises** results "or your money back." |
| Exagerar | **overstates**, **exaggerates** | The testimonial **overstates** the typical user experience. |
| Manipular emocionalmente | **exploits**, **manipulates** | The campaign **exploits** parental anxiety. |
| Aparentar algo | **purports to**, **appears to** | The packaging **appears to** suggest environmental certification. |
| Dar a entender | **suggests**, **indicates** | The before/after photos **suggest** dramatic results. |
| Omitir información | **omits**, **conceals** | The fine print **conceals** the conditions for the money-back guarantee. |

---

### Estructuras de análisis crítico

```
The ad claims that X, but [evidence/counterargument].
By [technique], the advertisement implies [message].
What the campaign exploits is [psychological mechanism].
The assumption underlying this message is that [unstated premise].
What is notable is not what the ad says, but what it omits.
```

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Análisis de una campaña de influencer marketing

> **Situación:** Carlos escribe un análisis crítico de técnicas persuasivas para su clase de inglés B2.

```
Deconstructing the Influencer Post: A Rhetorical Analysis

Consider a typical influencer post promoting a supplement brand:

"Honestly, I've tried everything and NOTHING worked until I found [Brand X].
I was skeptical at first but after just three weeks I can genuinely feel 
the difference. Use my code for 20% off — this is the real deal."

Let us analyze the persuasive architecture.

The post implies personal expertise by claiming the narrator 
"tried everything" — establishing relatability before the endorsement.

The phrase "I was skeptical at first" performs a particular function:
it preemptively addresses the viewer's skepticism, 
framing doubt as a temporary stage rather than a sustained rational response.

The verb "feel" — "I can genuinely feel the difference" — 
makes an unfalsifiable claim. The adverb "genuinely" functions as a 
sincerity marker, signaling authenticity while providing no verifiable evidence.

What the post exploits is the trust architecture of friendship.
Research on consumer behavior suggests that people assign 
significantly more weight to recommendations appearing to come from individuals 
than to institutional advertising.
Influencer marketing borrows that trust and monetizes it.

The format conceals the commercial nature of the transaction:
the discount code makes the relationship explicitly financial,
but wraps it in the language of personal generosity ("my code").

The question worth asking is not whether this is legal.
In most jurisdictions, with proper disclosure, it is.
The question is whether consumers who understand these mechanisms 
make systematically different decisions than those who don't.
Evidence consistently suggests: yes, they do.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `implies personal expertise`, `frames doubt as a stage`, `makes an unfalsifiable claim`, `functions as a sincerity marker`, `exploits the trust architecture`, `suggests that people assign`, `conceals the commercial nature`, `wraps it in the language of` — verbos de reporte y análisis retórico.

### Caso 3 — Artículo sobre greenwashing

> **Situación:** Valentina escribe sobre el greenwashing para el blog de RIWI.

```
When "Sustainable" Is Just a Word: Understanding Greenwashing

Greenwashing describes the practice of making environmental claims 
that mislead consumers into believing a product or company is more 
sustainable than it actually is.

The practice exploits growing consumer concern about climate change.
Research indicates that a majority of consumers in most high-income markets 
claim willingness to pay a premium for sustainable products.
The market for "eco-friendly" products has consequently grown rapidly —
as has the use of environmental language by companies with no measurable 
sustainability improvements to report.

The techniques vary:
- **Vague language**: terms like "natural," "eco-friendly," or "green" 
  carry no legal definition and imply certification they don't have.
- **Irrelevant claims**: a product claiming to be "CFC-free" 
  when CFCs have been banned for decades.
- **Hidden trade-offs**: a product marketed as "recycled packaging" 
  where the packaging represents 2% of the product's environmental impact.
- **Unverified certifications**: invented logos suggesting third-party review.

Regulators in the EU and several other jurisdictions 
have moved to restrict these practices.
The EU's Green Claims Directive, adopted in 2024, 
requires substantiation for any environmental marketing claims.

What the evidence suggests is that without enforcement, 
market pressure alone is insufficient to eliminate greenwashing —
because the asymmetry of information between company and consumer 
is precisely what the practice exploits.

The consumer tools that work best are not product scrutiny 
but institutional: verified certification schemes, regulatory enforcement, 
and legal liability for misleading claims.
Individual vigilance is necessary but not sufficient.
```

### Caso 4 — Análisis de un dark pattern (diseño engañoso)

> **Situación:** Diego escribe un artículo técnico sobre dark patterns en UX para el blog de RIWI.

```
Dark Patterns: When UX Design Is Used Against the User

A dark pattern is a user interface element designed to manipulate users 
into actions they would not take if they fully understood what they were doing.
The term was coined by UX designer Harry Brignull in 2010.

Common examples:
- **The hidden unsubscribe**: confirming a subscription is one click.
  Canceling it requires navigating five menus, 
  calling a phone number during business hours, 
  and confirming via email — for each of which you receive 
  a "are you sure?" prompt asserting you'll miss out on discounts.

- **The pre-checked box**: completing a purchase implies
  you've opted into a newsletter, insurance add-on, or recurring charge — 
  unless you noticed the pre-checked box and unchecked it.

- **The fake urgency counter**: "3 left in stock!" 
  and "7 people viewing this now!" — sometimes auto-refreshing —
  suggest scarcity that manipulates purchase timing.

- **The roach motel**: it's easy to get in, hard to get out.
  Signing up for a free trial requires a credit card.
  Canceling charges the card if you forget to cancel first.

These are not design failures. They are design choices.
Each implies knowledge of what the user wants and a deliberate 
decision to route around it.

As developers building UX, this is where ethics becomes practical:
the same skills that enable good design enable exploitative design.
The difference is intent — and the professional cultures 
we build around intent.

You will be asked, at some point in your career, 
to build something that exploits the people using it.
What the field needs are more developers who recognize that request 
for what it is.
```

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Claim` vs `say`

- `say` → neutral, cualquier declaración
- `claim` → implica escepticismo sobre la veracidad
> Use `claim` en análisis crítico cuando quieres señalar que algo no está verificado.

### `Imply` vs `infer`

- `The ad **implies** success.` → el ad da a entender (quien dice es el ad)
- `The consumer **infers** success.` → el consumidor deduce (quien deduce es el consumidor)
❌ The ad infers that success follows purchase.
✅ The ad **implies** that success follows purchase.
