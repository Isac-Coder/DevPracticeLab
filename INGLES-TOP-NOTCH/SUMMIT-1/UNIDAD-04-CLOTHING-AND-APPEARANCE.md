## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Passive reporting structures

Las estructuras de reporte pasivo atribuyen afirmaciones a una fuente general sin nombrarla. Son esenciales cuando hablas de normas sociales, estándares de belleza y presión cultural — donde la fuente del juicio es "la sociedad" en abstracto.

```
It is said that...          → Se dice que...
It is believed that...      → Se cree que...
It is widely accepted that... → Es ampliamente aceptado que...
It has been argued that...  → Se ha argumentado que...
It is reported that...      → Se reporta que...
It is thought that...       → Se cree que...

Subject + is said to + verb → She is said to have had surgery.
Subject + is believed to + verb → He is believed to be worth millions.
Subject + is reported to + verb → The procedure is reported to carry risks.
```

---

### Por qué son útiles aquí

Cuando analizas estándares de belleza, rara vez hay un único culpable identificable. El agente es "la industria", "la sociedad", "las redes sociales". La pasiva de reporte permite describir esto sin caer en generalizaciones absolutas:

- *It is widely believed that thinness equals health.* (no dice "everyone believes")
- *Beauty standards are said to have become increasingly unrealistic.*
- *The beauty industry is reported to generate over $500 billion annually.*

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Artículo de opinión sobre presión estética

> **Situación:** Valentina escribe un artículo para la clase de inglés sobre los estándares de belleza y las redes sociales.

```
The Filtered Self: How Social Media Rewrote Beauty Standards

It is widely believed that beauty standards have always existed.
This is true. What is new is the scale and speed at which they circulate.

It has been argued that social media has compressed the beauty trend cycle 
from decades to months. A look that is celebrated in January 
is said to be "over" by March. 
The pressure to keep up is not merely aesthetic — it is economic.

The beauty industry is reported to generate over $500 billion annually.
It is believed that a significant portion of this revenue 
depends on convincing consumers that their current appearance 
is inadequate.

Filters and digital editing are said to have created a feedback loop:
individuals edit their photos, see an edited version of themselves 
consistently, and gradually disassociate from their unedited appearance.
Research is said to show that this contributes to rising rates of 
body dysmorphia and cosmetic procedure anxiety among young people.

It has been argued that the solution is "representation" —
more diverse bodies, ages, and features in media.
This is thought to be necessary but not sufficient.
Representation within a framework that still prioritizes appearance 
above other values may shift which bodies are acceptable,
without shifting the premise that appearance defines worth.

What is genuinely new — and perhaps genuinely hopeful — 
is the growing number of voices that reject the premise entirely:
not "my body is acceptable," but "my body's acceptability is irrelevant."
That shift, if it is reported accurately by those who study it,
represents something more structural than inclusion alone.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `It is widely believed`, `It has been argued`, `is said to have created`, `is thought to be`, `is reported to generate`, `is said to show`, `is reported accurately` — uso extenso y variado de pasiva de reporte.

### Caso 2 — Debate en clase sobre cirugía cosmética

> **Situación:** El instructor plantea la pregunta: ¿La cirugía cosmética es una elección personal o un producto de la presión social?

```
Instructor: Is cosmetic surgery a personal choice, 
             or is the concept of "choice" complicated here?

Ana:        I think the question of choice is genuinely complicated.
             It's widely accepted that we make choices within contexts.
             The woman who gets rhinoplasty because she was bullied 
             throughout school is making a choice — 
             but the pressure that shaped that choice was external.
             It has been argued that true autonomy requires 
             freedom from coercion, and I think social pressure 
             around appearance is a form of soft coercion.

Carlos:     I see the point, but I'm cautious about going too far in 
             that direction. Because then we're saying people 
             can't really choose anything — everything is contextual.
             That seems to eliminate individual agency entirely.
             It is thought by some ethicists that respecting autonomy 
             means accepting choices you wouldn't make yourself.

Valentina:  The part I find hardest is the age question.
             Procedures performed on minors are reported to be increasing.
             At what point does a 16-year-old have the maturity 
             to make a permanent decision about their body?
             It is widely believed in pediatric psychology that 
             the adolescent brain isn't fully equipped to assess 
             long-term consequences.

Diego:      And there's the access dimension.
             Risky, non-regulated procedures are said to be significantly 
             more common among lower-income groups who can't afford 
             safe, certified surgeons.
             The choice framework tends to ignore that.
```

### Caso 3 — Política de código de vestimenta en el trabajo

> **Situación:** Ana redacta una propuesta para modernizar el código de vestimenta de una empresa.

```
Subject: Proposal — Updating the Employee Dress Code Policy

To: HR Management
From: Ana Rodríguez, Employee Experience Committee

CONTEXT:
It is widely believed in current HR practice that rigid dress codes 
reduce workplace inclusion and can negatively affect retention, 
particularly among younger employees.

Evidence suggests that strict appearance standards disproportionately 
affect employees from cultural backgrounds where professional dress 
differs from the assumed default (typically Western business attire).

CURRENT POLICY ISSUES:
The current policy is reported to have generated confusion 
around natural hairstyles, traditional cultural dress, 
and visible tattoos or piercings.

It has been argued within our team that the existing guidelines 
were written for a different era and don't reflect our company's 
stated values around diversity and inclusion.

PROPOSED UPDATE:
We propose moving from prescriptive rules to principle-based guidelines:
1. Clean and well-maintained at all times.
2. Appropriate for client-facing interactions.
3. Safe for the physical work environment where applicable.

Under this framework, employees are trusted to exercise judgment.
Specific exemptions for religious or cultural dress are explicitly protected.

EXPECTED OUTCOME:
It is believed that this change would improve morale, reduce HR complaints 
related to dress code enforcement, and signal genuine commitment 
to the inclusive workplace environment we claim to value.
```

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Passive reporting + infinitivo concordancia

❌ She is said to had plastic surgery.
✅ She is said to **have had** plastic surgery. (infinitivo perfecto para pasado)
✅ She is said to **be considering** surgery. (infinitivo continuo para presente)

### `It is said that` vs `is said to`

- `It is said that she had surgery.` → cláusula `that`
- `She is said to have had surgery.` → sujeto + `be said to` + infinitivo
Ambas son equivalentes — la segunda es más elegante en estilo formal.
