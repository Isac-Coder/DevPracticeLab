## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: USED TO y WOULD para hábitos pasados

### ¿Cuándo se usan?

Estas estructuras describen hábitos o estados del **pasado que ya no ocurren**. Son esenciales cuando hablas de cómo comías antes vs. ahora, o de tradiciones alimenticias de la infancia.

---

### USED TO

```
Subject + used to + verb (base)
```

| Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Hábito pasado que ya no ocurre | I **used to** eat fast food every day. |
| Estado pasado que ya cambió | She **used to be** a vegetarian. |
| Pregunta | **Did** you **use to** cook at home? |
| Negativa | I **didn't use to** eat breakfast. |

> ⚠️ Ortografía: `used to` (con -d) en afirmativas. `use to` (sin -d) en negativas y preguntas porque ya hay `did`.
> - ❌ I didn't **used** to eat it.
> - ✅ I didn't **use** to eat it.

---

### WOULD para hábitos pasados (no estados)

```
Subject + would + verb (base)
```

`Would` también describe **hábitos pasados repetidos**, pero NO estados o condiciones. Solo acciones.

| | Used to | Would |
|---|---|---|
| Hábito pasado (acción) | ✅ I used to cook on Sundays. | ✅ I would cook on Sundays. |
| Estado pasado | ✅ I used to be a chef. | ❌ I would be a chef. |
| Primera mención del hábito | ✅ Introduce el contexto | ❌ Necesita contexto previo |

**En práctica:** `would` para hábitos se usa más en narrativa escrita y menos en conversación informal.

---

### Diferencia con Past Simple

| | Significado | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Past Simple | solo ocurrió una vez o en ocasiones específicas | I ate sushi once. |
| Used to | hábito pasado regular que ya no existe | I used to eat sushi every week. |

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **cut out** something | eliminar de la dieta | I cut out sugar six months ago. |
| **cut back on** something | reducir el consumo | I've cut back on red meat. |
| **go on** a diet | ponerse a dieta | She went on a diet before the wedding. |
| **stick to** a diet | mantener una dieta | It's hard to stick to a vegan diet when traveling. |
| **have** a sweet tooth | ser goloso | I have a terrible sweet tooth. |
| **eat in moderation** | comer con moderación | Everything is fine in moderation. |
| **live on** something | vivir de algo | Students tend to live on instant noodles. |
| **be worth it** | valer la pena | The calories are totally worth it. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Conversación sobre cambios alimenticios

> **Situación:** Carlos y Ana hablan sobre sus hábitos de alimentación durante el almuerzo en RIWI.

```
Carlos:   You're eating so differently these days. 
           What happened?
Ana:      Ha! I used to eat terribly. I used to skip breakfast, 
           have chips for lunch, and order pizza three times a week.
Carlos:   That sounds familiar.
Ana:      I know! But I started feeling awful — no energy, always tired.
           So six months ago I cut out processed food almost completely.
Carlos:   That must have been hard.
Ana:      It was! I used to crave junk food constantly at first.
           But your body adjusts. Now I actually enjoy cooking.
           I used to hate it. I thought it was a waste of time.
Carlos:   I still think it's a waste of time, honestly.
Ana:      You've just never eaten a meal you cooked yourself.
           When I was a kid, my grandmother would cook these huge Sunday lunches.
           She would spend the whole morning in the kitchen.
           We would all sit together and eat for two hours.
           I used to love those Sundays. That's probably why I 
           finally got into cooking — it felt like that.
Carlos:   That's actually really nice. My family used to do the same.
           My mom would make sancocho every Sunday without fail.
           We don't do that anymore. Life got busier.
Ana:      Exactly. That's what I'm trying to recreate — 
           even if it's just for myself.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I used to eat`, `I used to skip`, `I used to crave`, `I used to hate` — hábitos pasados con `used to`. `She would cook`, `She would spend`, `We would all sit`, `My mom would make` — hábitos pasados narrados con `would` en contexto descriptivo.

### Caso 2 — Comunicando alergias en un restaurante (email de reserva)

> **Situación:** Sofía hace una reserva para su cumpleaños y comunica las restricciones alimenticias del grupo.

```
Subject: Dinner Reservation — Birthday Group, October 25

Dear La Terraza Team,

I'm writing to confirm a reservation for 8 people on Saturday, October 25th 
at 8:00 PM under the name Sofía Navarro.

I'd like to inform you of some dietary restrictions in our group:

- 2 guests are vegetarian (no meat or fish, but they eat dairy and eggs).
- 1 guest is vegan (no animal products of any kind).
- 1 guest has a severe nut allergy. 
  Please ensure no nuts or nut-based products are used in her dishes. 
  This is a serious allergy — cross-contamination must be avoided.
- 1 guest is lactose intolerant (can eat most dishes but needs 
  dairy-free alternatives where possible).
- The rest of us have no restrictions.

Could you please let us know if your menu can accommodate these needs?
If the nut allergy cannot be safely managed, please let us know 
so we can make alternative arrangements.

We are celebrating a birthday, so a small dessert surprise 
would be wonderful if possible — one of the guests is vegan, 
so a dairy-free option would be ideal.

Thank you very much for your help.

Best regards,
Sofía Navarro
```

### Caso 3 — Post de blog: cambio de dieta personal

> **Situación:** Diego escribe sobre su experiencia al volverse vegetariano.

```
Why I Stopped Eating Meat (And What I Learned)

Six months ago, I became vegetarian. It's the change that surprised me most.

I used to be a huge meat-eater. Growing up in Barranquilla, 
we used to eat meat at almost every meal. My family would make 
huge barbecues on weekends — my dad would grill for hours, 
and we would all gather around the table. 
I used to think vegetarians were missing out.

Then I watched a documentary. I won't name it, but it changed things.

The first two weeks were brutal. I used to reach for chicken automatically.
I would stand in front of the fridge not knowing what to eat.
My body used to crave things I wasn't giving it.

But around week three, something shifted. I started cooking more creatively.
I used to think vegetables were boring. Now I think meat was the boring option —
I just never explored the alternatives.

Here's what I've noticed:
- My energy levels are better in the mornings.
- I've learned to cook dishes I never would have tried before.
- I save money at the supermarket.
- I've become much more conscious about what I put in my body.

Is it hard? Sometimes. Especially when traveling.
I used to eat whatever was in front of me — now I have to think ahead.

But I don't miss meat the way I thought I would.
And that's the thing that surprises me most.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I used to be`, `we used to eat`, `I used to think`, `I used to reach`, `I used to crave`, `I used to think` — hábitos/estados pasados con `used to`. `My dad would grill`, `we would all gather`, `I would stand`, `My body used to crave` — `would` para acciones repetidas y `used to` para estados.

### Caso 4 — Artículo: comida tradicional colombiana en el mundo

> **Situación:** Un artículo de cultura explica la bandeja paisa a lectores internacionales.

```
Bandeja Paisa: Colombia's Most Iconic Dish Explained

If you visit Colombia and don't eat a bandeja paisa, 
did you really visit Colombia?

The bandeja paisa is a traditional dish from the Antioquia region 
and it is widely considered the country's most iconic meal.
The name literally means "Paisa tray" — paisa referring to people 
from the Andean region of Colombia.

What's in it?
A traditional bandeja paisa includes:
- Red beans cooked with pork (frijoles)
- White rice
- Ground beef (carne molida)
- Chicharrón (fried pork belly)
- A fried egg
- Sliced avocado
- Plantain (patacón or tajada)
- Arepa
- Chorizo

It's not a light meal. Historically, it used to be eaten by farmers 
who would work the fields all day and needed the calories.
People used to start the morning with a bandeja — not a cereal bowl.
It was fuel, not a trend.

Today, the dish has spread far beyond Antioquia.
You'll find versions of it across Colombia and in Colombian restaurants 
around the world. Chefs have started offering lighter interpretations —
but purists argue that if you change it, it's not really a bandeja paisa.

If you want to try one, go to Medellín. Sit at a local restaurant.
Order the full version. You won't regret it — 
or you'll need a very long nap. Probably both.
```

## 6. ⏱️ USED TO y WOULD en contextos distintos

| Tiempo | Estructura | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Pasado habitual (acción) | used to / would + verb | I used to eat out. / I would eat out every Friday. |
| Pasado habitual (estado) | used to only | I used to be vegetarian. |
| Negativa | didn't use to | I didn't use to like vegetables. |
| Pregunta | Did + S + use to? | Did you use to cook at home? |
| Pasado simple (una vez) | Past Simple only | I tried sushi once. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Used to` con el verbo `use` — confusión

❌ I use to eat breakfast every day.
✅ I **used to** eat breakfast every day.
> En presente no existe `use to`. Para hablar de costumbres presentes usa: *I usually eat breakfast.*

### `Would` para estados

❌ I would be very thin when I was a child.
✅ I **used to be** very thin when I was a child.

### Pronunciación: `used to` /juːst tuː/ — la -d no suena como en "used" el adjetivo
