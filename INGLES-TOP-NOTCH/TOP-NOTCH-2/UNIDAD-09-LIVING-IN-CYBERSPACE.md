## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Conectores de discurso (Discourse Markers)

### ¿Por qué son importantes en este tema?

El ciberespacio es un tema que se debate — tiene ventajas y desventajas, causas y efectos. Los conectores te permiten construir argumentos coherentes y matizados, en vez de una lista de oraciones desconectadas.

---

### Contraste

| Conector | Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **however** | contradicción formal | Social media connects people. **However**, it also increases anxiety. |
| **although / even though** | contraste dentro de oración | **Although** the internet is useful, it has serious risks. |
| **despite / in spite of** | + noun/gerund | **Despite** the risks, most people share personal data freely. |
| **on the other hand** | el otro lado del argumento | It can be addictive. **On the other hand**, it can be educational. |
| **while / whereas** | contraste simultáneo | **While** some benefit from social media, others are harmed by it. |
| **nevertheless / nonetheless** | a pesar de eso (formal) | The risks are real. **Nevertheless**, most people ignore them. |
| **yet** | contraste informal | It's convenient, **yet** it's destroying our attention spans. |

---

### Causa y consecuencia

| Conector | Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **because / since / as** | causa | We overshare **because** we crave connection. |
| **therefore / thus / hence** | consecuencia formal | Data is collected constantly. **Therefore**, privacy is an illusion. |
| **as a result / consequently** | consecuencia | We spend 7 hours online daily. **As a result**, sleep quality suffers. |
| **due to / owing to** | + noun | **Due to** poor passwords, accounts get hacked frequently. |
| **so that / in order to** | propósito | Companies collect data **in order to** target ads. |
| **lead to / result in** | consecuencia como verbo | Excessive screen time can **lead to** sleep disorders. |

---

### Adición

| Conector | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| **furthermore / moreover** | Social media spreads misinformation. **Moreover**, it amplifies it. |
| **in addition to** | **In addition to** connecting people, it monetizes their attention. |
| **not only... but also** | It is **not only** addictive **but also** psychologically manipulative. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Debate en clase sobre redes sociales

> **Situación:** El instructor de RIWI plantea el debate: "¿Las redes sociales hacen más daño que bien?"

```
Instructor: Okay — who thinks social media does more harm than good?
             Let's hear the argument.

Carlos:     I'll take that side. Social media platforms are designed 
             to be addictive. They exploit psychological vulnerabilities 
             — specifically our need for validation — in order to 
             maximize engagement. As a result, millions of people 
             spend hours scrolling through content that makes them 
             feel worse about themselves.
             
             Furthermore, the algorithm doesn't prioritize truth — 
             it prioritizes engagement. Therefore, outrage and 
             misinformation spread faster than facts.

Instructor: Strong case. Who has the counter?

Ana:        Although social media has real problems, I think 
             dismissing it entirely misses its value. 
             Despite the risks, it has democratized information.
             People who used to have no platform now have one.
             Social movements — including ones in Colombia — 
             have been organized through social media.
             
             However, I do agree with Carlos that the business model 
             is broken. The problem isn't social media itself — 
             it's the incentive structure. While platforms could 
             prioritize well-being, they prioritize profit instead.

Diego:      I think you're both right, which makes this complicated.
             Nevertheless, I lean toward Ana's position. 
             The tool isn't the problem — the design is.
             Due to the current advertising model, platforms are 
             incentivized to keep us online as long as possible.
             If that model changed, the harm would decrease significantly.

Valentina:  On the other hand, even if the model changed, 
             we'd still have the attention problem. 
             Not only do we spend too much time online, 
             but our capacity for deep focus is also shrinking 
             as a result.
```

**Nota gramatical:** Carlos: `in order to`, `As a result`, `Furthermore`, `Therefore`. Ana: `Although`, `Despite`, `However`, `While`. Diego: `Nevertheless`, `Due to`. Valentina: `On the other hand`, `Not only...but also`, `as a result`. Conectores distintos, bien distribuidos.

### Caso 2 — Guía de seguridad digital para empleados (documento interno)

> **Situación:** El equipo de TI de una empresa publica una guía de seguridad.

```
DIGITAL SECURITY GUIDE FOR EMPLOYEES

WHY THIS MATTERS
Data breaches are increasingly common. Due to weak passwords and 
careless behavior online, companies lose millions of dollars annually.
Therefore, every employee has a role in protecting company data.

PASSWORDS
— Use a unique password for every account. 
  Although it seems inconvenient, password managers make this easy.
— Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on all work accounts.
  Even if your password is stolen, 2FA prevents unauthorized access.

PHISHING
Phishing emails are designed to look legitimate. However, there are 
warning signs: urgency, unusual sender addresses, and suspicious links.
— Never click a link in an email you didn't expect.
  Despite looking real, most phishing attempts are detectable.
— If you're unsure, contact IT directly. Don't forward the email.

DATA
— Don't share login credentials with colleagues, even with good intentions.
  As a result of shared credentials, accountability becomes impossible.
— Work data must not be stored on personal devices.
  Owing to the risk of device theft, this policy is strictly enforced.

SOCIAL MEDIA
Be mindful of your digital footprint. While sharing your professional life 
is normal, avoid sharing sensitive company information publicly.
Not only can this violate your contract, but it also creates security risks.

Questions? Contact security@company.com.
IT Security Team
```

### Caso 3 — Post de blog: desintoxicación digital

> **Situación:** Sofía escribe sobre su experiencia con un "digital detox" de una semana.

```
I Deleted Social Media for a Week — Here's What Happened

I spend, on average, 4.5 hours on my phone per day.
I know this because my screen time app tells me so.
Most of that is social media — Instagram, TikTok, Twitter.
Although I knew it was too much, I kept doing it anyway.
So last week, I deleted the apps. Not deactivated — deleted.

Here's what I noticed:

Day 1-2: Restlessness
I kept picking up my phone for no reason. Due to the habit, 
my hand moved automatically toward the app that no longer existed.
The empty space where the icon used to be felt strangely confrontational.

Day 3-4: Boredom, then clarity
On the other hand, something shifted. I started reading again. 
I finished a book in two days — something I hadn't done in years.
My concentration improved noticeably. As a result, I was more productive 
at work. I wrote more code in three days than I had in the previous week.

Day 5-7: A calmer baseline
I still checked email and WhatsApp. However, without the infinite scroll, 
the anxiety level dropped. I stopped comparing myself to people online 
because those people simply weren't there.

What I learned:
The problem isn't the internet. It's the infinite, curated, 
algorithmically optimized version of the internet that lives in your pocket.
Despite the benefits of staying connected, there is a real cost 
to being permanently available to everyone's opinions.

Am I back on social media? Yes. But I set time limits now.
Not only did the detox help me reset, but it also made me realize 
I never really chose to be that online — I was designed into it.
```

> **Situación:** Diego le avisa a IT que recibió un intento de phishing.

```
Subject: Possible Phishing Attempt — Please Review

Hi IT Team,

I'm writing to report a suspicious email I received this morning at 9:17 AM.

The email appeared to be from our HR department, however, 
the sender's address was "hr.team@techcorp-security.net" — 
not our official domain. The message said my account would be suspended 
unless I clicked a link and "verified my identity within 24 hours."

Although the email looked professional, several things raised red flags:
— The urgency and threat of account suspension.
— The link, which redirected to a different domain when I hovered over it.
— The unusual phrasing in some sentences.

As a result of these warning signs, I did not click the link.
I have taken a screenshot of the email and can forward it to you.

I'm concerned that other employees may have received the same email.
Therefore, I'd suggest sending a company-wide alert in order to 
raise awareness and prevent anyone from falling for this scam.

Please let me know if you need anything else from me.

Best,
Diego Vargas
Frontend Team
```

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `However` en mitad de oración

❌ The internet is useful, however it can be dangerous.
✅ The internet is useful. **However**, it can be dangerous.
✅ The internet is useful; **however**, it can be dangerous.
> `However` necesita punto o punto y coma antes, y coma después.

### `Despite` + gerundio o sustantivo (no cláusula)

❌ Despite it was dangerous, she clicked the link.
✅ **Despite the danger**, she clicked the link.
✅ **Although it was dangerous**, she clicked the link.

### `Although` y `even though` en la misma oración que `but`

❌ Although it's useful, but it has risks.
✅ **Although** it's useful, it has risks. (sin `but`)
✅ It's useful, **but** it has risks. (sin `although`)
