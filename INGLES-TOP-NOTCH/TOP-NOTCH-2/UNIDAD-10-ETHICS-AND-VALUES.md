## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Tercer Condicional (If + Past Perfect, would have + PP)

### ¿Por qué es el tiempo de la ética?

El tercer condicional habla de situaciones **irreales en el pasado** — algo que no ocurrió y cuya consecuencia tampoco ocurrió. Es perfecto para dilemas morales porque constantemente reflexionamos sobre lo que habríamos hecho diferente, o qué habría pasado si alguien hubiera actuado de otra forma.

---

### Estructura

```
If + Past Perfect (had + PP), would have + Past Participle
```

| Parte | Estructura | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| If clause | if + had + PP | **If I had known** the truth, |
| Main clause | would have + PP | I **would have acted** differently. |

---

### Contracciones

- `I would have` → `I'd have` / `I would've`
- `I would not have` → `I wouldn't have`
- `If I had` → `If I'd`

---

### Segundo vs. Tercer Condicional

| Tipo | Tiempo | Realidad | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2nd** | Presente/Futuro | Hipotético | If I **found** money, I **would** return it. |
| **3rd** | Pasado | Irreal (ya no cambia) | If I **had found** money, I **would have** returned it. |

---

### Mixed Conditionals (mezcla)

Mezclan tiempo del 2nd y 3rd — muy comunes en discusiones éticas:

- *If I **had taken** that job (pasado irreal), I **would be** rich now.* (consecuencia en presente)
- *If I **were** a more honest person (presente hipotético), I **would have told** the truth then.* (consecuencia en pasado)

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Debate sobre un dilema ético en el trabajo

> **Situación:** El equipo de RIWI analiza un dilema: un desarrollador descubre que su empresa está vendiendo datos de usuarios sin consentimiento claro. ¿Qué debería hacer?

```
Instructor: You're a developer. You've just discovered your company 
             is selling user data in ways the privacy policy doesn't clearly cover.
             What do you do?

Carlos:     I think I would report it internally first — go to my manager 
             or the legal team. If I had discovered this without any clear 
             policy violation, I would have raised it quietly first.
             
Ana:        But what if management already knows and is doing it intentionally?
             If that had been the case, going to management 
             would have been pointless — or even dangerous.

Diego:      That's the real dilemma. If I had been in that position 
             and my internal complaint had been ignored, 
             I think I would have had to decide: keep my job or my integrity.
             
Valentina:  I don't think it's that binary. You could document everything,
             consult a lawyer, and blow the whistle to a regulator.
             If I had found that evidence, I would have made copies 
             before doing anything else.

Carlos:     That's smart. But you also have to consider the consequences.
             If you hadn't thought about retaliation risk, 
             you might have acted impulsively and lost your only leverage.

Ana:        What would you have done if it had been a close friend's company?
             That's where it gets really complicated.

Diego:      Honestly? I think I would have talked to the friend first.
             If they hadn't been willing to fix it, I would have had 
             to treat it the same as a stranger's company. 
             Friendship can't be a reason to let people be harmed.

Instructor: That last point — "friendship can't be a reason to let people 
             be harmed" — is a strong ethical principle. 
             What value is that rooted in?

Diego:      Justice, I think. Or maybe: integrity over loyalty when 
             they conflict.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `If I had discovered... I would have raised`, `If that had been the case... would have been`, `If I had been... and had been ignored... I would have had to`, `If I had found... I would have made`, `If you hadn't thought... you might have acted`, `If they hadn't been willing... I would have had to` — tercer condicional en argumentación ética, mezclado con análisis presente.

### Caso 2 — Reflexión personal sobre una decisión pasada (texto)

> **Situación:** Sofía escribe sobre una decisión ética difícil que tomó en el pasado.

```
The Day I Chose Comfort Over Honesty

Two years ago, I watched a classmate cheat on an important exam.
I didn't say anything. I told myself it wasn't my business.
I told myself it wasn't serious enough to report.
The truth is: I was afraid of the social consequences.

If I had reported it, I would have been seen as the person 
who got someone in trouble. In our group, that would have 
damaged my relationships.

If I had been braver, I would have done the right thing — 
but I took the easy way out.

The student passed the course. They eventually graduated.
And I've carried that small guilt ever since.

What I know now: if I had truly believed in fairness,
I would have acted on it regardless of the cost to my reputation.
The fact that I didn't tells me something uncomfortable — 
that I valued my social comfort more than my principles that day.

If I could go back, I would have spoken to the teacher privately.
I wouldn't have made it public or dramatic.
But I would have said something.

I think about this whenever I face situations where integrity 
and comfort are in conflict. It's made me more willing to 
cross that uncomfortable line — slowly, imperfectly, 
but more than before.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `If I had reported it, I would have been seen`, `If I had been braver, I would have done`, `if I had truly believed... I would have acted`, `If I could go back, I would have spoken` — tercer condicional distribuido en una narrativa de reflexión real.

### Caso 3 — Artículo de opinión: ética en la tecnología

> **Situación:** Diego escribe un artículo de opinión para el blog de RIWI sobre ética en el desarrollo de software.

```
The Code We Write Has Consequences

Most developers think about bugs. Fewer think about ethics.
But the code we write shapes how people experience reality, 
make decisions, and understand the world.

Consider this: if the engineers who built early social media algorithms 
had prioritized well-being over engagement, we would have a very different 
internet today. If they had asked "what does this do to people?" 
before shipping, millions of people might not have been manipulated 
into radicalization pipelines.

They didn't ask. Or if they did, they didn't act on the answer.
If they had acted on it, they would have had to push back against 
business pressure — and that takes a kind of courage that 
technical training rarely develops.

As developers, we have more power than we're taught to believe.
The systems we build encode values — even when we think we're 
just solving technical problems. An algorithm that ranks content 
by engagement is not neutral. A feature that makes it hard to 
delete your account is not neutral. A dark pattern that tricks 
users into accepting terms is not neutral.

If we had been taught to think ethically from day one,
maybe we would design differently. That's why I think ethics 
shouldn't be a philosophy elective — it should be a core part 
of every technical education.

The question isn't just: does this code work?
The question is: should this code exist?
```

### Caso 4 — Dilema clásico adaptado al contexto laboral

> **Situación:** Un módulo de soft skills en RIWI presenta dilemas para discutir en equipo.

```
ETHICAL DILEMMA CARDS — RIWI Soft Skills Module

DILEMMA 1: The bug you didn't report
You discover a bug in production that affects a small percentage of users.
Fixing it would delay the sprint by two days. Your manager is under 
pressure to ship. Do you report it?

Discussion questions:
- If you had reported the bug and the sprint was delayed, 
  what consequences would you have faced?
- If you hadn't reported it and a user was harmed, 
  how would you have felt?
- What value would you have been prioritizing in each case?

DILEMMA 2: The shortcut with borrowed code
You're behind on a deadline. You find code online that solves your problem —
but it's under a license that doesn't allow commercial use.
Nobody on your team would know. Do you use it?

Discussion questions:
- If you had used it and been caught, what would have happened?
- If you hadn't used it, how would you have met the deadline?
- What does intellectual honesty mean in a technical context?

DILEMMA 3: The colleague who's struggling
A teammate is clearly behind and producing poor work.
You suspect they're dealing with something personal.
Your manager hasn't noticed yet. Do you say something?

Discussion questions:
- If you had spoken to the colleague first and they had asked for privacy,
  would you have gone to the manager anyway?
- If you had stayed silent and it had become a team problem,
  what would that say about your accountability?
```

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Would have` en la cláusula `if`

❌ If I would have known, I would have helped.
✅ If I **had** known, I would have helped.

### `Had + PP` mal formado

❌ If I have known earlier...
✅ If I **had known** earlier... (Past Perfect)

### Confundir 2nd y 3rd condicional

- *If I **had** the money, I **would** donate.* → 2nd (no tengo el dinero ahora)
- *If I **had had** the money, I **would have** donated.* → 3rd (no tuve el dinero entonces)

## 8. 🧪 Ejercicios

### A — Tercer Condicional

1. If she ___ (tell) the truth earlier, things ___ (not get) so complicated.
2. If he ___ (not take) the bribe, he ___ (not lose) his job.
3. If they ___ (consult) a lawyer, they ___ (know) their options.
4. What ___ you ___ (do) if you ___ (find) the wallet?
5. If I ___ (be) in your position, I ___ (speak) up immediately.

### B — 2nd o 3rd Condicional

1. If she ___ (be) more careful now, fewer mistakes would happen.
2. If he ___ (listen) to the warning, the project would have succeeded.
3. If I ___ (have) more experience, I would apply for that role.

### C — Dilema personal

Piensa en una decisión pasada que tomarías diferente hoy. Escribe un párrafo reflexivo usando el tercer condicional al menos 4 veces.
