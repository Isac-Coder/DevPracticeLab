## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Segundo Condicional (If + Past Simple, would + verb)

### ¿Por qué se usa aquí?

El segundo condicional habla de situaciones **hipotéticas o irreales en el presente/futuro**. En el contexto de conducción es perfecto para hablar de qué harías en situaciones hipotéticas de tráfico, cómo cambiaría tu vida si tuvieras carro, o cómo responderías en un accidente imaginario.

---

### Estructura

```
If + Past Simple, would + verb (base)
```

| Parte | Tiempo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| If clause (hipótesis) | Past Simple | **If I had** a car, |
| Main clause (resultado imaginario) | would + verb | I **would commute** faster. |

> ⚠️ `If I were` — para el verbo `to be` en segundo condicional, la forma correcta es `were` para todos los sujetos (aunque en inglés informal se usa `was` para I/he/she/it).
>
> - *If I **were** you, I wouldn't drive in that storm.*
> - *If she **were** more careful, accidents wouldn't happen.*

---

### Primer vs. Segundo Condicional

| | Situación | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | Real/posible | If it rains, I **will take** the bus. |
| **2nd** | Hipotético/irreal | If it rained every day, I **would buy** a car. |

---

### Variaciones del segundo condicional

| Main clause | Significado | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| would + verb | resultado hipotético | I would drive more carefully. |
| could + verb | posibilidad hipotética | You could save money. |
| might + verb | posibilidad más débil | I might consider it. |
| would be able to | capacidad hipotética | You would be able to commute faster. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Alquilando un carro en el aeropuerto

> **Situación:** Sofía llega a Bogotá para una conferencia y quiere alquilar un carro.

```
Agent:   Good morning! Welcome to QuickRent. How can I help you?
Sofía:   Hi. I'd like to rent a car for three days, please.
Agent:   Of course. Do you have a reservation?
Sofía:   Yes, I booked online. The name is Sofía Navarro.
Agent:   I have it here. You booked an economy sedan.
          Would you like to upgrade to a larger model?
          An SUV is available for only 40,000 pesos more per day.
Sofía:   No thanks, the sedan is fine. I'm just going around the city.
Agent:   Perfect. Full insurance is included in your rate.
          Would you like to add a GPS?
Sofía:   I'll use my phone, but thanks. What's the fuel policy?
Agent:   The car is provided with a full tank.
          It must be returned full, or a refueling fee will be charged.
Sofía:   Got it. Any restrictions I should know about?
Agent:   The car may not be driven outside of Colombia.
          Smoking is not permitted in the vehicle.
          If the car is returned with any damage, 
          a damage report will need to be filed.
Sofía:   Understood. If I had any trouble — like a flat tire — 
          what would I do?
Agent:   You would call our 24-hour roadside assistance line.
          The number is on your contract.
Sofía:   Great. Can I see the car before I sign?
Agent:   Of course. Follow me to lot B.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `If I had any trouble... what would I do?` — segundo condicional hipotético. `You would call` — respuesta en segundo condicional. Pasiva en las políticas: `is provided`, `must be returned`, `will be charged`, `may not be driven`, `is not permitted`.

### Caso 2 — Reportando un accidente por teléfono

> **Situación:** Diego tuvo un accidente menor y llama al seguro.

```
Insurance rep: Good afternoon, SafeDrive Insurance. How can I help?
Diego:         Hi, I need to report an accident.
Rep:           I'm sorry to hear that. Is anyone injured?
Diego:         No, thankfully. It was a minor fender bender.
Rep:           Okay, good. Can you describe what happened?
Diego:         I was driving on Carrera 50 when the car in front of me 
               stopped suddenly. I braked, but I couldn't stop in time 
               and hit the rear of their car. 
               My front bumper is dented and their rear bumper too.
Rep:           Was your seatbelt on?
Diego:         Yes, always.
Rep:           Good. Were there any witnesses?
Diego:         Yes, a driver behind us stopped and offered to be a witness.
Rep:           Did you exchange information with the other driver?
Diego:         Yes. I have their name, plate number, and insurance company.
Rep:           Perfect. You'll need to file a claim online 
               or visit our nearest office.
               Photos of the damage should be taken and attached.
Diego:         I already took photos at the scene.
Rep:           Excellent. Your claim number is SC-2025-7741.
               If you have any more questions, don't hesitate to call.
Diego:         Thank you. One more thing — if I hadn't had insurance,
               what would have happened?
Rep:           Well, if you hadn't had insurance, you would have been 
               responsible for all damages. It would have been 
               very expensive.
Diego:         That's a good reminder. Thanks again.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `if I hadn't had insurance, what would have happened?` — tercer condicional (pasado hipotético). Mezcla natural entre narración en Past Simple y condicionales.

### Caso 3 — Debate sobre hábitos de conducción (conversación)

> **Situación:** Compañeros debaten sobre conducción en Colombia durante el almuerzo.

```
Carlos:   Do you think Colombian drivers are worse than in other countries?
Valentina: Honestly? I think driving here is more chaotic, but not necessarily
            more dangerous. People here just have different rules.
Ana:       If I drove everywhere, I'd be stressed all the time.
            That's why I prefer public transport.
Carlos:    I get that. But if the metro were extended to more neighborhoods,
            more people would use it. The problem is infrastructure.
Diego:     If I had to choose, I'd take a car over a bus any day.
            Even with the traffic. At least you control your own time.
Valentina: Would you really? If you could work remotely full time,
            would you still live in the city?
Diego:     Probably not. If I worked remotely, I'd move somewhere quieter —
            maybe near the coast.
Ana:       See, for me it's the opposite. If I lived far from the city,
            I'd need a car, and I can't drive. So the city wins.
Carlos:    Wait — you don't drive?
Ana:       Never learned. If I took lessons now, I'd probably be a 
            terrible student. I'm too impatient.
Carlos:    Ha! If I were you, I'd learn. It's useful for road trips at least.
Ana:       Maybe. If someone offered me free lessons, I'd consider it.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `If I drove... I'd be`, `if I had to choose... I'd take`, `If I worked remotely... I'd move`, `If I lived far... I'd need`, `If I were you, I'd learn`, `If someone offered... I'd consider` — segundo condicional en conversación natural y fluida.

### Caso 4 — Guía de conducción segura (artículo)

> **Situación:** Un blog de seguridad vial publica recomendaciones para conductores.

```
10 Habits That Make You a Better Driver

1. Always wear your seatbelt.
   If seatbelts were optional, accident fatalities would double.
   It takes two seconds and could save your life.

2. Never use your phone while driving.
   If every driver put their phone away, road accidents would drop 
   by an estimated 25%. It's not worth the risk.

3. Follow the speed limit.
   If you drove just 20 km/h over the limit, your braking distance 
   would increase dramatically. Speed kills — not just you.

4. Keep a safe following distance.
   If the car in front braked suddenly and you were too close, 
   you wouldn't have time to react. A 3-second gap is the minimum.

5. Don't drive when tired.
   If you drove while genuinely exhausted, your reaction time 
   would be comparable to driving drunk. Pull over and rest.

6. Signal before every turn.
   If every driver used their indicators consistently, 
   rear-end collisions would be significantly reduced.

7. Maintain your vehicle.
   If you neglected your tires, you would increase the risk of blowouts.
   Check tire pressure monthly.

8. Yield to pedestrians.
   If drivers stopped for pedestrians every time, 
   walking in the city would be far less dangerous.

9. Never drive under the influence.
   If alcohol or drugs impair your judgment, no amount of experience 
   makes you safe on the road.

10. Plan your route before you drive.
    If you looked at your GPS while moving, you would take your eyes 
    off the road for an average of 5 seconds. Use voice navigation.
```

## 6. ⏱️ El segundo condicional en contexto

| Variación | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Consejo (If I were you) | If I were you, I'd take the highway — it's faster. |
| Situación hipotética | If the car broke down here, we'd be stranded. |
| Crítica diplomática | If you signaled more, there would be fewer accidents. |
| Imaginación | If I had a sports car, I'd drive it to the coast every weekend. |
| Deseo imposible | If gas were free, everyone would own three cars. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Would` en la cláusula `if`

❌ If I would have a car, I would drive to work.
✅ If I **had** a car, I would drive to work.

### `Was` vs `were` en segundo condicional

Formalmente: `If I **were** you` ✅ (más correcto)
Informalmente: `If I **was** you` (muy común en inglés hablado)
Ambas son aceptadas, pero `were` es la forma estándar en escritura formal.

### Confundir primero y segundo condicional

- *If it **rains**, I **will** take an umbrella.* → (posible → 1st)
- *If it **rained** here every day, I **would** buy a car.* → (hipotético → 2nd)

## 8. 🧪 Ejercicios

### A — Forma el segundo condicional

1. (have / a car / commute faster) If I ___, I ___
2. (live / near work / not need / transport) If she ___, she ___
3. (drive / more carefully / have fewer accidents) If people ___, they ___
4. (be / you / take / driving lessons) If I ___, I ___

### B — 1er o 2do condicional

1. If I save enough, I ___ (buy) a car next year.
2. If gas ___ (be) free, everyone would drive everywhere.
3. If she ___ (pass) her driving test, she'll celebrate tonight.
4. If he ___ (know) the way, he wouldn't need GPS.
