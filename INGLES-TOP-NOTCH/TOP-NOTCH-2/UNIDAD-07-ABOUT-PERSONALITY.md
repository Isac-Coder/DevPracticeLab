## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Cláusulas de relativo (Relative Clauses)

### ¿Por qué son importantes aquí?

Cuando describes la personalidad de alguien, las cláusulas de relativo te permiten añadir información sin repetir el sujeto. Son la herramienta de la descripción precisa.

---

### Defining vs. Non-defining relative clauses

#### Defining (cláusula de relativo especificativa)
Identifica exactamente de quién se habla. **Sin comas**. Sin esta cláusula, la oración pierde sentido.

```
A person who never admits their mistakes is hard to work with.
She's someone that always sees the bright side.
```

#### Non-defining (cláusula de relativo explicativa)
Añade información extra sobre alguien ya identificado. **Con comas**. Se puede quitar sin perder el sentido central.

```
My manager, who is very empathetic, always listens before deciding.
Carlos, whom I've known since school, is the most reliable person I know.
```

---

### Pronombres relativos

| Pronombre | Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | personas (sujeto) | A person **who** is empathetic... |
| **whom** | personas (objeto) — formal | The manager **whom** I respect most... |
| **that** | personas y cosas (defining only) | Someone **that** never gives up... |
| **which** | cosas (non-defining y defining) | A quality **which** I admire is honesty. |
| **whose** | posesión | A colleague **whose** work ethic inspires me... |
| **where** | lugares | A workplace **where** people feel safe to fail... |

---

### Omisión del pronombre relativo

Cuando el pronombre relativo es el **objeto** de la cláusula (no el sujeto), se puede omitir:

- *The person (that/who) I trust most is my sister.* → omisión posible
- *The quality that defines her is resilience.* → NO se puede omitir (es el sujeto)

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Carta de recomendación profesional

> **Situación:** El instructor de RIWI escribe una carta de recomendación para Valentina.

```
To Whom It May Concern,

I am writing to recommend Valentina Torres, who completed our 
Frontend Development Program at RIWI in October 2025 with outstanding results.

Valentina is someone who approaches every challenge with both creativity 
and rigor. During the program, she consistently delivered work whose 
quality exceeded expectations — not just technically, but in terms 
of structure, documentation, and attention to detail.

What distinguishes her from others I have taught is her ability 
to combine two qualities that are rarely found together: 
she is highly detail-oriented, which makes her code reliable and clean, 
but she is also a big-picture thinker who understands how each component 
fits into the broader system.

She is also someone whom her teammates consistently sought out for help.
This reflects not only her competence, but her empathetic communication style —
she explains things in a way that makes the person feel capable, 
not dependent.

The aspect of her character that I respect most is her resilience.
When she encountered difficult problems, which she inevitably did 
in a rigorous technical program, she treated them as data rather than failure.
That mindset is rare and valuable.

I recommend Valentina without reservation to any organization 
that values technical skill, intellectual honesty, and genuine teamwork.

Sincerely,
Prof. Andrés Martínez
RIWI Frontend Development Program
```

**Nota gramatical:** `who completed`, `who approaches`, `whose quality`, `who understand`, `whom her teammates sought`, `which she inevitably did` — cláusulas de relativo defining y non-defining mezcladas naturalmente.

### Caso 2 — Descripción de personalidad en una conversación (psicología casual)

> **Situación:** Ana y Diego hablan sobre tipos de personalidad después de una discusión en el equipo.

```
Ana:    Did you notice how Carlos reacted in that meeting?
         He completely shut down when someone challenged his idea.
Diego:  Yeah. He's someone who doesn't handle criticism well.
         It's not that he's arrogant — he's actually quite insecure, I think.
Ana:    Exactly. The people who react most strongly to criticism 
         are often the ones whose self-worth is tied too closely to their work.
Diego:  That's a really interesting way to put it.
         I think I used to be like that.
Ana:    Really? You seem pretty confident.
Diego:  I am now, but I used to be very afraid of being wrong.
         I was the kind of person who would stay quiet rather than 
         risk saying something incorrect.
Ana:    What changed?
Diego:  Working in teams, mostly. When you see that the people 
         whose ideas get challenged are also the ones who grow fastest,
         you start to see failure differently.
Ana:    That's what I appreciate most about the culture at RIWI —
         it's an environment where making mistakes is expected 
         and learning from them is the point.
Diego:  Right. And honestly, the people who are the most uncomfortable 
         with that at first tend to become the most resilient later.
Ana:    I'm definitely prone to overthinking. That's my thing.
Diego:  Ha. You're also the person who gets things done while 
         everyone else is still thinking. So it balances out.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `someone who doesn't handle`, `The people who react`, `the ones whose self-worth`, `the kind of person who would stay quiet`, `the people whose ideas`, `an environment where`, `the people who are` — cláusulas de relativo como herramienta central de la descripción de personalidad.

### Caso 3 — Perfil de LinkedIn (sección About)

> **Situación:** Carlos escribe su sección "About" para LinkedIn después de graduarse.

```
I'm a frontend developer who genuinely enjoys the craft — 
not just the output, but the process of building things that work well 
and are a pleasure to use.

I'm someone who thinks in systems. When I look at a UI component, 
I'm already thinking about how it connects to the rest of the application, 
what states it needs to handle, and how someone who comes after me 
will maintain it.

What I value most in a team is psychological safety — 
the kind of environment where people feel comfortable saying 
"I don't know" or "I was wrong." I believe the teams whose work 
I've admired most have always had this in common.

I'm detail-oriented but not perfectionistic — I know when something 
is good enough to ship and when it genuinely needs more work.
I'm decisive in situations where decisions are needed 
and patient in situations that require iteration.

I'm looking for a team that values clean code, thoughtful architecture, 
and people who take their work seriously without taking themselves 
too seriously.

If that sounds like your team, I'd love to connect.
```

### Caso 4 — Evaluación de desempeño (fragmento formal)

> **Situación:** El equipo de RIWI hace una autoevaluación de fin de módulo.

```
Self-Evaluation — Q4 2025
Student: Diego Vargas

STRENGTHS:
I am someone who works well independently. I have consistently completed 
tasks on time and delivered work that meets the defined criteria.
I am comfortable in situations where the requirements aren't fully defined — 
I tend to clarify what's needed rather than making assumptions.

I have strong communication skills, particularly written communication.
Colleagues who have worked with me on collaborative tasks have described me 
as clear, direct, and easy to give feedback to.

AREAS FOR GROWTH:
I am prone to perfectionism in situations where shipping quickly is the priority.
I am someone whose first instinct is to refine before releasing,
which sometimes conflicts with sprint timelines.

I also tend to be overly self-critical when I make mistakes —
a quality that motivates improvement but that can also slow me down.

GOALS FOR NEXT QUARTER:
To become someone who ships faster without sacrificing quality —
to internalize that a good PR reviewed now is better than a perfect one 
reviewed next week.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Cláusulas de relativo en distintos tiempos verbales

El tiempo verbal va dentro de la cláusula relativa, no en el pronombre:

| Tiempo en la cláusula | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Present Simple | A person **who is** empathetic... |
| Past Simple | A colleague **who worked** here before... |
| Present Perfect | The team **that has grown** most this year... |
| Future | A leader **who will make** decisions calmly... |
| Continuous | The developer **who is building** the auth module... |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Who` para objetos en non-defining clauses

❌ My manager, who I respect, is very direct.
✅ My manager, **whom** I respect, is very direct. *(formal)*
✅ My manager, **who** I respect, is very direct. *(informal — aceptado)* 

### Comas en defining clauses

❌ A person, who never gives up, is admirable.
✅ A person **who never gives up** is admirable. *(sin comas → defining)*

### `That` en non-defining clauses

❌ My colleague, that I've known for years, is leaving.
✅ My colleague, **who** I've known for years, is leaving.
> `That` no se usa en non-defining clauses.

## 9. ✅ Respuestas

### A
1. She is a developer who writes very clean code. / 2. My manager gave me feedback that/which I found very helpful. / 3. I admire people who can admit when they're wrong. / 4. The company values employees whose ideas challenge the status quo.
