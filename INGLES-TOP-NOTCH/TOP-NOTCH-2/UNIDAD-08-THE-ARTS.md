## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Reported Speech (Estilo Indirecto)

### ¿Por qué aquí?

En discusiones de arte, constantemente reportas lo que dicen los críticos, artistas o compañeros. `"The painting means freedom"` → `She said the painting meant freedom.` El estilo indirecto es la herramienta de la cita y el análisis.

---

### Cambios en el tiempo verbal (backshift)

Cuando el verbo de reporte está en pasado, el verbo reportado retrocede un tiempo:

| Directo | Indirecto |
|---|---|
| `"I love this painting."` | She said she **loved** that painting. |
| `"It is powerful."` | He said it **was** powerful. |
| `"I have never seen this before."` | She said she **had never seen** it before. |
| `"I will buy it."` | He said he **would** buy it. |
| `"I'm working on a new piece."` | She said she **was working** on a new piece. |

---

### Verbos de reporte más usados en arte y crítica

| Verbo | Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| said | neutro | She said the piece was outstanding. |
| told | + objeto indirecto | He told me the artist was self-taught. |
| mentioned | información adicional | She mentioned she had studied in Paris. |
| explained | dar detalles | He explained that the colors symbolized conflict. |
| claimed | afirmación fuerte | The critic claimed it was the best piece of the decade. |
| argued | argumento | She argued that abstract art requires more imagination. |
| suggested | sugerencia/interpretación | He suggested the work was a commentary on isolation. |
| noted | observación formal | The curator noted that the texture was unusual. |
| insisted | énfasis | She insisted the painting was not for sale. |
| admitted | algo que costaba admitir | He admitted he didn't understand the piece at first. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Visita a una exposición (conversación)

> **Situación:** Sofía y Carlos visitan una exposición de fotografía documental en Medellín.

```
Sofía:   What do you think of this one?
Carlos:  It's striking. The composition is really clean — 
          he's placed the subject right at the edge of the frame.
          Most photographers would put them in the center.
Sofía:   I read in the catalogue that the photographer said 
          he wanted the viewer to feel like the subject might 
          disappear out of the frame at any moment.
Carlos:  That's exactly what it does. He said the work 
          was about people who live on the margins of society — 
          and the framing reinforces that literally.
Sofía:   What about this one over here? I find it more difficult.
Carlos:  Me too. The guide mentioned that this piece 
          was taken in a community that had been displaced.
          She explained that the photographer had lived there 
          for six months before taking a single photo.
Sofía:   That changes how you look at it completely.
          The trust in those eyes makes more sense now.
Carlos:  That's what I love about documentary photography —
          it's not just about the image. It's about what happened 
          before and after the shutter clicked.
Sofía:   A critic I follow on Instagram argued that documentary 
          photography is the most political art form there is.
          She claimed that every choice — what you photograph, 
          what you leave out — is a political act.
Carlos:  I don't entirely disagree. Though I'd say the same 
          is true of all art, to some extent.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `the photographer said he wanted` — reported speech. `the guide mentioned that this piece was taken` — cambio de `is` a `was`. `She explained that the photographer had lived` — backshift a pasado perfecto. `A critic argued that documentary photography is` — sin backshift porque es una verdad general (posible en inglés moderno).

### Caso 2 — Análisis de una obra para clase (texto académico)

> **Situación:** Diego escribe un análisis breve de "Guernica" de Picasso para su clase de inglés.

```
Guernica — Pablo Picasso (1937)

"Guernica" is one of the most analyzed anti-war statements in the 
history of visual art. Picasso painted it in response to the bombing 
of the Basque town of Guernica by Nazi forces during the Spanish Civil War.

Picasso himself said that the painting was not allegorical — he argued 
that it was a direct, visceral response to what had happened. He insisted 
that the distorted figures were not symbols but screams given visual form.

Art historians have noted that the work's power lies in its refusal 
to aestheticize suffering. The absence of color — working in black, white, 
and gray — suggested to contemporary critics that the painting was 
stripping war down to its bones.

The central figure, a screaming horse, has been interpreted in various ways.
Some critics claimed it represented the Spanish Republic. 
Others suggested it symbolized the innocent victims of war.
Picasso himself never confirmed either interpretation — 
he admitted he wanted the viewer to bring their own meaning.

What strikes most viewers today is its continued relevance. 
When the painting went on display at the Reina Sofía Museum in Madrid,
curators noted that visitors often stood in silence for several minutes.
It is, as one critic put it, a painting that makes you feel 
what history books merely describe.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `Picasso said the painting was not`, `he argued that it was`, `he insisted that the distorted figures were`, `critics claimed it represented`, `he admitted he wanted` — variedad de verbos de reporte con backshift consistente.

### Caso 3 — Debate sobre arte en clase

> **Situación:** La clase de RIWI tiene un debate sobre si el arte digital es "arte real".

```
Instructor: Today's question: Is digital art as valid as traditional art?
             Ana, start us off.

Ana:        I think it is. The medium doesn't determine the value 
             — the idea and execution do. Rembrandt's critics 
             probably said oil painting was less valid than fresco.
             
Instructor: Good point. Carlos, counter?

Carlos:     I'm not sure I agree completely. A digital artwork 
             can be duplicated infinitely without losing quality.
             A painting, once made, is unique. That uniqueness 
             carries something — weight, history, risk — 
             that a digital file can't replicate.

Ana:        But photographs can be duplicated too, 
             and nobody argues photography isn't art anymore.

Diego:      There's also the question of effort. A digital artist 
             can undo anything. I read an interview where a painter 
             said she worked in a way that made every mistake permanent —
             that constraint was part of the creative process.
             
Ana:        But a digital artist can also spend 300 hours on a piece. 
             The tool doesn't determine the labor.

Valentina:  I think the most interesting question is: 
             what does art do? If a digital work moves you, 
             makes you think, changes how you see something —
             isn't that the point?

Instructor: That's a strong place to land. 
             What did everyone hear each other arguing?

Carlos:     Ana argued that medium doesn't determine value.
Diego:      Valentina suggested that emotional impact is the real measure.
Ana:        Carlos claimed that uniqueness has intrinsic artistic value.
```

**Nota gramatical:** El cierre del debate fuerza el uso de reported speech directo: `Ana argued that`, `Valentina suggested that`, `Carlos claimed that` — todos con los verbos de reporte y backshift o sin él según el tipo de afirmación.

## 6. ⏱️ Reported Speech en todos los tiempos de reporte

| Verbo de reporte | Tiempo del verbo reportado | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **says / tells me** (presente) | Sin cambio | She **says** she **loves** abstract art. |
| **said / told me** (pasado) | Backshift | She **said** she **loved** abstract art. |
| **has said** (present perfect) | Opcional | She **has said** she **loves** / **loved** the piece. |

> 💡 Cuando el verbo de reporte está en **presente**, no hay backshift. Solo cambia cuando está en **pasado**.

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Olvidar el backshift

❌ She said she loves abstract art. *(cuando el reporte es en pasado)*
✅ She said she **loved** abstract art.

### `Tell` sin objeto indirecto

❌ He told that the painting was excellent.
✅ He **said** that the painting was excellent. (o: He **told me** that...)
> `Tell` siempre lleva objeto indirecto (a quién).

### Reported questions (preguntas indirectas)

Las preguntas reportadas usan orden normal de oración (no inversión):

❌ She asked where **was** the exhibition.
✅ She asked where **the exhibition was**.
