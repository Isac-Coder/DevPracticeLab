## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: SHOULD / SHOULDN'T

### ¿Por qué se usa?

`Should` es un modal para dar **consejos, recomendaciones y sugerencias**. No es una obligación fuerte — es una opinión sobre lo que sería bueno hacer.

---

### Estructura

```
Subject + should / shouldn't + verb (base)
```

| Tipo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Consejo positivo | You **should** drink more water. |
| Consejo negativo | You **shouldn't** skip breakfast. |
| Pregunta (pedir consejo) | **Should** I see a doctor? |
| Respuesta | Yes, you should. / No, you shouldn't. |

> ⚠️ Después de `should` siempre va el verbo en su **forma base** (infinitivo sin `to`).

❌ You should to rest.
✅ You should rest.

---

### SHOULD vs MUST vs HAVE TO

| Modal | Fuerza | Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|---|
| should | débil | consejo, sugerencia | You should see a doctor. |
| must | fuerte | obligación o convicción personal | You must take this medicine. |
| have to | fuerte | obligación externa / regla | You have to call the hospital. |

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **have** a headache / a cold | tener dolor de cabeza / gripa | I have a bad headache. |
| **feel** sick / better / worse | sentirse enfermo / mejor / peor | I feel much better today. |
| **take** medicine / a pill | tomar medicina / una pastilla | Take this pill twice a day. |
| **see** a doctor | ir al médico | You should see a doctor. |
| **make** an appointment | hacer una cita | I need to make an appointment. |
| **look like** someone | parecerse a alguien | She looks like her mother. |
| **look** + adjective | verse de cierta manera | You look tired. Are you okay? |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — En el consultorio médico

> **Situación:** Andrés visita al médico con síntomas de gripa.

```
Doctor:  Good morning, Andrés. What seems to be the problem?
Andrés:  I don't feel well. I have a sore throat, a headache, 
         and a runny nose. I also have a slight fever.
Doctor:  How long have you had these symptoms?
Andrés:  Since Monday. That's three days.
Doctor:  Do you have a cough?
Andrés:  A little, yes. But it's not too bad.
Doctor:  Let me take a look. Open your mouth, please.
         ...Your throat is a bit red. It looks like a viral cold.
Andrés:  Should I take antibiotics?
Doctor:  No, you shouldn't. This is a virus, not bacteria. 
         Antibiotics won't help. You should rest as much as possible.
         You should drink plenty of fluids — water, juice, warm tea.
         You shouldn't go to work for two days.
Andrés:  Should I take anything for the fever?
Doctor:  Yes, you should take a painkiller like ibuprofen. 
         Take one tablet every 8 hours with food.
         If your fever goes above 39°C, you should come back immediately.
Andrés:  Okay, thank you, Doctor.
Doctor:  You're welcome. Feel better soon!
```

**Nota gramatical:** `Should I take...?` — pregunta de consejo. Alternancia de `should` y `shouldn't` para recomendaciones positivas y negativas. `How long have you had...?` — Present Perfect para duración (anticipa U14).

---

### Caso 2 — Describir a una persona desaparecida (reporte a la policía)

> **Situación:** Carmen reporta a su hijo perdido en un centro comercial.

```
Officer: Can you describe your son for me?
Carmen:  Yes. His name is Tomás. He's 8 years old.
Officer: What does he look like?
Carmen:  He's short for his age — about 1.15 meters. 
         He's slim. He has dark, curly hair and brown eyes.
         He has a small scar on his left cheek from an old accident.
Officer: What is he wearing?
Carmen:  He's wearing a blue T-shirt, gray shorts, and white sneakers.
         He's also carrying a small red backpack.
Officer: Does he wear glasses?
Carmen:  No, he doesn't. But he has a birthmark on his right arm.
Officer: Don't worry. We'll find him. Can you wait here?
Carmen:  Yes, of course. Please hurry.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `What does he look like?` — pregunta de descripción física. `He's wearing` / `He's carrying` — Present Continuous para lo que lleva puesto ahora. `He has dark, curly hair` — `have` para características físicas.

### Caso 3 — Mensaje de texto: quedarse en casa por enfermedad

> **Situación:** David le avisa a su instructor que no puede ir a clase.

```
David:    Good morning! I'm sorry but I can't come to class today.
          I woke up with a fever and a terrible headache. 
          I feel really sick.

Instructor: Oh no! I hope you feel better soon.
            You should stay in bed and rest.
            Don't worry about class — I'll share the notes with you.

David:    Thank you! Should I take the quiz online instead?

Instructor: Yes, you can take it tomorrow when you feel better.
            Drink lots of water and take something for the fever.

David:    I will. Thanks for understanding!

Instructor: Take care! Feel better! 🤒
```

### Caso 4 — Blog de salud: consejos generales

> **Situación:** Una nutricionista escribe un artículo de bienestar.

```
5 Things You Should Do Every Day for Better Health

1. You should drink at least 8 glasses of water daily.
   Dehydration causes headaches, fatigue, and poor concentration.

2. You should sleep 7 to 9 hours every night.
   Adults who sleep less have a higher risk of illness.
   You shouldn't look at your phone right before bed — the blue light 
   affects your sleep quality.

3. You should eat breakfast every morning.
   You shouldn't skip it — your brain needs fuel in the morning.

4. You should move your body for at least 30 minutes a day.
   You don't have to go to the gym — a walk is enough.

5. You should see a doctor for a general checkup once a year, 
   even if you feel healthy. Prevention is better than treatment.

Remember: these are habits, not quick fixes. Small, consistent changes 
make a big difference over time. Your body will thank you!
```

## 6. ⏱️ SHOULD y vocabulario de salud en distintos tiempos verbales

| Tiempo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| **Present (should)** | You should drink more water. |
| **Past (should have)** | You should have gone to the doctor sooner. |
| **Future (will)** | If you rest, you'll feel better tomorrow. |
| **Conditional** | If I were you, I would see a specialist. |
| **Present Perfect** | I've had this headache since this morning. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Should` + infinitivo con `to`

❌ You should to rest.
✅ You should rest.

### `Have a headache` vs `I'm headache`

❌ I'm headache.
✅ I **have** a headache.
> Los síntomas usan `have`, no `be`.

### `Feel` como verbo de estado

❌ I am feeling sick. (cuando es estado general)
✅ I **feel** sick.
> En estado general → Present Simple. `I'm feeling sick` solo cuando el estado está cambiando o es temporal.

### `Look like` vs `Look`

- `She **looks like** her sister.` → parecerse a alguien (+ noun)
- `She **looks** tired.` → verse de cierta manera (+ adjective)
