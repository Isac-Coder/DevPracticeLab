## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Have/Has + adjetivos descriptivos

### ¿Por qué se usa `have/has`?

En inglés, `have` (tener) se usa para expresar relaciones familiares, posesiones y características físicas. Es el verbo estándar para describir de quién es alguien o qué características tiene.

---

### Estructura: HAVE / HAS

| Sujeto | Verbo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | **have** | I have two brothers. |
| He / She / It | **has** | She has a big family. |

**Negativa:**

| Sujeto | Negativa | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | **don't have** | I don't have any sisters. |
| He / She / It | **doesn't have** | He doesn't have children. |

**Pregunta:**

```
Do you have...? → Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
Does she have...? → Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
```

---

### Adjetivos descriptivos (apariencia)

Los adjetivos van **antes** del sustantivo en inglés.

| Categoría | Adjetivos |
|---|---|
| Estatura | tall, short, medium height |
| Complexión | slim, thin, heavy, athletic |
| Edad | young, old, middle-aged, elderly |
| Cabello | long, short, curly, straight, dark, blond, gray, bald |
| Ojos | blue, brown, green, dark |
| Rasgos | handsome, beautiful, cute, serious |

**Orden de adjetivos (cuando hay más de uno):**
Opinion → Size → Age → Color → Noun
> She has beautiful long dark hair.

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Describir la familia en clase (presentación oral)

> **Situación:** Valentina presenta a su familia en su clase de inglés de RIWI.

```
"My name is Valentina. I have a small family. My father's name is Roberto.
He's a teacher. He's tall and has dark hair. My mother is Carmen.
She's an accountant. She has short, curly hair. She's very friendly.

I have one brother. His name is Sebastián. He's 20 years old.
He's a student like me. We look alike — we both have our mom's eyes.

I don't have any sisters. My grandparents live with us too.
My grandmother's name is Rosa. She's elderly but very active.
We have a big, happy family."
```

**Nota gramatical:** `He's tall and has dark hair` — `to be` para cualidades permanentes + `have` para características físicas. `We both have` — `both` con plural.

---

### Caso 2 — Formulario escolar

> **Situación:** Una escuela pide información familiar para el expediente del estudiante.

```
Student: Carlos Mendoza, 22 years old.
Father: Pedro Mendoza — occupation: engineer — phone: 311-555-0022
Mother: Gloria Ruiz de Mendoza — occupation: nurse — phone: 314-555-0089
Emergency contact: Gloria Ruiz (his mother)
Number of siblings: 2 (one brother, one sister)
Does the student live with both parents? Yes.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `his mother` — posesivo masculino. Formulario usa tercera persona para describir al estudiante.

---

### Caso 3 — Conversación casual sobre la familia

> **Situación:** Dos compañeros de trabajo hablan durante el almuerzo.

```
Ana:    Do you have any brothers or sisters?
David:  Yes, I have two sisters and one brother. You?
Ana:    I'm an only child. No siblings.
David:  Really? Is it lonely?
Ana:    Sometimes. But I have a big extended family — lots of cousins.
David:  How about your parents? Do they live near you?
Ana:    My mom does. She lives next door, actually!
        My dad doesn't. He lives in Bogotá with his new wife.
David:  Oh. Does she get along with your mom?
Ana:    Ha! That's a long story...
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I'm an only child` — expresión fija para hijo único. `He lives in Bogotá with his new wife` — tercera persona masculino consistente. `Does she get along with` — Does + tercera persona singular para preguntas.

---

### Caso 4 — Árbol genealógico descrito en texto

> **Situación:** Marco describe su árbol familiar para un proyecto escolar.

```
My name is Marco Rivera. I have a large family.

My grandparents on my father's side are Eduardo and Lucia Rivera.
They are both retired. My grandfather is 78. He's tall and bald.
My grandmother is 75. She has white hair and brown eyes.

My father is Alberto Rivera. He's 50 years old and has gray hair.
My mother is Patricia Díaz de Rivera. She's 47. She has long, straight hair.
They have three children: my brother Felipe, my sister Diana, and me.

Felipe is 25. He's married. His wife's name is Carla.
They have one daughter. Her name is Sofia — she's my niece!
Diana is 19. She's single and studies medicine.

On my mother's side, I have two aunts and one uncle.
My aunt Laura is a journalist. Her husband is a chef.
They don't have children yet.
```

**Nota gramatical:** Uso consistente de posesivos y tercera persona para toda la familia. `They don't have children yet` — `yet` en negativa = todavía no.

## 6. ⏱️ Have/Has en distintos tiempos verbales

| Tiempo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| **Present Simple** | I have two brothers. |
| **Past Simple** | When I was young, I had a dog. |
| **Future (will)** | Someday I will have children. |
| **Future (going to)** | They're going to have a baby in March. |
| **Present Perfect** | She has had the same job for ten years. |
| **Present Continuous** | We're having a family dinner tonight. |

> ⚠️ `having` en continuous = acción temporal/evento (tener una cena, tener una discusión). No se usa para relaciones permanentes.
> - ❌ I'm having two brothers.
> - ✅ I have two brothers.

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Confundir `have` y `be` para características

❌ She is long hair.
✅ She **has** long hair.
> El cabello es una posesión/característica → `have`.
> La altura es un estado → `She is tall.`

### Olvidar la `-s` en tercera persona

❌ She have a big family.
✅ She **has** a big family.

### `Parents` ≠ "parientes"

❌ My parents are my cousins and uncles. (falso cognado)
✅ My **relatives** are my cousins and uncles.
> `Parents` = papá y mamá solamente. `Relatives` = parientes en general.

### `Siblings` — sin distinción de género

> `siblings` cubre hermanos y hermanas. Es el término neutro e inclusivo.
