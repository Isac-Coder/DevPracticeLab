## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Pronombres personales y adjetivos posesivos

### ¿Por qué se usan?

Los pronombres reemplazan nombres para no repetirlos. Los adjetivos posesivos indican a quién pertenece algo. Sin ellos, cada oración se volvería torpe y repetitiva.

---

### Pronombres personales (sujeto)

| Pronombre | Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| I | primera persona singular | I am Carlos. |
| You | segunda persona (singular/plural) | You are my friend. |
| He | tercera persona masculino | He is a doctor. |
| She | tercera persona femenino | She is my sister. |
| It | objetos, animales, ideas | It is a great idea. |
| We | primera persona plural | We are colleagues. |
| They | tercera persona plural | They are my parents. |

---

### Adjetivos posesivos

| Pronombre | Posesivo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| I | **my** | My name is Ana. |
| You | **your** | What's your phone number? |
| He | **his** | His email is john@mail.com. |
| She | **her** | Her address is on the form. |
| It | **its** | Its name is unclear. |
| We | **our** | Our teacher is Mr. Park. |
| They | **their** | Their contact info is here. |

> ⚠️ `its` (posesivo de *it*) vs `it's` (contracción de *it is*) — son diferentes.
> - `Its name is Rex.` → el nombre de él/ella/eso es Rex.
> - `It's a good idea.` → Es una buena idea.

---

### Preguntas con WHO y WHOSE

- **Who** → pregunta por la identidad de una persona
  - *Who is she?* → She's my colleague.
  - *Who are they?* → They're my parents.

- **Whose** → pregunta por posesión
  - *Whose phone is this?* → It's Ana's phone.
  - *Whose address is that?* → It's his address.

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **give** someone your number | dar tu número a alguien | Can I give you my number? |
| **leave** a message | dejar un mensaje | Please leave a message. |
| **look up** someone's info | buscar información de alguien | I'll look up her email. |
| **get in touch** (with) | ponerse en contacto | Let's get in touch. |
| **keep in touch** | mantenerse en contacto | Let's keep in touch! |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Formulario de registro en un evento

> **Situación:** David llena una hoja de registro para una conferencia tecnológica.

```
Name: David Ospina
Occupation: Software Engineer
Company: TechCorp Colombia
Phone number: +57 301 555 0012
Email address: d.ospina@techcorp.co
Emergency contact: María Ospina (his sister)
Emergency contact phone: +57 310 555 0034
```

**Nota gramatical:** `his sister` → adjetivo posesivo de *he*. En un formulario impreso se usa la tercera persona para referirse al registro.

---

### Caso 2 — Conversación por teléfono (dar información de contacto)

> **Situación:** Ana llama a una clínica para registrar a su esposo.

```
Receptionist: City Clinic, good morning. How can I help you?
Ana:          Hi. I'd like to register my husband.
Receptionist: Of course. What's his name?
Ana:          His name is Rodrigo Vargas.
Receptionist: And what's his date of birth?
Ana:          His birthday is March 15, 1990.
Receptionist: What's his phone number?
Ana:          His cell phone is 300-555-0045.
Receptionist: And his email address?
Ana:          It's r.vargas@mail.com. That's R-V-A-R-G-A-S at mail dot com.
Receptionist: Perfect. Is he your husband?
Ana:          Yes, he is.
```

**Nota gramatical:** Observa la alternancia de `his` para todo lo que pertenece al esposo. Al final, `Yes, he is.` — respuesta corta sin contracción.

---

### Caso 3 — Presentar a un compañero nuevo

> **Situación:** La profesora presenta a un estudiante nuevo al grupo.

```
Teacher:     Class, this is our new student. His name is Peter Kim.
             He is from South Korea. His English is very good.
             His email is p.kim@school.edu. Please add him to your group chat.
Peter:       Hi everyone! Nice to meet you all.
Student:     Hi Peter! I'm Laura. She's my friend Sofia.
Peter:       Nice to meet you, Laura. Nice to meet you too, Sofia.
Sofia:       Welcome! Our class starts at 8. Don't be late!
```

**Nota gramatical:** `Our class` → adjetivo posesivo de *we*. `His email is...` → se introduce información de contacto con adjetivo posesivo + sustantivo.

## 6. ⏱️ Adjetivos posesivos en todos los tiempos

Los posesivos no cambian con el tiempo verbal — lo que cambia es el verbo de la oración.

| Tiempo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Present Simple | Her office is on the second floor. |
| Past Simple | Her office was on the second floor before the renovation. |
| Future (will) | Her new office will be on the third floor. |
| Present Perfect | Her contact info has changed three times this year. |
| Present Continuous | Their team is working on a new project right now. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Confundir `his` y `her`

❌ She is my friend. His name is Ana.
✅ She is my friend. **Her** name is Ana.
> `His` → masculino. `Her` → femenino. Concuerda con la persona, no con el objeto.

### Usar pronombre de objeto donde va sujeto

❌ Me am a student.
✅ **I** am a student.

### Confundir `its` e `it's`

❌ Its a great idea. / The company lost it's data.
✅ **It's** a great idea. / The company lost **its** data.

### Olvidar el sujeto en preguntas indirectas

❌ What's the name?
✅ What's **your** name? / What's **his/her** name?
