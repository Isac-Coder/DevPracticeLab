## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Futuro con BE GOING TO y WILL

### Las dos formas principales de futuro en inglés para este nivel

| Forma | Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **be going to** | planes ya decididos, intenciones, predicciones con evidencia | I'm going to study tonight. |
| **will** | decisiones espontáneas, predicciones generales, promesas, ofrecimientos | I'll help you with that. |

---

### BE GOING TO — estructura

```
Subject + am/is/are + going to + verb (base)
```

| Tipo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Afirmativa | I **am going to** attend the workshop. |
| Negativa | She **isn't going to** work tomorrow. |
| Pregunta | **Are** you **going to** join the meeting? |
| Respuesta | Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. |

---

### WILL — estructura

```
Subject + will + verb (base)
```

| Tipo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Afirmativa | I **will** send you the file. |
| Negativa | She **won't** be here on Friday. |
| Pregunta | **Will** you help me? |
| Respuesta | Yes, I will. / No, I won't. |

**Contracciones de will:**
- I will → **I'll**
- You will → **You'll**
- He will → **He'll**
- She will → **She'll**
- We will → **We'll**
- They will → **They'll**
- will not → **won't**

---

### Present Continuous con valor de futuro

El Present Continuous también se usa para planes muy cercanos y concretos (con hora, fecha o lugar):

```
I'm meeting Carlos at 3 PM tomorrow.
She's flying to Bogotá next Monday.
They're having dinner with clients tonight.
```

> ⚠️ Solo funciona con verbos de acción y cuando hay una referencia de tiempo futura clara. No funciona con verbos de estado.

---

### ¿`Going to` o `Will`?

| Situación | Usa | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Plan ya decidido | going to | I'm going to call her after class. *(ya decidí antes)* |
| Decisión en el momento | will | "The phone is ringing." "I'll get it!" *(decido ahora mismo)* |
| Predicción con evidencia visible | going to | Look at those clouds — it's going to rain. |
| Predicción general sin evidencia | will | I think it will rain tomorrow. |
| Promesa | will | I'll be there on time, I promise. |
| Ofrecimiento | will | I'll carry that for you. |

## 4. 🔗 Collocations clave

| Collocation | Traducción | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **make** plans | hacer planes | Let's make plans for the weekend. |
| **have** plans | tener planes | Do you have plans for tonight? |
| **change** plans | cambiar los planes | Sorry, I have to change my plans. |
| **cancel** plans | cancelar planes | She had to cancel her plans. |
| **look forward to** | tener ganas de | I'm looking forward to the vacation. |
| **be about to** | estar a punto de | The meeting is about to start. |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Coordinar planes con un compañero (chat)

> **Situación:** Dos estudiantes de RIWI coordinan por WhatsApp para el fin de semana.

```
Carlos:  Hey! Do you have plans this weekend?
Valentina: Yeah, I'm going to visit my family on Saturday. 
            But Sunday I'm free. What about you?
Carlos:  I'm going to work on the React project on Saturday morning.
         On Sunday, I'll probably relax. Or maybe we can study together?
Valentina: That sounds good! Let's meet at the library at 2 PM on Sunday.
Carlos:  Perfect. I'll bring my laptop and the TypeScript notes.
Valentina: Great. Oh, and I'm going to stop by a café first — 
            do you want anything?
Carlos:  I'll have a large coffee, please. Thanks!
Valentina: Ha! See you Sunday.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I'm going to visit` — plan ya decidido. `I'll probably relax` — decisión no fija. `I'll bring` — promesa/ofrecimiento. `I'll have a coffee` — decisión espontánea en el momento.

### Caso 3 — Entrevista: planes de carrera

> **Situación:** Ana responde preguntas sobre sus planes futuros en una entrevista de trabajo.

```
Interviewer: Where do you see yourself in two years?
Ana:         I'm going to finish my degree in software engineering next year.
             After that, I'm planning to specialize in frontend development.
             I'd like to work for a company where I can grow and learn.
Interviewer: Are you going to stay in Medellín?
Ana:         I'm not sure yet. I'll probably stay here for the first year,
             but I'm open to working remotely for international companies.
Interviewer: Will you consider relocating if needed?
Ana:         Yes, I will. I'm flexible. I think in five years 
             I'll be a senior developer. That's my goal.
Interviewer: Great. We'll be in touch soon.
Ana:         Thank you! I look forward to hearing from you.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I'm going to finish` — plan concreto con fecha. `I'll probably stay` — predicción personal sin certeza. `I'll consider` — respuesta espontánea a la pregunta. `I look forward to` — expresión fija para expresar anticipación positiva.

## 6. ⏱️ Planes en distintos tiempos verbales

| Tiempo | Ejemplo | Cuándo |
|---|---|---|
| **Present Continuous** | I'm meeting the client at 4. | Plan muy próximo y confirmado |
| **Going to** | I'm going to apply for the job. | Intención / decisión previa |
| **Will** | I'll call you when I finish. | Decisión espontánea / promesa |
| **Present Simple** | The flight leaves at 6 AM. | Horarios fijos (timetables) |
| **Past Simple** | I was going to go, but I stayed home. | Plan que no se cumplió |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Usar `going to go` cuando puedes simplificarlo

`I'm going to go to the store` → ✅ correcto, pero también puedes decir:
`I'm going to the store` (Present Continuous para planes inmediatos)

### Usar `will` para planes ya decididos

❌ I will visit my family this weekend. *(ya decidiste)*
✅ I'm **going to** visit my family this weekend.
> `Will` en este contexto suena como si decidieras en el momento.

### `Won't` ≠ `Don't want to`

- `I won't go` → decisión firme de no ir / negativa futura
- `I don't want to go` → no tengo ganas de ir
