## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Countable vs. Uncountable nouns + Quantifiers

### ¿Por qué importa esto?

En inglés, los sustantivos se dividen en **contables** (se pueden contar: *3 apples*) e **incontables** (no se pueden contar directamente: *water, rice, sugar*). Los cuantificadores cambian según esta clasificación.

---

### Contables vs. Incontables

| Contables | Incontables |
|---|---|
| an apple / two apples | rice (no "a rice") |
| a sandwich / three sandwiches | water, juice, milk |
| an egg / six eggs | bread, butter, flour |
| a banana / four bananas | sugar, salt, coffee |

---

### Cuantificadores

| Cuantificador | Con contables | Con incontables |
|---|---|---|
| **some** | some oranges | some water |
| **any** (neg/preg) | Are there any eggs? | Is there any milk? |
| **many** | How many apples? | ❌ no se usa |
| **much** | ❌ no se usa | How much sugar? |
| **a lot of / lots of** | a lot of sandwiches | a lot of rice |
| **a few** | a few bananas | ❌ no se usa |
| **a little** | ❌ no se usa | a little sugar |
| **no** | no eggs | no milk |

**Truco:**
- Contables → `many`, `a few`, `(how) many`
- Incontables → `much`, `a little`, `(how) much`
- Ambos → `some`, `any`, `a lot of`, `no`

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Ordenar en un restaurante

> **Situación:** Carlos y Ana van a almorzar en un restaurante del centro.

```
Waiter:   Good afternoon! Are you ready to order?
Carlos:   Almost. What do you recommend?
Waiter:   Today's special is grilled chicken with rice and salad.
          It comes with a glass of juice or water.
Carlos:   That sounds good. I'll have the special, please.
          And can I have sparkling water instead of juice?
Waiter:   Of course. And for you?
Ana:      I'd like the pasta with tomato sauce. 
          Does it have any meat?
Waiter:   Yes, it has some ground beef. We can make it without meat 
          if you prefer.
Ana:      Yes, please — without meat. I'm vegetarian.
Waiter:   No problem. Anything else?
Carlos:   How much is the soup of the day?
Waiter:   It's 8,000 pesos. It's a chicken and vegetable soup.
Carlos:   I'll have a bowl, please. Thanks.
Waiter:   Great! Your food will be ready in about 15 minutes.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I'll have` — decisión espontánea en el momento. `Does it have any meat?` — `any` en pregunta con incontable implícito. `some ground beef` — `some` en afirmativa.

---

### Caso 2 — Lista del mercado (mensaje de texto)

> **Situación:** Laura le manda la lista del mercado a su roommate.

```
Hey! Can you stop by the supermarket?
We don't have much food at home. Here's what we need:

Fruits & veggies:
- Some bananas (a bunch)
- 3 avocados
- A bag of carrots
- Some tomatoes (a kilo)

Proteins:
- A dozen eggs
- 2 chicken breasts

Pantry:
- A bag of rice (no white rice — brown rice please)
- Some olive oil
- A little sugar (just a small bag)

Drinks:
- A lot of water (3 big bottles)
- Some orange juice
- No soda please!

Thanks!! 🛒
```

**Nota gramatical:** `A bunch of`, `a kilo of`, `a bag of` — contenedores que se usan con incontables para hacerlos "contables". `A little sugar` — pequeña cantidad de incontable. `A lot of water` — gran cantidad de incontable.

---

### Caso 3 — Blog de recetas (texto descriptivo)

> **Situación:** Valentina escribe una entrada en su blog de cocina.

```
My Favorite Quick Breakfast: Avocado Toast

I eat avocado toast almost every morning. It's healthy, fast, and delicious.
Here's what you need:

Ingredients (for 1 person):
- 2 slices of whole wheat bread
- 1 ripe avocado
- A little salt and pepper
- Some cherry tomatoes (optional)
- A few drops of lemon juice
- 1 egg (optional, for extra protein)

You don't need much time — it takes about 5 minutes.
I don't add much salt because avocado has a natural flavor.
If you like spicy food, add a little chili flakes!

This breakfast gives me a lot of energy in the morning.
I don't have much time to cook, so this is perfect.
How many eggs do you eat for breakfast? Let me know in the comments!
```

**Nota gramatical:** `a little salt` (incontable → a little), `a few drops` (contable → a few), `much time` (incontable → much en negativa), `many eggs` (contable → many en pregunta).

### Caso 4 — Preferencias alimenticias (formulario médico)

> **Situación:** Un centro de salud recopila información sobre la dieta de sus pacientes.

```
Patient Dietary Information Form

Patient name: Diego Vargas
Date: October 5, 2025

1. Do you have any food allergies? 
   ✅ Yes. I'm allergic to shellfish and nuts.

2. Are you following any special diet?
   ✅ Yes. I'm vegetarian. I don't eat any meat or fish.
   I eat some dairy products (cheese, milk) but not many eggs.

3. How much water do you drink per day?
   About 2 liters. I drink a lot of coffee too — maybe too much!

4. How many servings of fruit or vegetables do you eat daily?
   Usually 3 or 4. I eat a lot of fruit in the morning.

5. Do you eat fast food?
   Rarely. Maybe once a week.

6. Do you drink alcohol?
   No, I don't drink any alcohol.

Notes: Patient is in good health. Recommend reducing coffee intake.
```

## 6. ⏱️ Cuantificadores en distintos tiempos verbales

Los cuantificadores son adjetivos — el tiempo verbal está en el verbo, no en ellos.

| Tiempo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| **Present Simple** | I don't eat much sugar. |
| **Past Simple** | We had a lot of food at the party. |
| **Future (will)** | I'll need some vegetables for tonight. |
| **Future (going to)** | I'm going to buy a few fruits tomorrow. |
| **Present Perfect** | She's eaten many different cuisines this year. |
| **Present Continuous** | I'm drinking a lot of water today — it's so hot! |

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `Much` en oraciones afirmativas (suena formal/extraño)

❌ I eat much rice. (suena raro en conversación)
✅ I eat **a lot of** rice.
> `Much` va principalmente en negativas y preguntas.

### `Many` con incontables

❌ How many water do you drink?
✅ How **much** water do you drink?

### Olvidar el contenedor con incontables

❌ I'd like two coffees. *(técnicamente incorrecto)*
✅ I'd like **two cups of** coffee. *(más preciso)*
> En la práctica, "two coffees" es muy común y entendido en contexto de restaurante.

### `Some` en preguntas negativas vs. neutras

- `Do you want **any** coffee?` → pregunta neutra
- `Do you want **some** coffee?` → ofrecimiento implícito (se espera que diga sí)
