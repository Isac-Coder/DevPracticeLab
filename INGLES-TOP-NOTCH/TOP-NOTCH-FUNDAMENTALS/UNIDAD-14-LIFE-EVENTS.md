## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Present Perfect

### ¿Por qué se usa?

El Present Perfect conecta el pasado con el presente. Lo usas cuando:
1. Hablas de una **experiencia de vida** sin especificar cuándo: *I have visited Paris.*
2. Una acción **comenzó en el pasado y continúa**: *She has worked here for 5 years.*
3. Una acción pasada tiene **relevancia en el presente**: *I've lost my keys!* (no los encuentro ahora)

---

### Estructura

```
Subject + have/has + past participle
```

| Sujeto | Auxiliar | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | **have** | I **have** graduated. |
| He / She / It | **has** | She **has** gotten married. |

**Negativa:**

| Sujeto | Negativa | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | **haven't** | I **haven't** finished yet. |
| He / She / It | **hasn't** | He **hasn't** started. |

**Pregunta:**

```
Have/Has + subject + past participle?
→ Have you ever visited Bogotá?
→ Has she finished her degree?
```

---

### Palabras clave del Present Perfect

| Palabra | Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **ever** | experiencia (preguntas) | Have you ever lived abroad? |
| **never** | sin experiencia | I've never eaten sushi. |
| **already** | antes de lo esperado | She has already graduated. |
| **yet** | hasta ahora (neg/preg) | I haven't finished yet. / Have you eaten yet? |
| **just** | hace muy poco | He has just arrived. |
| **for** | duración | I've lived here for 5 years. |
| **since** | desde un punto | I've worked here since 2022. |

---

### Present Perfect vs. Past Simple — la diferencia clave

| Present Perfect | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| Sin tiempo específico | Con tiempo específico |
| Experiencia general | Momento concreto |
| Conexión con el presente | Completamente en el pasado |

| Present Perfect | Past Simple |
|---|---|
| I **have visited** Bogotá. | I **visited** Bogotá **last year**. |
| She **has gotten** married. | She **got** married **in 2020**. |
| **Have** you **ever** eaten arepas? | **Did** you eat arepas **yesterday**? |

> 💡 Agregar una expresión de tiempo específica (`yesterday`, `last year`, `in 2020`) → automáticamente cambias a Past Simple.

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Hoja de vida / CV (sección biográfica)

> **Situación:** Valentina escribe su perfil profesional para su primer CV.

```
PROFESSIONAL PROFILE

My name is Valentina Ospina. I am 22 years old and I was born in Medellín, 
Colombia. I have grown up in a family that values education and hard work.

I started my programming journey in 2023 when I enrolled at RIWI. 
Since then, I have learned React, TypeScript, and JavaScript. 
I have built several projects, including a cinema app and a local events platform.

I have completed over 800 hours of training in frontend development.
I have also given a team presentation on asynchronous culture and 
professional communication.

I haven't worked professionally yet, but I have done several personal 
and academic projects. I am now ready to take my first professional step.

GOALS:
- I would like to work as a junior frontend developer.
- I plan to specialize in React and TypeScript.
- I hope to work on international projects someday.
- I dream of building apps that help people in my community.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `I have learned`, `I have built` — Present Perfect para experiencias sin tiempo específico. `I haven't worked yet` — negativa con `yet`. Metas con `would like to`, `plan to`, `hope to`, `dream of`.

---

### Caso 2 — Entrevista: "Tell me about yourself"

> **Situación:** Diego responde la pregunta clásica de entrevistas de trabajo.

```
Interviewer: Tell me about yourself.
Diego:       Of course. I'm Diego Vargas, 23 years old, from Barranquilla.
             I've always been passionate about technology.
             I started learning to code in 2022 — I watched online tutorials 
             at first, then I joined RIWI in 2024.
             
             Since I started at RIWI, I've built multiple projects in React 
             and TypeScript. I've worked in teams, I've given presentations,
             and I've learned how to handle feedback professionally.
             
             I graduated from the program last month. This is my first 
             formal job application. I haven't worked in a tech company yet,
             but I've gained a lot of practical experience.
             
             My goal is to grow as a developer. I'd like to specialize in 
             frontend architecture, and someday I hope to lead a development team.
             
Interviewer: Have you ever worked remotely?
Diego:       Yes, I have. During the program, we worked in remote teams.
             I've used Slack, Jira, and Zoom regularly.
             
Interviewer: Have you ever used agile methodologies?
Diego:       Yes, we've worked with Scrum during most of our projects.
             I've been part of daily standups, sprint planning, and retrospectives.
```

**Nota gramatical:** Mezcla estratégica de Past Simple (`I started in 2022`) para hechos con fecha, y Present Perfect (`I've built`, `I've worked`) para experiencias sin tiempo específico. `Have you ever...?` — pregunta de experiencia clásica en entrevistas.

### Caso 3 — Carta de motivación (fragmento)

> **Situación:** Ana escribe una carta de motivación para un programa de intercambio.

```
Subject: Motivation Letter — International Exchange Program 2026

Dear Selection Committee,

My name is Ana Rodríguez. I am a frontend development student at RIWI 
in Cartagena, Colombia.

I have always dreamed of studying abroad. Since I started coding, 
I have seen how international collaboration changes the quality of 
the work we produce. I have already participated in two cross-team projects
with students from other cities, and those experiences have shaped
my perspective significantly.

I have studied English for five years. I have taken the TOEFL exam twice
and I scored 85 on my last attempt. I haven't lived abroad yet, 
but I have traveled to three countries and I adapt easily to new environments.

During my time at RIWI, I have grown not only technically but personally.
I have learned to work under pressure, to give and receive constructive 
feedback, and to communicate my ideas clearly in both Spanish and English.

I believe this program will help me achieve my goal of becoming 
an internationally-minded developer who can make a real difference.

Thank you for considering my application.

Sincerely,
Ana Rodríguez
```

### Caso 4 — Conversación: comparar experiencias de vida

> **Situación:** Dos compañeros se conocen mejor durante una pausa en RIWI.

```
Camila:  Have you ever lived in another city?
Andrés:  Yes, I have. I lived in Bogotá for two years. I moved back last year.
Camila:  Really? I've never left Cartagena. What was it like?
Andrés:  It was cold but exciting. I met amazing people there.
          Have you ever considered moving?
Camila:  I've thought about it. I'd like to move to Medellín someday.
          Have you ever traveled outside Colombia?
Andrés:  No, I haven't. But I've always wanted to go to Canada.
         Have you?
Camila:  Yes! I went to Mexico last year for a family trip. 
         It was incredible. Have you ever eaten real Mexican food?
Andrés:  Ha! Not in Mexico, no. But I've eaten at a Mexican restaurant here.
Camila:  It's not the same! You should go someday.
Andrés:  I will. It's on my bucket list.
```

**Nota gramatical:** Alternancia natural y correcta entre `Have you ever...?` (experiencia) y Past Simple cuando se da un tiempo específico (`I lived in Bogotá for two years`, `I went to Mexico last year`).

## 6. ⏱️ Present Perfect en distintos contextos

| Uso | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| Experiencia (ever/never) | I've never seen snow. |
| Resultado presente | I've lost my keys. (no los encuentro) |
| Continuidad (for/since) | She has lived here for 10 years. |
| Reciente (just) | He has just arrived. |
| Antes de lo esperado (already) | They've already finished the project. |
| Hasta ahora (yet) | I haven't submitted the form yet. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### Usar Present Perfect con tiempo específico

❌ I have visited Cartagena **last year**.
✅ I visited Cartagena last year. *(Past Simple)*
✅ I have visited Cartagena. *(sin tiempo → Present Perfect)*

### Usar Past Simple para experiencias generales

❌ Did you ever eat sushi?
✅ **Have** you ever eaten sushi?

### Participio irregular mal usado

❌ I have went to school.
✅ I have **gone** to school.

### `For` vs `Since`

- `for` → duración: *for 3 years, for a month*
- `since` → punto de inicio: *since 2020, since Monday*

❌ I've worked here since 3 years.
✅ I've worked here **for** 3 years.
