## 2. 🧠 Gramática principal: Preposiciones de lugar + Imperativo

### ¿Por qué se usan?

Las preposiciones de lugar ubican objetos y personas en el espacio. El imperativo da instrucciones directas sin sujeto — es la forma base del verbo. Juntos son la herramienta principal para navegar y orientarse.

---

### Preposiciones de lugar

| Preposición | Significado | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **on** | sobre una superficie / en una calle | The bank is on Main Street. |
| **in** | dentro de / en un lugar cerrado | The ATM is in the mall. |
| **at** | en un punto específico | Meet me at the corner. |
| **next to** | al lado de | The pharmacy is next to the school. |
| **across from** | al frente de | The park is across from the hotel. |
| **between** | entre (dos cosas) | The café is between the bank and the library. |
| **behind** | detrás de | The parking lot is behind the supermarket. |
| **in front of** | delante de | The bus stop is in front of the hospital. |
| **around the corner** | a la vuelta | The ATM is around the corner. |
| **on the left/right** | a la izquierda/derecha | Turn left. The hotel is on the right. |

---

### El imperativo para dar direcciones

El imperativo usa la **forma base del verbo**, sin sujeto. Es directo pero no es grosero en contexto de instrucciones.

| Afirmativo | Negativo |
|---|---|
| Go straight. | Don't turn left. |
| Turn right. | Don't stop here. |
| Take the first left. | Don't cross the street here. |
| Walk two blocks. | Don't go that way. |

**Verbos clave para direcciones:**

| Verbo | Significado |
|---|---|
| go straight | seguir derecho |
| turn left / right | girar a la izquierda/derecha |
| take the first/second left | tomar la primera/segunda a la izquierda |
| walk (two blocks) | caminar (dos cuadras) |
| cross the street | cruzar la calle |
| pass the light | pasar el semáforo |
| go past the church | pasar la iglesia |
| you'll see it on your left/right | lo verás a tu izquierda/derecha |

## 5. 🗣️ Casos de uso reales

### Caso 1 — Turista pregunta por el hotel

> **Situación:** María llega a una ciudad nueva y le pregunta a un local cómo llegar al hotel desde la estación.

```
María:   Excuse me. Is there a hotel near here?
Local:   Yes! The Grand Hotel is about three blocks from here.
María:   How do I get there?
Local:   Go straight on this street. Walk two blocks.
         Then turn right at the traffic light.
         The hotel is on the left, across from the park.
María:   Is it far?
Local:   No, it's about five minutes on foot.
María:   Thank you so much!
Local:   You're welcome. Have a good stay!
```

**Nota gramatical:** `Go straight`, `Walk two blocks`, `Turn right` — imperativos sin sujeto. `It's about five minutes on foot` — `it` como sujeto impersonal para distancia/tiempo.

---

### Caso 2 — App de mensajería (instrucciones escritas)

> **Situación:** Luis le manda las indicaciones para llegar a su oficina a un cliente nuevo por WhatsApp.

```
Luis:  Hey! Here are the directions to our office:

       1. Take the bus to Avenida El Poblado.
       2. Get off at the mall stop.
       3. Walk straight for one block.
       4. Turn left at the corner. Don't turn right — that's a dead end.
       5. Our building is between the pharmacy and the café. 
          It's the blue one. You can't miss it!
       6. We're on the 4th floor. Take the elevator.

       See you at 3 pm!
```

**Nota gramatical:** Instrucciones numeradas con imperativo. `Don't turn right` — negativo del imperativo. `You can't miss it` — expresión idiomática muy común: "es imposible perdérselo".

---

### Caso 3 — Describir el vecindario (texto escrito)

> **Situación:** Sandra describe su barrio en una tarea de inglés.

```
My neighborhood is very convenient. There is a supermarket on my street,
next to a pharmacy. The school is across from my house, and the park is
behind it. The bus stop is in front of the school, so it's easy to
get to work. There is no subway station near here, but there is a
taxi stand at the corner. The nearest hospital is three blocks away,
on Calle 50. I love my neighborhood because everything is close.
```

**Nota gramatical:** `There is` (singular) / `There are` (plural) para indicar existencia. Preposiciones encadenadas para construir una descripción espacial completa.

---

### Caso 4 — Reserva de hotel por email

> **Situación:** Un viajero confirma la ubicación del hotel antes de llegar.

```
Subject: Directions from the Airport

Dear Hotel Staff,

I have a reservation for next Friday (check-in: June 14).
My flight arrives at 10 PM. Is there a bus from the airport to the hotel?
Or is the hotel near a subway station?

Also, is the hotel next to a restaurant? I'll be hungry when I arrive!

Thank you,
James Walker
---
Reply from hotel:

Dear Mr. Walker,

Welcome! Here are the directions from the airport:

Take the blue metro line. Get off at Central Station.
The hotel is across from the station, on the right side.
It's a 2-minute walk. You can't miss the green sign.

There are two restaurants next to the hotel, both open until midnight.
See you on Friday!

Best regards,
Front Desk Team
```

## 6. ⏱️ Preposiciones y expresiones en distintos contextos temporales

Las preposiciones de lugar no cambian, pero el tiempo verbal sí indica cuándo ocurre la acción.

| Tiempo | Ejemplo |
|---|---|
| **Present Simple** | The hospital is next to the school. |
| **Past Simple** | The café was across from the park, but it closed. |
| **Future (will)** | The new station will be on Avenida Norte. |
| **Future (going to)** | I'm going to meet you in front of the library. |
| **Present Continuous** | They're building a mall behind the stadium right now. |

---

## 7. ⚠️ Errores frecuentes

### `in` vs `on` vs `at` para lugares

❌ I am in the corner.
✅ I am **at** the corner.
> `at` = punto específico. `on` = superficie/calle. `in` = interior.

### Traducción literal de "a la vuelta"

❌ It's to the turn.
✅ It's **around the corner**.

### Olvidar el artículo con nombres de lugares genéricos

❌ Go to hospital.
✅ Go to **the** hospital.
> Con lugares únicos o ya mencionados se usa `the`.

### Usar `go to` con lugares que no lo necesitan

❌ Go to home.
✅ Go **home**. (sin `to`)
> `home` no lleva preposición cuando es destino.
